Q: A milkman adds a small amount of baking soda to fresh milk. How does it shift the pH, and why does this milk take longer to set as curd? {demo}

A: Baking soda makes milk slightly alkaline (pH above 6), slowing lactic-acid formation; curd-setting needs acidic conditions, so it takes longer. (Outline — expand with pH scale reasoning.)

Q: Give one example each of a salt used in (a) baking, (b) washing, (c) plaster work, with its chemical name. {demo}

A: (a) Baking soda — sodium hydrogencarbonate. (b) Washing soda — sodium carbonate decahydrate. (c) Plaster of Paris — calcium sulphate hemihydrate. (Outline — add preparation/properties for full marks.)
