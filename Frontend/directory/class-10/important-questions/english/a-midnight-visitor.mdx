Chapter revision of The Midnight Visitor by Robert Arthur. Practise short, long, competency-based, extract-based and additional questions. Tap any question to reveal its answer.

### Section A: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

Q: How was Ausable different from other secret agents?

A: Ausable was very different from the secret agents Fowler had read about. He was extremely fat, spoke with an American accent and lived in an ordinary hotel room. Unlike the glamorous spies in novels, he did not appear mysterious or adventurous. However, he possessed exceptional intelligence and presence of mind.

Q: Who was Fowler, and why was he disappointed with Ausable?

A: Fowler was a young writer who wanted to experience the excitement and adventure associated with secret agents. He was disappointed because Ausable did not match his imagination of a typical spy. Ausable was fat, lived in a dull hotel room and lacked the mysterious appearance Fowler had expected.

Q: What was the important report that Ausable was expecting?

A: Ausable was expecting an important report concerning some new missiles. The report was significant because it contained information that could affect the course of history. Several people had risked their lives to obtain it, making it a valuable target for enemy agents.

Q: Who was Max, and why had he entered Ausable's room?

A: Max was a rival secret agent who had entered Ausable's room to steal the important report concerning new missiles. He threatened Ausable with a pistol and intended to take the report before it could reach him.

Q: How had Max entered Ausable's room?

A: Max claimed that he had entered Ausable's room using a passkey. He was unaware of the supposed balcony outside Ausable's window and believed that it could have provided him with an alternative way into the room.

Q: How did Ausable convince Max that there was a balcony outside the window?

A: Ausable complained about a balcony that supposedly extended beneath his window and explained that it belonged to the neighbouring apartment. He added that someone had previously entered his room through it. His confident and detailed explanation convinced Max that the balcony existed, although it was entirely imaginary.

Q: How did Ausable react when he found Max in his room?

A: Ausable remained calm and showed no visible panic when he discovered Max holding a pistol. He spoke to Max as though the situation were merely an inconvenience. His composure allowed him to think quickly and devise a plan to outwit the armed intruder.

Q: Why did Ausable tell Max that the police were at the door?

A: Ausable wanted to frighten Max into leaving the room. He claimed that he had arranged for the police to check on him because the report was important. When someone knocked, Max believed that the police had arrived and became desperate to escape.

Q: Who was actually knocking at the door?

A: The person knocking at the door was Henry, a waiter who had come to deliver the drinks Ausable had ordered. There were no police officers outside the room. Ausable had invented the story about the police to deceive Max.

Q: Why did Max decide to jump through the window?

A: Max believed that the police were outside the door and feared being caught. Ausable had convinced him that a balcony extended beneath the window. Therefore, Max decided to escape through the window and wait on the supposed balcony.

Q: How did Ausable get rid of Max without using a weapon?

A: Ausable used his intelligence rather than physical force. He invented a story about a balcony outside the window and then claimed that the police were knocking at the door. Frightened by the supposed arrival of the police, Max climbed out of the window and fell because there was no balcony.

Q: Why was Fowler astonished at the end of the story?

A: Fowler was astonished because he discovered that Ausable had defeated an armed rival agent without using a weapon or engaging in physical combat. He had expected a thrilling encounter involving conventional spy techniques, but Ausable had succeeded through clever deception and quick thinking.

Q: What is the significance of the imaginary balcony in the story?

A: The imaginary balcony is central to Ausable's plan. By convincing Max that it exists, Ausable creates a false escape route. When Max tries to use it to avoid the supposed police, he falls. The incident demonstrates Ausable's ability to exploit his opponent's assumptions.

Q: What does the story reveal about Ausable's intelligence?

A: The story reveals that Ausable is observant, resourceful and capable of thinking quickly under pressure. Despite being threatened by an armed man, he remains calm and invents convincing stories about a balcony and the police. His intelligence enables him to overcome a dangerous situation without physical force.

### Section B: Long Answer Questions (5-6 Marks)

Q: Write a character sketch of Ausable.

A: Ausable is an intelligent, resourceful and calm secret agent who differs considerably from the conventional image of a spy. He is extremely fat, speaks with an American accent and lives in an ordinary hotel room. His appearance initially disappoints Fowler, who expects secret agents to be glamorous, mysterious and adventurous. However, Ausable's actions reveal his true abilities. When he discovers Max in his room with a pistol, he does not panic. Instead, he uses his presence of mind to invent a convincing story about a balcony outside his window. He then claims that the police are at the door, making Max believe that his escape route is through the supposed balcony. As a result, Max climbs out of the window and falls. Ausable demonstrates that intelligence, composure and quick thinking are more valuable than physical appearance or weapons in a dangerous situation. His character challenges conventional assumptions about secret agents and highlights the importance of mental alertness.

Q: Write a character sketch of Fowler.

A: Fowler is a young writer who is fascinated by the mysterious and adventurous world of secret agents. He expects to encounter dramatic situations involving secret messages, weapons, danger and glamorous spies. However, he is initially disappointed by Ausable's ordinary appearance and unremarkable hotel room. When Fowler discovers Max standing in the room with a pistol, he experiences the excitement he had been seeking. However, he also becomes nervous and is unable to understand how Ausable remains calm in such a dangerous situation. At the end of the story, Fowler is astonished by Ausable's cleverness. He realises that a successful secret agent does not necessarily need a glamorous appearance or physical strength. Fowler's character helps the reader understand the difference between the romanticised image of espionage and the practical intelligence required in real situations.

Q: How did Ausable outwit Max? Explain the sequence of events.

A: Ausable outwitted Max through intelligence, deception and presence of mind. When Ausable entered his hotel room with Fowler, he discovered Max standing inside with a pistol. Max demanded an important report concerning new missiles. Instead of panicking, Ausable complained about a balcony outside his window. He explained that someone had previously entered his room through it, convincing Max that the balcony existed. Ausable then claimed that the police were knocking at the door because he had arranged additional protection for the important report. Frightened by the supposed arrival of the police, Max decided to escape through the window. Believing that he would land on the balcony, he climbed out and fell because there was no balcony. The person knocking at the door turned out to be Henry, a waiter who had brought Ausable's drinks. Thus, Ausable defeated Max without using a weapon. His quick thinking and ability to manipulate Max's assumptions enabled him to escape danger.

Q: Presence of mind is more powerful than physical strength. Justify this statement with reference to the story.

A: The story The Midnight Visitor demonstrates that presence of mind can be more effective than physical strength in a dangerous situation. Max is armed with a pistol and appears to have complete control over Ausable and Fowler. However, Ausable does not panic or attempt to overpower him physically. Instead, Ausable invents a convincing story about a balcony outside his window. He then claims that the police are knocking at the door. Max believes both statements and becomes frightened about being caught. Consequently, he attempts to escape through the window, expecting to land on the balcony, but falls because no balcony exists. Ausable succeeds through intelligence and calm decision-making rather than physical force. The story therefore teaches that remaining composed and thinking clearly under pressure can help a person overcome even a seemingly powerful opponent.

Q: Justify the title The Midnight Visitor.

A: The title The Midnight Visitor is appropriate because the central incident revolves around Max's unexpected presence in Ausable's hotel room at night. Max enters the room to steal an important report concerning new missiles and threatens Ausable with a pistol. His arrival creates suspense and places Ausable in a dangerous situation. However, Ausable remains calm and cleverly convinces Max that a balcony exists outside the window and that the police are knocking at the door. Max attempts to escape through the imaginary balcony and falls. The unexpected visitor, Max, therefore becomes the centre of the story's suspense and action. The title captures the mysterious atmosphere of the narrative and the incident that allows Ausable to demonstrate his intelligence.

Q: How does the story challenge the conventional image of a secret agent?

A: The story challenges the conventional image of a secret agent through the character of Ausable. Fowler expects a spy to be physically fit, glamorous, mysterious and involved in exciting adventures. Instead, he finds Ausable extremely fat, ordinary-looking and living in an unremarkable hotel room. However, Ausable proves to be a highly capable secret agent. When Max threatens him with a pistol, Ausable does not rely on physical strength or weapons. He invents a convincing story about a balcony and the police, manipulating Max into making a fatal mistake. The contrast between Ausable's ordinary appearance and extraordinary intelligence demonstrates that successful espionage depends more on mental alertness, resourcefulness and quick thinking than on physical appearance.

Q: How does Robert Arthur create suspense and surprise in The Midnight Visitor?

A: Robert Arthur creates suspense by introducing Max, an armed rival agent, into Ausable's hotel room just as Fowler expects an exciting adventure. Max's demand for the important report creates an immediate threat. The reader wonders how Ausable will escape the dangerous situation. The suspense increases when Ausable claims that the police are knocking at the door and describes a balcony outside his window. Max believes these statements and attempts to escape through the window. The final revelation creates surprise because the person at the door is merely a waiter, and the supposed balcony does not exist. Max has been defeated entirely through Ausable's clever deception. These unexpected developments make the story suspenseful and highlight the power of intelligence.

### Section C: Competency-Based and Value-Based Questions

Q: If you were in Ausable's position, how would you react when confronted by an armed intruder?

A: If I were in Ausable's position, I would try to remain calm and avoid provoking the armed intruder. I would assess the situation carefully and look for a safe opportunity to seek help or escape. I would prioritise personal safety rather than attempting a physical confrontation. The story demonstrates the importance of remaining mentally alert and making sensible decisions during emergencies.

Q: What does Max's behaviour reveal about his character?

A: Max appears confident and threatening because he possesses a pistol and expects to obtain the report through force. However, he is also gullible and easily influenced by Ausable's convincing explanations. His belief in the imaginary balcony and the supposed police arrival leads him to make a fatal mistake. His character shows that overconfidence and poor judgement can undermine even a seemingly strong position.

Q: Why was Fowler's experience different from what he had expected?

A: Fowler expected a glamorous and adventurous experience involving a mysterious secret agent, secret messages and dangerous encounters. Initially, Ausable's ordinary appearance disappointed him. However, Max's arrival with a pistol gave Fowler the excitement he had been seeking. The incident ultimately taught him that real intelligence work may depend more on quick thinking and deception than on dramatic physical action.

Q: What lesson does the story teach about handling difficult situations?

A: The story teaches that remaining calm, thinking clearly and understanding the situation are essential when facing difficulties. Ausable does not panic when threatened by Max. Instead, he uses his intelligence to manipulate the situation and escape danger. His actions demonstrate that quick thinking and sound judgement can be more effective than impulsive reactions.

Q: Why was Max's confidence ultimately responsible for his downfall?

A: Max's confidence made him believe that he could easily obtain the report and escape from Ausable's room. However, he failed to question Ausable's claims about the balcony and the police. His assumptions led him to climb out of the window without checking whether the balcony existed. His downfall demonstrates that overconfidence and a failure to assess information critically can lead to serious mistakes.

### Section E: Additional Important Questions

A: Ausable complained that someone had entered his room through the balcony the previous month and that the management had failed to block it off as promised. This complaint was part of his plan to make Max believe that a balcony existed outside the window.

Q: Why did Max want to obtain the report before Ausable received it?

A: Max wanted to steal the report concerning new missiles because it contained important information. He believed that taking it would prevent it from reaching Ausable and place the information in his own hands.

Q: What role does Henry play in the story?

A: Henry is the waiter who arrives with the drinks Ausable has ordered. His knock at the door provides Ausable with an opportunity to convince Max that the police have arrived. Although Henry is unaware of Ausable's plan, his arrival helps bring the deception to its conclusion.

Q: What is the significance of Fowler's changing reaction throughout the story?

A: Fowler initially feels disappointed because Ausable does not resemble the secret agents he has read about. When Max appears with a pistol, Fowler experiences genuine excitement but also fear. By the end, he is astonished by Ausable's intelligence. His changing reaction highlights the difference between romantic expectations and the realities of espionage.
