### Section A: Short Answer Questions

Q: Why did Gavin Maxwell decide to keep an otter instead of a dog?

A: After the death of his dog Jonnie, Gavin Maxwell was too sad to think of keeping another dog. However, he found life without a pet lonely. He eventually decided to keep an otter instead of a dog because he felt that his cottage at Camusfearna, surrounded by water, would be an ideal place for an otter.

Q: How did Maxwell come to possess Mijbil?

A: In 1956, Maxwell travelled to Southern Iraq, where he learned that otters were commonly found in the marshes of the Tigris region. His friend arranged for an otter to be sent to him. When Maxwell returned to his bedroom in Basra after collecting his mail, he found two Arabs sitting on the floor with a sack beside them. They handed him a note from his friend saying that the otter was for him.

Q: Why was Maxwell surprised when he first saw Mijbil?

A: When Mijbil emerged from the sack, Maxwell found that he resembled a small dragon from medieval imagination. His body was covered with symmetrical pointed scales of dried mud, which concealed his actual appearance. It took Maxwell nearly a month to remove all the mud and see the otter in his true colours.

Q: Why was the otter named Maxwell's otter?

A: Mijbil belonged to a species that was previously unknown to science. Zoologists eventually identified and named the species Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli, commonly referred to as Maxwell's otter, in recognition of Maxwell and his discovery of the animal.

Q: How did Mijbil behave during the first twenty-four hours after arriving?

A: During the first twenty-four hours, Mijbil was neither hostile nor friendly. He remained aloof and indifferent and preferred to sleep on the floor as far away from Maxwell's bed as possible. However, on the second night, he climbed onto Maxwell's bed and slept in the crook of his knees, indicating the beginning of a bond between them.

Q: What happened when Maxwell took Mijbil to the bathroom for the first time?

A: When Maxwell took Mijbil to the bathroom, the otter became extremely excited on seeing water. He plunged, rolled, and swam underwater in the bathtub, splashing water everywhere. This behaviour revealed his natural love for water and his playful personality.

Q: How did Mijbil demonstrate his intelligence?

A: Mijbil demonstrated his intelligence by learning to operate the bathroom tap with his paws. He also followed Maxwell without a lead and responded when his name was called. Furthermore, he invented games using a ping-pong ball and displayed remarkable skill while playing with marbles.

Q: Why did Maxwell face difficulties while transporting Mijbil to England?

A: Maxwell faced difficulties because the airline required Mijbil to travel inside a small box measuring no more than eighteen inches square. When left inside the box, Mijbil struggled desperately and tore its lining, injuring himself in the process. Maxwell was deeply distressed by the sight but had very little time before the flight.

Q: Why does Maxwell describe the air hostess as the very queen of her kind?

A: Maxwell admired the air hostess because she responded kindly and helpfully to his difficult situation. She suggested that he could keep Mijbil on his knee instead of leaving him confined in the box. Her understanding and compassionate behaviour relieved Maxwell of considerable anxiety, making him deeply grateful to her.

Q: What happened when Mijbil escaped from the box during the flight?

A: As soon as the box was opened, Mijbil rushed out and ran down the aircraft aisle. His sudden appearance frightened the passengers, and a woman screamed that she had seen a rat. Maxwell tried to catch him but accidentally landed face-first in someone's curry. Eventually, Mijbil recognised Maxwell, jumped onto his knee, and affectionately nuzzled his face and neck.

Q: What game did Mijbil invent using the damaged suitcase?

A: Mijbil discovered that the sloping lid of a damaged suitcase could be used to create an entertaining game. He placed a ping-pong ball at the higher end and watched it roll down. He then ran to the lower end, hid, and suddenly jumped out to catch the ball. He repeatedly carried the ball back to the higher end and continued playing.

Q: What were Mijbil's unusual habits during his walks in London?

A: During his walks, Mijbil developed certain repetitive habits. He would sometimes run along a low wall opposite Maxwell's flat, particularly on his way home. His playful behaviour attracted the attention of schoolchildren and teachers, who watched him with considerable interest and distraction.

Q: Why did the Londoners fail to recognise Mijbil?

A: Most Londoners were unfamiliar with otters and could not identify Mijbil correctly. They made several amusing guesses about his identity, mistaking him for animals such as a baby seal, squirrel, walrus, hippopotamus, beaver, bear cub, and even a brontosaur. Their confusion highlights how unusual Mijbil appeared to them.

Q: What does the chapter reveal about the natural behaviour of otters?

A: The chapter reveals that otters are playful, intelligent, curious, and strongly attracted to water. They enjoy splashing, swimming, and manipulating objects with their paws. Mijbil's ability to invent games, operate a tap, and develop routines also demonstrates the intelligence and individuality of these animals.

Q: How did Mijbil develop a bond with Maxwell?

A: Initially, Mijbil remained distant and indifferent towards Maxwell. Gradually, he became comfortable in Maxwell's presence and began sleeping close to him. He followed Maxwell without a lead, responded to his name, played around him, and showed affection by nuzzling his face and neck. These behaviours demonstrated the development of trust and companionship between them.

In London, Maxwell continued to observe Mijbil's unusual habits and playful activities. Q: Describe the difficulties Maxwell faced while bringing Mijbil from Iraq to England.

A: Transporting Mijbil from Iraq to England was one of the most challenging experiences in Maxwell's account. The airline travelling directly to London did not accept animals, so Maxwell had to arrange a journey through Paris. The airline required Mijbil to be transported in a small box measuring no more than eighteen inches square. Maxwell placed the otter inside the box before leaving for a hurried meal, hoping that the animal would become accustomed to it. However, when Maxwell returned, he found blood around the air holes and gaps in the box. Mijbil had struggled violently and torn the lining to shreds, injuring himself. Maxwell removed the damaged lining to eliminate sharp edges, but there was very little time left before departure. During the journey to the airport, Maxwell had to hold down the box's lid. On the aircraft, the air hostess kindly suggested that Mijbil could sit on Maxwell's knee. When the box was opened, however, Mijbil immediately escaped and ran through the aircraft, frightening several passengers. After a brief search, Mijbil returned to Maxwell and affectionately nuzzled his face and neck. The episode highlights the practical difficulties of transporting an unfamiliar animal and Maxwell's concern for his pet's safety and comfort.
