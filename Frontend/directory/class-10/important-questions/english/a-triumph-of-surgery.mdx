Chapter revision of A Triumph of Surgery by James Herriot. Practise short, long, competency-based and extract-based questions. Tap any question to reveal its answer.

### Section A: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

Q: Why was Mrs Pumphrey worried about Tricki?

A: Mrs Pumphrey was worried because Tricki had become listless and lacked energy. She believed that he was suffering from malnutrition. However, her excessive love and overfeeding had made him extremely fat and unhealthy. His lack of exercise further worsened his condition.

Q: What was the main cause of Tricki's illness?

A: The main cause of Tricki's illness was overfeeding and lack of exercise. Mrs Pumphrey regularly gave him rich food, cream cakes, chocolates, malt, cod-liver oil and Horlicks. She could not refuse him his favourite treats, which made him obese and lethargic.

Q: Why did Mr Herriot advise Mrs Pumphrey to reduce Tricki's food and increase his exercise?

A: Mr Herriot realised that Tricki's obesity and lack of energy were caused by overfeeding and insufficient physical activity. He advised Mrs Pumphrey to reduce his food intake and provide him with more exercise. He knew that a strict diet and an active routine were necessary to restore Tricki's health.

Q: Why did Mr Herriot decide to take Tricki to the hospital?

A: Mr Herriot decided to hospitalise Tricki when his condition deteriorated. Tricki refused to eat, suffered from vomiting, panted continuously and showed no interest in going for walks. Herriot realised that removing him from his overindulgent environment was necessary for his recovery.

Q: Why was Mrs Pumphrey reluctant to send Tricki to the hospital?

A: Mrs Pumphrey was deeply attached to Tricki and feared that he would pine and die without seeing her every day. She was emotionally dependent on him and found it difficult to imagine being separated from him. However, Mr Herriot firmly insisted on hospitalising Tricki for his own good.

Q: What arrangements did Mrs Pumphrey make when Tricki was taken to the hospital?

A: Mrs Pumphrey's household staff brought Tricki's day bed, night bed, cushions, toys, rubber rings, food bowls and other belongings. She also threw an armful of his coats through the car window as Mr Herriot drove away. These arrangements reflected her excessive concern for Tricki's comfort.

Q: How did Tricki initially behave at the veterinary hospital?

A: Initially, Tricki appeared weak and uninterested in his surroundings. He lay motionless on the carpet while the other dogs briefly inspected him and then ignored him. Mr Herriot provided him with a comfortable bed, withheld food for two days and gave him plenty of water.

Q: How did the company of other dogs help Tricki recover?

A: The company of other dogs encouraged Tricki to become active and playful. He began running around the garden, playing with them and participating in their activities. Their active lifestyle helped him lose excess weight and regain his strength and energy.

Q: Why did Mr Herriot not give Tricki any medicine?

A: Mr Herriot understood that Tricki's illness was primarily caused by overfeeding and a lack of exercise rather than a condition requiring medication. Therefore, he allowed Tricki to recover naturally through controlled feeding, plenty of water, regular activity and the company of other dogs. Tricki recovered without any medicinal treatment.

Q: Why did Mrs Pumphrey send eggs, wine and brandy to the veterinary hospital?

A: Mrs Pumphrey sent eggs, wine and brandy because she believed that these items would help restore Tricki's strength and improve his health. Ironically, the eggs were enjoyed by Mr Herriot and his colleagues, while the wine and brandy provided them with additional comforts.

Q: Why was Mr Herriot tempted to keep Tricki as a permanent guest?

A: Mr Herriot was tempted to keep Tricki at the hospital because Mrs Pumphrey's generous supplies of eggs, wine and brandy made life very comfortable for him and his colleagues. However, he knew that Mrs Pumphrey was worried about her pet, so he decided to inform her when Tricki had recovered.

Q: How did Tricki look when Mrs Pumphrey came to collect him?

A: After two weeks at the hospital, Tricki had transformed into a healthy, active and muscular dog. He ran around the garden with the other dogs and kept up with them effortlessly. His remarkable physical transformation showed that proper food and exercise had restored his health.

Q: Why did Tricki leap into Mrs Pumphrey's lap when he saw her?

A: Tricki was delighted to see his mistress after being separated from her for a fortnight. He leapt into her lap, licked her face and barked excitedly. His behaviour showed his affection for Mrs Pumphrey and his happiness at returning home.

Q: Why did Mrs Pumphrey call Tricki's recovery a triumph of surgery?

A: Mrs Pumphrey was amazed by Tricki's remarkable recovery and believed that Mr Herriot had performed a great medical achievement. She called it a triumph of surgery out of gratitude and admiration. However, the statement is ironic because Tricki had undergone no surgery or medicinal treatment; he had recovered through a balanced diet and exercise.

### Section B: Long Answer Questions (5-6 Marks)

Q: Give a character sketch of Mrs Pumphrey.

A: Mrs Pumphrey is a wealthy, affectionate and emotionally attached woman who loves her pet dog, Tricki, immensely. However, her love is expressed through excessive pampering and overindulgence rather than responsible care. She feeds Tricki rich food, cream cakes, chocolates and various supplements, believing that he needs additional nourishment. Despite Mr Herriot's repeated advice to control his diet and increase his exercise, she cannot bear to refuse him his favourite treats. Her actions ultimately make Tricki obese and seriously ill. Mrs Pumphrey is also extremely anxious and emotionally dependent on Tricki. When Mr Herriot takes him to the hospital, she fears that he will die without her. She frequently calls to enquire about his recovery and sends eggs, wine and brandy to improve his strength. Although her intentions are loving, her lack of understanding causes harm to her beloved pet. Her character highlights the difference between excessive indulgence and responsible affection.

Q: Give a character sketch of Mr James Herriot.

A: Mr James Herriot is a knowledgeable, observant, practical and compassionate veterinary surgeon. He quickly identifies that Tricki's illness is caused by overfeeding and lack of exercise rather than malnutrition. He advises Mrs Pumphrey to regulate Tricki's diet and provide him with adequate physical activity. When Tricki's condition deteriorates, Herriot takes decisive action by admitting him to the veterinary hospital. He understands that Tricki needs to be removed from his indulgent environment and placed in a healthier one. Instead of relying on unnecessary medicines or surgery, he allows the dog to recover through controlled feeding, exercise and interaction with other dogs. Herriot is also tactful and sensible. Although he recognises the benefits of Mrs Pumphrey's generous gifts for himself and his colleagues, he does not exploit the situation indefinitely. He informs her when Tricki has recovered. His character demonstrates that professional competence, practical judgement and genuine concern are essential for effective care.

Q: Justify the title A Triumph of Surgery.

A: The title A Triumph of Surgery is ironic because Tricki's recovery does not involve any surgery or medicinal treatment. Mrs Pumphrey believes that Mr Herriot has achieved an extraordinary medical success when she sees her dog transformed from an obese, lethargic animal into a healthy and energetic one. In reality, Tricki's illness is caused by excessive feeding and insufficient exercise. Mr Herriot treats him by controlling his diet, providing plenty of water and allowing him to play and exercise with other dogs. Within two weeks, Tricki regains his strength and loses his excess weight. Mrs Pumphrey misunderstands the actual cause of his recovery and attributes it to the veterinary surgeon's medical expertise. Thus, the title creates humour through irony and highlights the effectiveness of simple, sensible care over unnecessary medical intervention.

Q: Excessive love and care can sometimes be harmful. Explain with reference to the story.

A: The story A Triumph of Surgery demonstrates that excessive love and care can become harmful when they are not guided by understanding and responsibility. Mrs Pumphrey loves Tricki deeply and wants to provide him with every possible comfort. She feeds him rich food, cream cakes, chocolates and nutritional supplements, believing that these will improve his health. However, her indulgence makes Tricki obese, lethargic and seriously ill. Even when Mr Herriot advises her to reduce his food intake and increase his exercise, she cannot bring herself to follow his instructions consistently. Her affection, therefore, has the opposite effect of what she intends. Tricki recovers only after being placed under Mr Herriot's care, where he receives a controlled diet and regular exercise. The story teaches us that genuine love is not simply about fulfilling every desire. Responsible care sometimes requires discipline, restraint and decisions that may initially seem unpleasant.

Q: Compare Tricki's life at Mrs Pumphrey's house with his life at the veterinary hospital.

A: Tricki's life at Mrs Pumphrey's house is characterised by luxury, excessive pampering and inactivity. He receives rich food, cream cakes, chocolates and various supplements. He also has numerous coats, cushions, beds and toys. However, his comfortable lifestyle deprives him of adequate exercise and eventually makes him obese and unhealthy. In contrast, life at the veterinary hospital is simple and active. Tricki initially receives only water, and his food is gradually reintroduced. He lives alongside other dogs, runs around the garden, plays with them and develops healthier eating habits. He receives no medicines but gradually becomes fit and energetic. The contrast demonstrates that material comfort and indulgence do not necessarily ensure well-being. Tricki's recovery proves that a balanced diet, physical activity and a healthy environment are more important than excessive luxury.

Q: Describe Tricki's recovery at the veterinary hospital.

A: When Tricki arrives at the veterinary hospital, he is weak, lethargic and uninterested in his surroundings. Mr Herriot provides him with a comfortable bed, gives him plenty of water and withholds food for the first two days. Gradually, Tricki begins to show interest in his surroundings and responds to the sounds of the other dogs. On the third day, he starts interacting with them and follows them into the garden. He also develops an appetite and begins eating with enthusiasm. As the days pass, he becomes an active member of the group, running, playing and participating in their activities. His recovery takes place without any medicinal treatment. Within a fortnight, Tricki becomes a healthy, muscular and energetic dog. His transformation demonstrates the importance of proper nutrition, exercise and a suitable environment.

Q: How does the story use humour and irony to convey its message?

A: James Herriot uses humour and irony throughout A Triumph of Surgery to highlight the consequences of excessive pampering. Tricki's appearance is humorously compared to a bloated sausage, emphasising his extreme obesity. Mrs Pumphrey's elaborate preparations for his hospital stay, including numerous beds, cushions, toys, bowls and coats, further highlight her excessive concern for material comforts. The humour becomes more pronounced when Mrs Pumphrey sends eggs, wine and brandy to improve Tricki's health. Instead of being consumed by the dog, these provisions are enjoyed by Mr Herriot and his colleagues. The greatest irony occurs when Mrs Pumphrey calls Tricki's recovery a triumph of surgery, even though no surgery or medicine is used. His recovery results from a controlled diet and regular exercise. Through humour and irony, the author criticises excessive indulgence and emphasises the value of practical, responsible care.

### Section C: Competency-Based and Value-Based Questions

Q: Mrs Pumphrey believed Tricki was suffering from malnutrition, although he was overweight. What does this reveal about her understanding of pet care?

A: This reveals that Mrs Pumphrey lacked a proper understanding of Tricki's nutritional and physical needs. She mistakenly believed that his lack of energy indicated malnutrition and responded by feeding him more food and supplements. She failed to recognise that overfeeding and insufficient exercise were responsible for his poor health. Her example shows that affection must be supported by knowledge and responsible decision-making.

Q: If Mr Herriot had not intervened, what might have happened to Tricki? Explain.

A: If Mr Herriot had not intervened, Tricki's condition might have continued to deteriorate because Mrs Pumphrey was unable to control his diet or provide adequate exercise. His obesity, lethargy and other symptoms could have led to more serious health complications. By removing Tricki from his unhealthy environment and providing appropriate care, Mr Herriot prevented his condition from worsening and helped him regain his health.

Q: What role did discipline and routine play in Tricki's recovery?

A: Discipline and routine played a crucial role in Tricki's recovery. At the hospital, his food intake was controlled, and he was encouraged to remain active. Interacting with other dogs motivated him to run, play and exercise regularly. Unlike his previous lifestyle, which involved excessive feeding and little activity, his new routine restored his fitness and energy. The story shows that consistent healthy habits are essential for physical well-being.

Q: What would you have done if you had been in Mrs Pumphrey's position?

A: If I had been in Mrs Pumphrey's position, I would have followed the veterinary surgeon's advice carefully. I would have provided Tricki with a balanced diet, controlled his intake of treats and ensured that he received adequate exercise. I would also have monitored his health regularly instead of assuming that additional food would improve his condition. This would have allowed me to express my affection through responsible care rather than excessive indulgence.

Q: What is the central message of the story? How is it relevant to our lives?

A: The central message of the story is that excessive indulgence can be harmful, even when it arises from genuine affection. Mrs Pumphrey's desire to provide Tricki with every comfort ultimately damages his health. His recovery demonstrates that discipline, moderation and proper care are essential for well-being. The message is relevant to our lives because excessive indulgence can also affect children, family members and others we care about. Giving someone everything they desire may prevent them from developing healthy habits and self-discipline. True care involves understanding their needs, setting appropriate limits and making responsible decisions for their long-term benefit.

### Section D: Important Extract-Based Questions

Q: Who is being described in this extract: He had become hugely fat, like a bloated sausage with a leg at each corner? Why had he become so fat?

A: Tricki, Mrs Pumphrey's pet dog, is being described. He had become fat because Mrs Pumphrey overfed him with rich food, cream cakes, chocolates and nutritional supplements while providing insufficient exercise.

Q: What does the comparison of Tricki with a bloated sausage suggest? Which character trait of Mrs Pumphrey is responsible for this situation?

A: The comparison emphasises Tricki's extreme obesity and unhealthy physical condition. It also creates a humorous image of his appearance. Mrs Pumphrey's excessive affection and overindulgence are responsible for Tricki's condition.

Q: Where was Tricki at this point in the story: He had no medicinal treatment of any kind but all day he ran about with the dogs, joining in their friendly scrimmages? How did his activities help him recover?

A: Tricki was staying at Mr Herriot's veterinary hospital. Running and playing with other dogs provided him with regular physical exercise. This helped him lose excess weight, regain his strength and become active.

Q: What does Tricki's hospital routine reveal about Mr Herriot's approach to treatment? What does the word scrimmages mean in this context?

A: It reveals that Mr Herriot was practical and observant. He understood that Tricki needed a controlled diet and exercise rather than unnecessary medication. The word scrimmages refers to the rough but friendly physical play among the dogs.

Q: Who said these words, and why: Oh, Mr Herriot, how can I ever thank you? This is a triumph of surgery?

A: Mrs Pumphrey said these words to Mr Herriot when she saw Tricki's remarkable recovery. She was overwhelmed with gratitude and believed that he had achieved an extraordinary medical success.

Q: Why is the expression triumph of surgery ironic? What is the main message conveyed through this incident?

A: It is ironic because Tricki had not undergone surgery or received any medicinal treatment. He had recovered through a controlled diet, exercise and interaction with other dogs. The incident conveys that simple, appropriate care and healthy habits can sometimes be more effective than unnecessary medical intervention.

### Section E: Additional Important Questions for Practice

Q: Why is Mrs Pumphrey's behaviour described as foolishly indulgent?

A: Mrs Pumphrey is described as foolishly indulgent because she repeatedly fulfils Tricki's desire for rich food and treats without considering their effect on his health. Even when advised to reduce his food intake, she gives in to his demands. Her excessive pampering causes his obesity and illness, showing that affection without understanding can be harmful.

Q: What does Tricki's transformation tell us about the relationship between lifestyle and health?

A: Tricki's transformation demonstrates that lifestyle has a significant influence on health. His earlier routine of excessive eating and minimal exercise makes him obese and lethargic. At the hospital, a controlled diet and regular physical activity help him become fit and energetic. His recovery highlights the importance of moderation, exercise and healthy daily habits.

Q: Why was Tricki happier and healthier at the veterinary hospital despite receiving fewer comforts?

A: Tricki was happier and healthier at the veterinary hospital because he had opportunities to run, play and interact with other dogs. Although he received fewer luxuries than he had at home, his daily routine was more suitable for his physical needs. The experience demonstrates that health and happiness depend more on appropriate care and activity than on material comforts.

Q: How does James Herriot contrast appearance with actual well-being in the story?

A: The story contrasts Tricki's luxurious appearance and comfortable surroundings with his poor physical health. At home, he has numerous coats, beds, cushions and toys, yet he is obese and lethargic. At the hospital, he lives with fewer comforts but becomes active and healthy. The contrast shows that outward luxury does not necessarily indicate genuine well-being.

Q: What does the story teach us about professional responsibility?

A: The story teaches that professional responsibility requires sound judgement and a commitment to the client's or patient's actual needs. Mr Herriot recognises that Tricki's illness results from overfeeding and lack of exercise. He takes decisive action despite Mrs Pumphrey's emotional objections and provides the care Tricki genuinely needs. His conduct demonstrates the importance of expertise, practical decision-making and professional integrity.
