### Section A: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

Q: What was Bholi's real name? Why was she called Bholi?

A: Bholi's real name was Sulekha. Since childhood, everyone called her Bholi, meaning a simpleton, because she had suffered possible brain damage after falling off a cot when she was ten months old. She was considered slow in learning and understanding. She also stammered while speaking, which made others mock her.

Q: Why was Bholi neglected by her family?

A: Bholi was neglected because her family considered her unattractive and less intelligent than her siblings. Smallpox had left deep marks on her face, and she stammered while speaking. Her parents paid little attention to her needs, and she was often made to wear her sisters' old clothes. Unlike her siblings, she received little encouragement or affection.

Q: What physical and speech difficulties did Bholi face during childhood?

A: When Bholi was ten months old, she fell off a cot and may have suffered brain damage. At the age of two, she contracted smallpox, which left deep black marks on her face. She did not begin speaking until she was five, and when she eventually started speaking, she stammered. These difficulties made her timid and vulnerable to ridicule.

Q: Why was Ramlal worried about Bholi?

A: Ramlal was worried about Bholi because she had pockmarks on her face and was considered less intelligent than other children. He feared that these characteristics would make it difficult for her to find a suitable husband. Unlike his other daughters, who were healthy and good-looking, Bholi's future marriage prospects concerned him.

Q: Why was Bholi sent to school?

A: A primary school for girls opened in Bholi's village, and the Tehsildar instructed Ramlal to set an example by sending his daughters to school. Ramlal's wife opposed girls' education because she believed that educated girls would have difficulty getting married. However, she suggested sending Bholi because she thought that her appearance and perceived lack of intelligence made marriage unlikely anyway.

Q: Why was Bholi frightened when her father took her to school?

A: Bholi was frightened because she did not know what a school was like or what would happen there. She also remembered that their old cow, Lakshmi, had recently been taken away and sold. She feared that something similar might happen to her and initially resisted going to school.

Q: How was Bholi treated differently on her first day at school?

A: At school, Bholi encountered a teacher who treated her with kindness and patience. Unlike the people who mocked her, the teacher smiled at her, encouraged her to speak and praised her efforts. She reassured Bholi that she could overcome her difficulties through education and regular attendance. This positive treatment gave Bholi hope and confidence.

Q: How did Bholi react when her teacher asked her name?

A: When the teacher asked her name, Bholi tried to speak but could not get beyond a few stammered sounds. She began crying because she feared that the other girls would laugh at her. However, the teacher encouraged her patiently, and Bholi eventually managed to say her full name, Sulekha, with considerable relief.

Q: What promise did Bholi's teacher make to her?

A: Bholi's teacher promised that if she attended school regularly, she would learn to read and become more knowledgeable than anyone else in the village. She assured Bholi that education would help her speak confidently and earn the respect of others. The teacher's encouragement filled Bholi with hope for a better future.

Q: How did education transform Bholi?

A: Education transformed Bholi from a timid, neglected girl into a confident and self-respecting young woman. Her teacher's kindness helped her overcome her fear of speaking, while her education gave her knowledge and the courage to express her opinions. Ultimately, she refused to marry a greedy man who demanded dowry, demonstrating her independence and self-respect.

Q: Who was Bishamber Nath? Why did Bholi's parents agree to marry her to him?

A: Bishamber Nath was a wealthy grocer from another village. He was considerably older than Bholi's father, walked with a limp and had children from his first marriage. Bholi's parents accepted his proposal because he owned a shop and a house, had money in the bank and initially demanded no dowry. They considered the match fortunate because they feared Bholi might otherwise remain unmarried.

Q: Why did Bishamber Nath demand five thousand rupees during the wedding?

A: When Bishamber Nath saw the pockmarks on Bholi's face during the wedding ceremony, he decided to exploit the situation. He demanded five thousand rupees from Ramlal before agreeing to marry her. His demand revealed his greed and his willingness to humiliate the bride's family for financial gain.

Q: Why did Ramlal agree to pay Bishamber Nath?

A: Ramlal agreed to pay because he feared that Bishamber's withdrawal would bring shame upon his family. Bishamber threatened to leave unless he received five thousand rupees. Ramlal pleaded with him to accept two thousand rupees, but eventually paid the demanded amount because he was worried about his family's reputation in the village.

Q: Why did Bholi refuse to marry Bishamber Nath?

A: Bholi refused to marry Bishamber because he behaved greedily and demanded dowry after noticing the marks on her face. She realised that he was willing to humiliate her family for money and did not respect her as a person. Her education had given her the confidence to reject this unjust treatment and defend her dignity.

Q: How did Bholi surprise everyone at the wedding?

A: At the wedding, Bholi refused to accept Bishamber's demand for dowry. She threw the wedding garland into the sacred fire, removed her veil and spoke clearly without stammering. She told her father to take back the money and declared that she would not marry Bishamber. Her confidence astonished the guests and family members who had always considered her weak and helpless.

Q: What did Bholi say when her father worried about her future after she rejected the marriage?

A: Bholi calmly reassured her father that he need not worry about her future. She promised to serve her parents in their old age and stated that she would teach at the same school where she had received her education. Her response demonstrated her independence, gratitude and confidence in her ability to support herself.

Q: What role did Bholi's teacher play in her life?

A: Bholi's teacher played a decisive role in transforming her life. She treated Bholi with kindness, encouraged her to speak despite her stammering and assured her that education would help her gain confidence and respect. Through her patience and support, the teacher helped Bholi overcome her fear and develop the courage to stand up against injustice.

Q: What is the significance of Bholi's real name, Sulekha, at the end of the story?

A: At the end of the story, Bholi is referred to by her real name, Sulekha, symbolising her transformation. Earlier, the nickname Bholi reflected how others perceived her as a simpleton. After receiving education, she develops confidence, intelligence and self-respect. The use of her real name highlights that she has emerged from the identity imposed on her by society.

### Section B: Long Answer Questions (5-6 Marks)

Q: Write a character sketch of Bholi.

A: Bholi, whose real name is Sulekha, is the protagonist of K. A. Abbas's story Bholi. During childhood, she suffers from possible brain damage after falling off a cot and later contracts smallpox, which leaves deep marks on her face. She also stammers while speaking. Because of these difficulties, her family neglects her, and other children make fun of her. Initially, Bholi is timid, frightened and lacking in confidence. Her life changes when she begins attending school and encounters a kind and encouraging teacher. The teacher helps her overcome her fear of speaking and inspires her to value education. As Bholi grows older, education gives her confidence, knowledge and self-respect. When Bishamber Nath demands dowry during their wedding ceremony, she courageously rejects him. She refuses to sacrifice her dignity merely to satisfy society's expectations. Bholi's transformation demonstrates the power of education and encouragement. She emerges as an independent, courageous and self-respecting young woman who is willing to challenge injustice.

Q: Describe the role of Bholi's teacher in transforming her life.

A: Bholi's teacher plays the most important role in transforming her from a timid and neglected girl into a confident young woman. Before attending school, Bholi is mocked by other children and receives little attention from her family. Her physical appearance and stammering make her afraid to speak. On her first day at school, the teacher treats her with kindness and patience. When Bholi struggles to say her name, the teacher does not laugh at her. Instead, she praises her efforts and encourages her to try again. The teacher promises that regular attendance will help Bholi learn to read, speak confidently and earn the respect of others. She also gives Bholi a picture book, making learning an enjoyable experience. Over time, education strengthens Bholi's confidence and self-respect. At her wedding, she refuses to marry Bishamber Nath after he demands dowry. Her decision demonstrates the lasting influence of her teacher's encouragement. The teacher's role shows that kindness, education and positive guidance can empower individuals to overcome social barriers and stand up for their rights.

Q: How does education bring about a transformation in Bholi's personality?

A: Education brings about a remarkable transformation in Bholi's personality. Initially, she is a timid and neglected child who suffers from stammering and is frequently mocked because of her appearance. Her family considers her less intelligent than her siblings, and she lacks the confidence to express herself. Her first day at school marks a turning point. Her teacher treats her with kindness, encourages her to speak and assures her that education will help her gain knowledge and respect. This support gives Bholi hope and motivates her to continue learning. As the years pass, Bholi develops confidence, independence and self-respect. She learns to think for herself instead of blindly accepting society's expectations. When Bishamber Nath demands dowry at the wedding, Bholi refuses to marry him despite pressure from her family and guests. She speaks clearly and defends her dignity. Thus, education empowers Bholi to challenge injustice and make independent decisions. Her story demonstrates that education is not merely about acquiring knowledge; it also develops confidence, awareness and the courage to stand up for one's rights.

Q: Describe the events that led Bholi to reject Bishamber Nath at the wedding.

A: Bholi's parents arrange her marriage to Bishamber Nath, a wealthy grocer who is much older than Bholi and has children from his first marriage. They accept his proposal because he owns property and initially demands no dowry. They fear that Bholi may not find another suitable match. During the wedding ceremony, Bishamber sees the pockmarks on Bholi's face. He becomes unwilling to marry her unless Ramlal pays him five thousand rupees. Despite Ramlal's pleas, Bishamber refuses to reduce his demand. Ramlal eventually pays the money because he fears public humiliation. However, Bholi refuses to accept the situation. She throws the wedding garland into the sacred fire, removes her veil and declares that she will not marry a greedy and contemptible man. Her decision shocks the guests and family members, who had always considered her weak and helpless. Bholi's refusal demonstrates her courage, self-respect and independence. It also exposes Bishamber's greed and challenges the social practice of dowry.

Q: Bholi's teacher was the real architect of her transformation. Explain.

A: Bholi's teacher was instrumental in transforming her personality. Before entering school, Bholi was neglected by her family and mocked by other children. Her physical appearance and stammering made her fearful and withdrawn. On her first day at school, the teacher treated her with compassion and patience. When Bholi struggled to speak, the teacher encouraged her instead of ridiculing her. She praised her efforts and assured her that education would help her become knowledgeable and confident. The teacher's support gave Bholi hope and helped her recognise her own potential. Through education, Bholi developed the confidence to express her thoughts and make independent decisions. At the wedding, she refused to marry Bishamber Nath after he demanded dowry. This act demonstrated that she had developed the self-respect and courage to challenge injustice. The teacher's influence proves that a supportive educator can change a student's life by providing knowledge, encouragement and emotional security.

Q: Bholi's refusal to marry Bishamber Nath was an act of courage and self-respect. Justify.

A: Bholi's refusal to marry Bishamber Nath demonstrates her courage and self-respect. Her parents had arranged the marriage because Bishamber was wealthy and appeared to be a suitable match despite his advanced age and physical disability. During the wedding, Bishamber noticed the marks on Bholi's face and demanded five thousand rupees from her father. His demand revealed that he valued money and appearances more than Bholi's dignity. Although her father feared social humiliation and paid the money, Bholi refused to accept the marriage. She threw the garland into the sacred fire and declared that she would not marry a greedy and contemptible man. Her decision was particularly significant because her family and the wedding guests had always underestimated her. Education had given her the confidence to challenge social expectations and reject an exploitative relationship. Thus, Bholi's refusal demonstrates that self-respect is more important than social approval or financial security.

Q: Discuss the theme of gender discrimination in the story Bholi.

A: Gender discrimination is an important theme in Bholi. The story portrays a society in which girls are often valued according to their physical appearance and marriage prospects rather than their abilities or individuality. Ramlal's sons receive an education in the city, while his daughters face restrictions based on traditional beliefs. His wife opposes girls' education because she fears that educated girls may have difficulty getting married. Bholi is particularly neglected because she has pockmarks on her face and stammers. Her family considers her a burden and sends her to school partly because they believe she has little chance of marriage. The discrimination becomes more evident when Bishamber Nath demands dowry after noticing Bholi's facial marks. He treats her as someone whose family must pay for the privilege of marrying her. However, education empowers Bholi to reject this treatment. Her decision challenges gender inequality, the dowry system and the belief that a woman's worth depends on marriage.

Q: What message does K. A. Abbas convey through the story Bholi?

A: Through Bholi, K. A. Abbas conveys the message that education, kindness and encouragement can transform lives. Bholi begins as a neglected and frightened child who lacks confidence because of her physical appearance and speech difficulties. Her teacher treats her with respect and encourages her to learn. Education gradually gives Bholi the knowledge and confidence needed to express herself and make independent decisions. The story also condemns gender discrimination, social prejudice and the dowry system. Bishamber Nath's demand for money exposes the cruelty of a society that values wealth and appearance over human dignity. Bholi's refusal to marry him demonstrates that self-respect is more important than social approval. Her decision also challenges the assumption that a woman's future depends entirely on marriage. Ultimately, the story highlights the transformative power of education and the importance of treating every individual with dignity and respect.

Q: Compare Bholi's personality at the beginning and at the end of the story.

A: At the beginning of the story, Bholi is a timid, neglected and insecure child. She suffers from stammering and has deep marks on her face because of smallpox. Her family considers her less intelligent than her siblings, and other children frequently mock her. Consequently, she is afraid to speak and has little confidence in herself. Her personality begins to change when she attends school and meets a kind teacher. The teacher encourages her to speak, praises her efforts and assures her that education will help her earn respect. At the end of the story, Bholi has become confident, courageous and self-respecting. She openly rejects Bishamber Nath when he demands dowry and refuses to marry him despite pressure from her family and society. The contrast between Bholi's initial fearfulness and her final confidence demonstrates the power of education, encouragement and self-belief.

Q: Why did Bholi's parents accept Bishamber Nath's marriage proposal despite his age and physical condition?

A: Bholi's parents accepted Bishamber Nath's proposal because they believed that he offered a rare opportunity for Bholi to get married. She had pockmarks on her face and was considered less intelligent, so her parents feared that she might remain unmarried. Bishamber was a wealthy grocer who owned a shop and a house and had money in the bank. He initially demanded no dowry, which made the proposal appear especially attractive to Bholi's parents. Although Ramlal recognised that Bishamber was much older than himself, walked with a limp and had grown-up children from his first marriage, his wife persuaded him to accept the proposal. Their decision reflects the social pressure on parents to marry off their daughters and the tendency to prioritise financial security and social acceptance over the daughter's wishes and well-being.

### Section C: Higher-Order Thinking Questions

Q: Was Bholi right to reject Bishamber Nath? Give reasons for your answer.

A: Yes, Bholi was right to reject Bishamber Nath. Although her parents considered him a suitable match because of his wealth, his behaviour during the wedding revealed his greed and lack of respect for her. After noticing the pockmarks on Bholi's face, Bishamber demanded five thousand rupees before agreeing to marry her. His demand humiliated Bholi's father and demonstrated that he valued money more than human dignity. Bholi had every right to refuse a marriage in which she was being treated as an object of financial negotiation. Her education had helped her understand her worth and develop the courage to defend herself. Her decision shows that marriage should be based on mutual respect rather than financial exploitation or social pressure.

Q: Why did Bholi agree to the marriage initially but reject it later?

A: Initially, Bholi agreed to the marriage because she had grown up being neglected and considered herself fortunate that someone was willing to marry her. She was also concerned about her parents' wishes and the social expectations surrounding marriage. However, when Bishamber Nath demanded five thousand rupees after seeing her face, she recognised his greed and disrespect. She understood that he was willing to exploit her family's vulnerability for financial gain. Education had given Bholi the confidence to question such treatment and recognise her own dignity. Consequently, she rejected the marriage rather than submit to humiliation. Her decision demonstrates how education can change a person's understanding of self-worth and empower them to challenge injustice.

Q: What does the story reveal about society's attitude towards physical appearance?

A: The story reveals that society often judges people by their physical appearance rather than their character or abilities. Bholi is neglected by her family and mocked by other children because smallpox has left marks on her face and she stammers while speaking. Her parents worry about her marriage prospects because they believe that her appearance makes her less desirable. Bishamber Nath initially agrees to marry her but demands money after noticing her pockmarks. These incidents show how physical differences can lead to discrimination and humiliation. However, Bholi's intelligence, courage and self-respect become evident when she rejects Bishamber's demand. The story challenges superficial judgments and emphasises that human worth should be determined by character, dignity and abilities rather than outward appearance.

Q: How does the story challenge the traditional belief that a woman's future depends entirely on marriage?

A: The story challenges this belief through Bholi's decision to reject Bishamber Nath and choose an independent future. Her parents consider marriage essential because they fear that she may remain unmarried due to her appearance and speech difficulties. They accept Bishamber's proposal despite his age and physical condition because he is wealthy and initially demands no dowry. When he later demands money, they worry more about social humiliation than Bholi's dignity. However, Bholi refuses to marry him and calmly assures her father that she will support her parents in their old age and teach at the school where she studied. Her decision demonstrates that a woman can lead a meaningful and respectable life through education, employment and independence. The story challenges the idea that marriage is the only acceptable future for a woman.

Q: What is the significance of the teacher's reaction at the end of the story?

A: At the end of the story, Bholi's teacher expresses satisfaction and pride in her transformation. The teacher had encouraged Bholi from her first day at school, helping her overcome her fear of speaking and develop confidence. When Bholi rejects Bishamber Nath's dowry demand, the teacher witnesses the results of her efforts. Bholi's decision demonstrates that she has become independent, courageous and capable of standing up for herself. The teacher's satisfaction is compared to the fulfilment an artist feels when looking at a completed masterpiece. This comparison highlights the teacher's contribution to Bholi's development and reinforces the transformative power of education.

### Section D: NCERT Textbook Questions and Answers

Q: Bholi had many apprehensions about going to school. What made her feel that she was going to a better place than her home?

A: Bholi was frightened because she did not know what to expect at school. She was also accustomed to neglect and ridicule at home. However, on the day she was taken to school, she received a clean dress, was bathed and had oil applied to her hair. This unusual care made her feel that she was being taken somewhere better than her home. At school, she saw many girls of her age and hoped that she might make friends. Most importantly, her teacher treated her kindly, encouraged her to speak and gave her a picture book. These experiences filled her with hope and made school seem like a place where she could learn and be respected.

Instead, she praised her efforts and encouraged her to try again. The teacher assured Bholi that education would help her become knowledgeable, speak confidently and earn the respect of others. She also gave her a picture book and encouraged her to attend school regularly. Gradually, Bholi gained confidence and self-respect. At her wedding, she refused to marry Bishamber Nath when he demanded dowry. This decision demonstrated that education had empowered her to challenge injustice and make independent choices.

It also shows that education had empowered her to recognise injustice and stand up for her dignity.

Q: Bholi's real name is Sulekha. Why is she called Sulekha again towards the end of the story?

A: Bholi is called Sulekha again towards the end of the story to highlight her transformation. The nickname Bholi, meaning a simpleton, reflects the way her family and society perceived her as a timid and less intelligent girl. By rejecting Bishamber Nath's dowry demand, she proves that she is capable of making independent decisions. The use of her real name, Sulekha, symbolises her emergence from the identity imposed on her by others. It marks her transformation into a confident and independent young woman.

A: The story shows that girls are not always treated equally to boys. Ramlal's sons are sent to the city for education, while his wife initially opposes sending girls to school because she fears that education may affect their marriage prospects. Bholi is neglected because of her appearance and speech difficulties. Her parents worry about her marriage rather than focusing on her education or personal development. Bishamber Nath's demand for dowry further demonstrates the exploitation and discrimination faced by women. However, Bholi's education empowers her to challenge these social attitudes. The story advocates gender equality and highlights the importance of education in challenging discriminatory traditions.
