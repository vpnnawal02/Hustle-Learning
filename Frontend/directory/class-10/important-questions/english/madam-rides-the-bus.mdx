### Section A: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

Q: What was Valli's favourite pastime? What was her strongest desire?

A: Valli's favourite pastime was standing at the front doorway of her house and watching the activities on the street. The most fascinating sight for her was the bus travelling between her village and the nearby town. Gradually, she developed a strong desire to experience a bus ride herself, which became the most important wish of her childhood.

Q: How did Valli gather information about the bus journey?

A: Valli carefully listened to conversations between her neighbours and regular bus passengers. She also asked discreet questions to collect information about the distance to the town, the bus fare, and the duration of the journey. She learnt that the town was six miles away, the fare was thirty paise one way, and the journey took forty-five minutes.

Q: How did Valli arrange the money for her first bus journey? Was it easy for her?

A: Valli saved sixty paise by carefully collecting whatever coins came her way. She resisted the temptation to buy peppermints, toys, balloons, and other things she liked. Even at the village fair, she controlled her strong desire to ride the merry-go-round. Saving the required amount was difficult, but her determination helped her achieve her goal.

Q: How did Valli plan her first bus journey?

A: Valli planned her journey carefully. She learnt the bus timings, fare, distance, and duration of the journey. She decided to take the one-o'clock afternoon bus, reach the town at 1:45 p.m., and return by approximately 2:45 p.m. She also planned to leave the house while her mother was asleep after lunch so that she could travel without her mother's knowledge.

Q: Why did the conductor call Valli madam?

A: The conductor called Valli madam playfully because she behaved like a grown-up and insisted on managing everything independently. She refused his help while boarding the bus and confidently paid her fare. The conductor found her seriousness and self-confidence amusing, so he addressed her respectfully in a teasing manner.

Q: How did Valli react when the elderly man asked her to sit down?

A: Valli became annoyed when the elderly man advised her to sit down for her safety. She felt that he was treating her like a small child. She proudly declared that she was not a child and had paid the same fare as everyone else. Her reaction reveals her strong desire to be treated as an independent individual.

Q: Why did Valli not want to talk to the elderly woman sitting beside her?

A: Valli found the elderly woman unpleasant because of her large ear holes, ugly earrings, and the smell of betel nut she was chewing. She also disliked the woman's unnecessary questions about her journey. Valli considered herself capable of managing the trip independently and did not appreciate the woman's interference.

Q: What made Valli laugh during the bus journey?

A: Valli was amused by a young cow running in front of the bus with its tail raised. Whenever the driver sounded the horn, the frightened cow ran even faster. The sight of the cow running desperately ahead of the bus appeared extremely funny to Valli, and she laughed until tears came to her eyes.

Q: Why did Valli refuse to get off the bus at the town?

A: Valli's primary purpose was to experience a bus ride rather than explore the town. Although she was fascinated by the busy streets and shops, she admitted that she would be too afraid to explore the unfamiliar place alone. Therefore, she remained on the bus, paid another thirty paise, and began her return journey.

Q: Why did Valli refuse the conductor's offer of a cold drink?

A: Valli refused the offer because she did not have enough money to buy a cold drink and wanted to stick to her carefully planned budget. Although the conductor offered to pay for it, she firmly declined. This incident demonstrates her self-respect, financial discipline, and determination to manage her journey independently.

Q: Why did Valli's excitement diminish during the return journey?

A: During the return journey, Valli saw the same young cow that had amused her earlier lying dead beside the road after being struck by a fast-moving vehicle. The sight shocked and saddened her. She realised that the beautiful, lively animal had suddenly lost its life. The incident disturbed her so deeply that she stopped enjoying the scenery.

Q: What did Valli learn from seeing the dead cow?

A: Seeing the dead cow exposed Valli to the harsh reality of death. Earlier, she had viewed the cow as a lively and amusing creature, but seeing its lifeless body made her realise how suddenly life could end. The incident marked a change in her understanding of the world and introduced her to an experience beyond childhood excitement.

Q: Why did Valli smile to herself at the end of the story?

A: Valli smiled to herself because she had experienced an adventure that her mother and aunt knew nothing about. When her mother spoke about how many things happen without our knowledge, Valli recognised the truth of her words through her own experience. She kept her bus journey a secret and quietly enjoyed the memory of her achievement.

Q: What does the conversation between Valli's mother and aunt reveal?

A: The conversation reveals that people cannot know everything happening around them and that even when they possess information, they may not fully understand its meaning. Valli's mother expresses this idea while discussing the mysteries of life. Valli silently agrees because her journey has given her new experiences and a deeper understanding of the world.

### Section B: Long Answer Questions (5-6 Marks)

Q: Write a character sketch of Valli.

A: Valliammai, popularly known as Valli, is an eight-year-old girl who is curious, determined, self-confident, and independent. Her favourite pastime is watching the activities on the street from the doorway of her house. The bus travelling between her village and the nearby town fascinates her, and she develops a strong desire to experience a ride. Valli demonstrates remarkable determination by carefully collecting information about the journey and saving the required money. She resists the temptation to spend her savings on toys, sweets, and other attractions. Her careful planning and ability to manage her journey show her maturity and resourcefulness. She is also proud and independent. She refuses help while boarding the bus and dislikes being treated as a child. However, her confidence has limitations, as she admits that she is afraid to explore the town alone. Initially, Valli experiences the journey with excitement and amusement. However, seeing the dead cow on her return journey makes her sad and introduces her to the reality of death. Thus, Valli's character represents childhood curiosity, determination, self-respect, and the gradual development of emotional maturity.

Q: Describe Valli's first bus journey and the important experiences she had during it.

A: Valli's first bus journey was the result of careful planning and a strong desire to explore the world beyond her village. She collected information about the bus timings, fare, distance, and duration of the journey by listening to conversations and asking questions. After saving sixty paise, she boarded the afternoon bus without informing her mother. The cheerful conductor addressed her as madam, while Valli insisted on managing everything independently. During the journey, she admired the beautiful scenery, including green fields, palm trees, distant mountains, and busy streets. She was particularly amused by a young cow running in front of the bus, which made her laugh uncontrollably. When the bus reached the town, Valli decided not to get off because she was afraid to explore the unfamiliar place alone. She paid another thirty paise and began her return journey. However, her excitement turned into sadness when she saw the same cow lying dead beside the road. The sight disturbed her and made her think about the reality of death. By the time she returned home, Valli had experienced both the excitement of independence and the sadness of loss. Her journey became an important learning experience that broadened her understanding of life.

Q: How does Valli demonstrate self-confidence and independence throughout the story?

A: Valli demonstrates self-confidence and independence through her planning, decisions, and interactions with others. She independently gathers information about the bus journey by listening to conversations and asking discreet questions. She then saves the required money by resisting the temptation to spend it on toys and sweets. When she boards the bus, she refuses the conductor's help and confidently pays her fare. She dislikes being treated as a child and insists that she can take care of herself. She also refuses unnecessary advice from the elderly passengers. Valli's independence is further demonstrated by her refusal to accept the conductor's offer of a cold drink, even when he offers to pay for it. She prefers to manage her limited money herself. However, her independence does not mean that she has overcome every fear. She refuses to explore the town alone because she feels uncomfortable in an unfamiliar environment. Thus, Valli's behaviour demonstrates that she is resourceful, determined, and self-reliant, while also retaining the natural fears and limitations of a young child.

Q: Explain how Valli's first bus journey becomes a learning experience for her.

A: Valli's first bus journey becomes a learning experience because it exposes her to situations that broaden her understanding of the world. Before the journey, Valli's knowledge comes mainly from observing the street outside her house. Her fascination with the bus motivates her to gather information, save money, and plan an independent trip. This process teaches her patience, determination, and responsibility. During the journey, she experiences the excitement of travelling independently and observes the beauty of the countryside and the activity of the town. She also discovers that her confidence has limits when she decides not to explore the town alone. The most significant experience occurs when she sees the dead cow on the return journey. The animal that had earlier amused her now lies lifeless beside the road. This sight shocks her and introduces her to the reality of death. By the end of the story, Valli has gained more than the experience of a bus ride. She has learnt about independence, uncertainty, and the difference between knowing about life and understanding its deeper realities.

Q: The story presents a contrast between childhood innocence and the harsh realities of life. Explain with examples.

A: The story presents this contrast through Valli's experiences during her first bus journey. At the beginning, Valli views the world with curiosity and excitement. She is fascinated by the bus, admires the green fields and busy streets, and finds the young cow running in front of the bus extremely amusing. Her reactions reflect the innocence and enthusiasm of childhood. She enjoys observing new things and experiences the journey as an exciting adventure. However, the mood changes when Valli sees the same cow lying dead beside the road. The animal that had appeared lively and beautiful earlier now looks frightening and lifeless. Valli is deeply disturbed by the sight and loses her enthusiasm for the remaining journey. This incident introduces her to the painful reality that life can end suddenly. Her experience demonstrates that the world contains both joy and sorrow, and that growing up involves confronting experiences that challenge childhood innocence.

Q: Justify the title Madam Rides the Bus.

A: The title Madam Rides the Bus is appropriate because the entire story revolves around Valli's first bus journey and her desire to experience independence. Valli is an eight-year-old girl who dreams of travelling to the nearby town. She carefully plans the journey, saves the required money, and boards the bus without informing her mother. Her confidence and insistence on managing everything herself amuse the conductor, who playfully addresses her as madam. The title captures the humorous contrast between Valli's young age and her determination to behave like an independent adult. At the same time, it highlights the central event of the story: her bus journey, which becomes an important experience in her emotional development. Therefore, the title is simple, engaging, and closely connected to both the main event and the personality of the protagonist.

Q: What is the significance of the dead cow in the story?

A: The dead cow is a significant symbol in the story because it marks a turning point in Valli's experience. During the onward journey, she sees a young cow running in front of the bus and finds its behaviour extremely amusing. The incident fills her with joy and laughter. However, during the return journey, she sees the same cow lying dead beside the road. The sight shocks her because the lively creature she had enjoyed watching has suddenly lost its life. This experience introduces Valli to the harsh reality of death and changes her perception of the journey. She no longer feels excited about the scenery and remains disturbed by the memory of the animal. The dead cow therefore represents the suddenness of death and the painful experiences through which a person gains a deeper understanding of life.

### Section C: Competency-Based Questions

Q: Valli carefully plans her journey and saves money despite several temptations. What does this reveal about her personality?

A: Valli's careful planning and disciplined saving reveal that she is determined, patient, resourceful, and self-controlled. She understands the cost of the journey and saves sixty paise by avoiding unnecessary purchases. Even when she has an opportunity to enjoy the village fair, she suppresses her desire to ride the merry-go-round. These actions show that she is willing to make sacrifices to achieve a goal that matters to her.

Q: Why does Valli refuse the conductor's offer of a cold drink even though she would like to enjoy the town?

A: Valli refuses the offer because she wants to remain within her carefully planned budget and does not have enough money for a cold drink. She also prefers to manage her journey independently rather than accept someone else's financial help. Her decision reflects self-respect and discipline. It also shows that her primary objective is the bus ride itself, not spending money on other attractions.

Q: What can we infer about Valli from her decision not to get off the bus at the town?

A: We can infer that Valli is confident enough to undertake a planned bus journey but is still a young child with understandable fears. She is fascinated by the town's shops and busy streets, yet she admits that she would be too afraid to explore the place alone. Her decision shows that independence develops gradually and that confidence in one situation does not necessarily eliminate fear in another.

Q: How does the author use the same cow to create a contrast between the two parts of Valli's journey?

A: The author uses the same cow to create a sharp emotional contrast. During the onward journey, the cow runs playfully in front of the bus, and Valli laughs at the amusing sight. During the return journey, she sees the cow lying dead beside the road after being struck by a vehicle. The contrast between the lively animal and its lifeless body transforms Valli's excitement into sadness. This incident highlights the unpredictability of life and introduces her to the reality of death.

Q: Valli's mother says that people may know about something without fully understanding it. How does Valli's journey support this idea?

A: Valli's journey supports this idea because she gains direct experience of things she previously knew only through observation. Before the journey, she watches the bus and hears people talk about travelling to the town, but she does not fully understand the experience herself. During the trip, she experiences excitement, fear, independence, and sadness. Seeing the dead cow particularly deepens her understanding of life and death. Her experience demonstrates that personal encounters can provide a deeper understanding than information alone.

### Section E: Important Value-Based Questions

Q: What values can students learn from Valli's character?

A: Students can learn determination, self-discipline, confidence, and resourcefulness from Valli. She carefully plans her journey, saves money, and resists unnecessary spending to achieve her goal. Her actions demonstrate the importance of preparation and perseverance. At the same time, her experience with the dead cow teaches readers that life contains unexpected events and that maturity involves learning from both joyful and painful experiences.

Q: Do you think Valli was right to undertake the bus journey without informing her mother? Give a reasoned answer.

A: Valli's journey demonstrates independence and determination, but undertaking it without informing her mother was not a responsible decision. At eight years old, she was too young to travel alone without an adult's knowledge or supervision. Although she planned the journey carefully and managed her money well, careful planning cannot eliminate every risk. She could have discussed her wish with her mother and requested permission to travel with a trusted adult. Her adventure is an important learning experience, but it also highlights the need to balance independence with personal safety.

Q: How does the story show that real-life experiences can teach lessons that cannot be learnt through observation alone?

A: Before her journey, Valli learns about the bus and the town by watching the street and listening to other people. However, these observations cannot fully prepare her for the emotions she experiences during the trip. She feels excitement while travelling, amusement when watching the cow, and sadness when she later sees it dead. These experiences deepen her understanding of the world. The story demonstrates that direct experiences can teach important lessons about independence, uncertainty, and mortality that cannot be fully understood through observation alone.
