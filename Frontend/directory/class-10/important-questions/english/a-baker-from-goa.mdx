### Section A: Very Short Answer Questions (1-2 Marks)

Q: Who are the traditional bakers of Goa known as?

A: The traditional bakers of Goa are known as paders.

Q: What indicates that the traditional baking profession is still alive in Goa?

A: The existence of age-old furnaces, traditional baking methods, and bakers who continue their family profession indicates that the baking tradition is still alive in Goa.

Q: How did the baker announce his arrival in the village?

A: The baker announced his arrival by striking his bamboo staff against the ground, producing a distinctive musical sound.

Q: How many times did the baker usually visit the author's house in a day?

A: The baker usually visited the author's house twice a day, once in the morning while beginning his selling round and again after emptying his basket.

Q: Why did the children eagerly wait for the baker?

A: The children eagerly waited for the baker because they wanted to buy bread-bangles and sometimes special sweet bread.

Q: What was the traditional dress of the baker called?

A: The traditional dress of the baker was called the kabai, a single-piece, long frock reaching down to the knees.

Q: What were the two types of bread particularly associated with children and elders?

A: The elders generally received loaves of bread, while the children eagerly looked forward to bread-bangles.

Q: How did the baker collect his payments?

A: The baker collected his payments at the end of each month. The monthly accounts were recorded on the walls with a pencil.

Q: What does the baker's prosperous appearance suggest about his profession?

A: The baker's plump physique and prosperous appearance suggest that baking was a profitable profession and provided a comfortable livelihood for him and his family.

Q: What was the significance of the baker's furnace in a Goan village?

A: The baker's furnace was essential because bread and other baked products were required for daily consumption, marriages, engagements, Christmas and other festivals.

### Section B: Short Answer Questions (3 Marks)

Q: Why does the author feel nostalgic about the bakers of Goa?

A: The author feels nostalgic because the baker was an inseparable part of his childhood. The baker visited the house twice a day and was considered a friend, companion and guide. The children eagerly waited for his arrival, attracted by the sound of his bamboo staff and the aroma of freshly baked bread. These memories remind the author of Goa's traditional culture and simpler way of life.

Q: Describe the relationship between the baker and the children during the author's childhood.

A: The baker shared a special bond with the children of the village. His arrival was an exciting event for them, and they rushed to greet him when they heard the sound of his bamboo staff. Although the loaves were meant for the elders, the children eagerly waited for bread-bangles and sweet bread. They sometimes climbed benches or parapets to look into his basket. This shows the affection and excitement associated with the baker's visits.

Q: Explain the importance of bread in Goan culture.

A: Bread was an essential part of Goan culture and was associated with everyday life as well as important celebrations. Marriage gifts were considered incomplete without bol, a special sweet bread. Bread was also necessary for parties and feasts, while sandwiches were prepared for engagements. Cakes and bolinhas were essential during Christmas and other festivals. Thus, the baker played a significant role in preserving Goan customs and traditions.

Q: How did the baker's arrival create excitement among the children?

A: The baker's arrival created excitement because he announced himself with the rhythmic sound of his bamboo staff. Hearing this familiar sound, the children rushed to greet him. They were eager to receive their favourite bread-bangles and sweet bread. Even when the elders' loaves were being delivered to the household servant, the children tried to peep into the basket. The fragrance of freshly baked bread made the experience even more delightful.

Q: Describe the traditional dress of the baker and explain how it influenced popular expressions in Goa.

A: Traditionally, the baker wore a single-piece, long frock called the kabai, which reached down to his knees. During the author's childhood, bakers also wore shirts and trousers that were shorter than full-length trousers but longer than half-pants. This distinctive style of dressing became so closely associated with bakers that anyone wearing half-pants reaching just below the knees was compared to a pader. This shows how deeply the baker was embedded in Goan culture.

Q: Why was baking considered a profitable profession in Goa?

A: Baking was considered profitable because bread was in constant demand for daily consumption and special occasions. It was an essential part of marriages, engagements, parties, feasts and festivals. Bakers supplied these products regularly and collected payments at the end of each month. Their prosperous appearance and well-fed families served as evidence that the profession provided a comfortable livelihood.

Q: What does the description of the baker's daily routine reveal about traditional Goan life?

A: The baker's daily routine reveals the close relationship between traditional occupations and community life in Goa. His morning visits were familiar events, and his distinctive bamboo staff announced his arrival. He supplied bread to households and returned after completing his selling round. The monthly payment system and the continued use of traditional baking furnaces reflect the customs, social relationships and cultural continuity of Goan society.

Q: Why does the author mention the tiger's teeth and the use of hot tea?

A: The author uses humour to recall the carefree attitude of childhood. The children were so eager to eat the freshly delivered bread that they did not bother to brush their teeth or wash their mouths properly. They jokingly justified their behaviour by arguing that even a tiger did not brush its teeth and that hot tea could clean their mouths. This humorous incident highlights their innocence and excitement.

Q: Why is bread important in Goan culture?

A: Bread is central to Goan life because marriage gifts need bol, engagements need sandwiches, and Christmas and festivals need cakes and bolinhas, making the baker indispensable to both everyday meals and celebrations.
