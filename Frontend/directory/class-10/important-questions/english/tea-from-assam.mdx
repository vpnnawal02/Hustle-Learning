### Section A: Very Short Answer Questions (1-2 Marks)

Q: Who were Rajvir and Pranjol?

A: Rajvir and Pranjol were friends and classmates. Pranjol belonged to Assam, where his father managed a tea garden, while Rajvir was visiting Assam with him.

Q: Why was Rajvir excited during the train journey?

A: Rajvir was excited because he saw vast stretches of tea plantations for the first time. The beautiful scenery and the sight of tea gardens fascinated him.

Q: What did Rajvir tell Pranjol about tea consumption?

A: Rajvir told Pranjol that more than eighty crore cups of tea were drunk every day throughout the world.

Q: Which state has the largest concentration of tea plantations in the world?

A: Assam has the largest concentration of tea plantations in the world.

Q: What are the two legends about the discovery of tea mentioned by Rajvir?

A: The first legend describes how tea leaves accidentally fell into boiling water prepared by a Chinese emperor. The second is an Indian legend about Bodhidharma, whose eyelids supposedly gave rise to tea plants that could prevent sleep.

Q: According to the Chinese legend, how was tea discovered?

A: According to the legend, a few leaves from burning twigs accidentally fell into the boiling water prepared by a Chinese emperor. The leaves gave the water a delicious flavour, leading to the discovery of tea.

Q: Who was Bodhidharma?

A: Bodhidharma was an ancient Buddhist ascetic who, according to an Indian legend, cut off his eyelids because he felt sleepy during meditation. Ten tea plants supposedly grew from his eyelids.

Q: When was tea first drunk in China?

A: According to Rajvir, tea was first drunk in China as far back as 2700 B.C.

Q: When did tea reach Europe, and how was it initially used there?

A: Tea reached Europe in the sixteenth century and was initially consumed more as a medicine than as a beverage.

Q: What is the second-flush period in tea cultivation?

A: The second-flush period is the sprouting period from May to July when newly sprouted tea leaves are harvested. It is considered to yield the best tea.

Q: What did Rajvir observe at Dhekiabari Tea Estate?

A: Rajvir observed neatly pruned tea bushes, tea-pluckers collecting newly sprouted leaves, bamboo baskets carried on their backs and a tractor transporting freshly plucked tea leaves.

Q: Why was Mr Barua surprised by Rajvir's knowledge?

A: Mr Barua was surprised because Rajvir knew several details about tea cultivation, including the second-flush period and its importance in producing the best tea.

### Section B: Short Answer Questions (3 Marks)

Q: Describe the scenery that Rajvir observed during the train journey through Assam.

A: Rajvir was fascinated by the lush green scenery of Assam. The soft green paddy fields gradually gave way to vast stretches of tea bushes. Against the background of densely wooded hills, tea plantations extended as far as the eye could see. Tall shade trees stood above the tea plants, while workers moved between the orderly rows of bushes. The beautiful landscape filled Rajvir with excitement.

Q: Why was Pranjol less excited than Rajvir on seeing the tea gardens?

A: Pranjol had been born and brought up on a tea plantation, so tea gardens were a familiar part of his everyday life. For him, the sight of tea plantations was ordinary. Rajvir, however, was seeing such extensive tea gardens for the first time and was fascinated by their beauty. Their different backgrounds explain their contrasting reactions.

Q: Explain the two legends about the discovery of tea.

A: The first legend is associated with a Chinese emperor who boiled water before drinking it. Some leaves from burning twigs accidentally fell into the water and gave it a delicious flavour, leading to the discovery of tea. The second is an Indian legend about Bodhidharma, a Buddhist ascetic who cut off his eyelids to avoid sleeping during meditation. Ten tea plants supposedly grew from his eyelids, and their leaves were believed to prevent sleep when consumed in hot water.

Q: What historical information about tea did Rajvir share with Pranjol?

A: Rajvir explained that tea was first drunk in China as far back as 2700 B.C. He also mentioned that words such as tea, chai and chini originated from Chinese. Tea reached Europe in the sixteenth century, where it was initially used more as a medicine than as a beverage. His knowledge demonstrated his interest in the history and development of tea.

Q: Describe the activities of the workers at Dhekiabari Tea Estate.

A: At Dhekiabari Tea Estate, groups of tea-pluckers were collecting newly sprouted tea leaves from neatly pruned bushes. They carried bamboo baskets on their backs and wore plastic aprons while working. The tea bushes were maintained at a uniform height, creating orderly rows across the estate. A tractor pulling a trailer loaded with freshly plucked tea leaves was also seen transporting the harvest.

Q: How did Rajvir demonstrate that he had researched tea before visiting Assam?

A: Rajvir demonstrated his preparation by sharing information about tea consumption, its historical origins and the legends associated with its discovery. He knew that tea had been consumed in China since 2700 B.C. and had reached Europe in the sixteenth century. He also correctly identified the second-flush period at the tea estate and explained that it produced the best tea. His knowledge impressed Mr Barua.

Q: Why did Rajvir want to visit Assam's tea gardens?

A: Rajvir wanted to visit Assam because he was deeply interested in learning about tea. He had already read extensively about its history, legends and cultivation, but he wanted to gain practical knowledge by observing the plantations. His visit to Dhekiabari Tea Estate offered him an opportunity to understand tea production directly and learn more from people involved in the industry.

Q: What is the significance of the second-flush period in tea cultivation?

A: The second-flush period extends from May to July and is characterised by the sprouting of new tea leaves. According to Rajvir, this period yields the best tea. It is therefore an important phase in the tea cultivation cycle because the quality of the harvested leaves contributes to the quality of the final product.

Q: How does the chapter highlight Assam's importance in the tea industry?

A: The chapter highlights Assam's importance by describing its extensive tea plantations, which form a major part of its landscape. Pranjol explains that Assam has the largest concentration of tea gardens in the world. The visit to Dhekiabari Tea Estate further demonstrates the scale of tea cultivation, with neatly maintained bushes, numerous tea-pluckers and tractors transporting harvested leaves. These details establish Assam as a significant tea-producing region.
