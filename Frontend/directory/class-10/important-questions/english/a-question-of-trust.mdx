### Section A: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

Q: Who was Horace Danby? Why was he considered an unusual thief?

A: Horace Danby was a fifty-year-old man who ran a successful business of making locks and employed two assistants. He appeared to be a respectable citizen but secretly robbed a safe every year. Unlike ordinary thieves, he did not steal to fulfil his basic needs or live a luxurious life. He stole money to purchase rare and expensive books, which he was passionate about collecting.

Q: What was Horace Danby's passion? How did he fulfil it?

A: Horace Danby was passionate about collecting rare and expensive books. Since these books were costly, he robbed a safe every year and stole enough money to support his hobby for twelve months. He secretly purchased the books he wanted through an agent.

Q: Why did Horace Danby plan to rob Shotover Grange?

A: Horace planned to rob Shotover Grange because he knew that its safe contained jewellery worth approximately fifteen thousand pounds. He expected to sell the stolen jewellery for at least five thousand pounds, which would allow him to purchase three rare books that were going to be sold in the autumn.

Q: How did Horace prepare for the robbery at Shotover Grange?

A: Horace carefully studied Shotover Grange for two weeks before attempting the robbery. He examined its rooms, electric wiring, paths and garden. He also knew the location of the safe because a magazine article had described the house and mentioned that a painting concealed the safe. He chose a time when the servants were away.

Q: How did Horace manage to enter Shotover Grange?

A: Horace waited until the two servants had left for the cinema. He had previously noticed that the kitchen door key was kept on a hook outside the house. Wearing gloves, he took the key, opened the door and entered the house without attracting attention.

Q: How did Horace deal with the dog inside the house?

A: Horace encountered a small dog named Sherry in the kitchen. He addressed the dog by its name and behaved affectionately towards it. He believed that calling a dog by its correct name and showing it love was enough to keep it quiet.

Q: Why was Horace Danby sneezing while opening the safe?

A: Horace suffered from hay fever, an allergy that affected his nose and throat. The flowers kept in the drawing room irritated him and caused him to sneeze repeatedly. His sneezing eventually attracted the attention of the young woman who entered the room.

Q: How did Horace know about the location of the safe?

A: Horace had read a magazine article about Shotover Grange. The article included a plan of the house, a picture of the drawing room and information that a painting concealed the safe. This information helped him locate the safe easily.

Q: Who interrupted Horace during the robbery? How did she react?

A: A young, attractive woman dressed in red interrupted Horace while he was trying to open the safe. She remained calm and confident instead of panicking. She addressed him directly as a burglar and threatened to inform the police, making him believe that she was the owner of the house.

Q: Why did Horace believe that the young woman was the lady of the house?

A: The young woman behaved confidently and moved around the house as if she owned it. She spoke affectionately to the dog, adjusted the ornaments near the fireplace and showed no fear of Horace. Her convincing behaviour and knowledge of the house made him believe that she was its owner.

Q: How did the young woman manipulate Horace into opening the safe?

A: The young woman threatened to call the police but later offered to let Horace go if he helped her. She claimed that she had forgotten the safe's combination and needed her jewellery for a party that evening. Believing her story, Horace agreed to open the safe for her.

Q: Why did Horace remove his gloves while opening the safe?

A: Horace removed his gloves to give the young woman his cigarette lighter when she took a cigarette from a silver box. He was eager to please her because he believed she might help him escape punishment. Consequently, he left his fingerprints throughout the room, providing evidence that later led to his arrest.

Q: Why did Horace agree to open the safe for the young woman?

A: Horace believed that the woman was the owner of the house and that she would allow him to escape if he helped her. She claimed to have forgotten the combination of the safe and needed her jewellery for a party. Since Horace was afraid of going to prison, he eagerly accepted her offer and opened the safe.

Q: Why was Horace arrested even though he believed he had escaped successfully?

A: Horace was arrested because his fingerprints were found throughout the room after he removed his gloves. The police identified him as the person responsible for the jewellery theft at Shotover Grange. His explanation that the lady of the house had asked him to open the safe was rejected because the real owner denied his story.

Q: What happened to Horace Danby after his arrest?

A: Horace was imprisoned for the robbery at Shotover Grange. He eventually became an assistant librarian in the prison. He often remembered the clever young woman who had deceived him and felt angry whenever anyone mentioned the saying honour among thieves.

Q: Why did Horace become angry whenever someone mentioned honour among thieves?

A: Horace believed that the young woman had betrayed him by deceiving him and stealing the jewellery. Although he was himself a thief, he had trusted her because he believed she was the owner of the house. Her successful deception destroyed his faith in the idea that thieves could be trusted to behave honourably towards one another.

Q: Why can Horace Danby not be categorised as a typical thief?

A: Horace was different from a typical thief because he did not steal to earn his livelihood or satisfy a desire for wealth and luxury. He operated a successful lock-making business and appeared respectable. His annual thefts were motivated by his passion for rare and expensive books. However, his unusual motive did not make his criminal actions acceptable.

Q: What mistake did Horace make despite being a meticulous planner?

A: Horace's biggest mistake was trusting the young woman without verifying her identity. He believed her claim that she owned the house and needed help opening the safe. He also removed his gloves, leaving his fingerprints behind. His overconfidence and poor judgement ultimately resulted in his arrest.

Q: Why did Horace not suspect that the young woman was an impostor?

A: The woman was confident, persuasive and familiar with the house. She behaved naturally around the dog and the ornaments, making her appear to be the owner. Horace was also preoccupied with avoiding arrest and believed that helping her would secure his freedom. His overconfidence prevented him from questioning her story.

Q: Who was the real culprit in the story?

A: The real culprit was the clever young woman who pretended to be the lady of Shotover Grange. She deceived Horace into opening the safe and handing over the jewellery. The actual owner was a sixty-year-old, grey-haired woman who later denied knowing anything about Horace's explanation.

### Section B: Long Answer Questions (5-6 Marks)

Q: Write a character sketch of Horace Danby.

A: Horace Danby is the central character of Victor Canning's story A Question of Trust. He is a fifty-year-old unmarried man who runs a successful business of making locks and employs two assistants. Outwardly, he appears to be an honest and respectable citizen, but he secretly commits thefts. Horace is intelligent, methodical and careful. He plans his robberies in advance, studies the targeted houses and takes precautions to avoid leaving fingerprints. However, his passion for rare and expensive books motivates him to commit a crime every year. Unlike a conventional thief, he does not steal to become wealthy but to finance his unusual hobby. Despite his intelligence, Horace is overconfident and gullible. He believes the young woman's false story and opens the safe for her. By removing his gloves, he leaves fingerprints that lead to his arrest. His failure demonstrates that intelligence and careful planning are insufficient when a person lacks sound judgement. Horace's character illustrates the irony that even a clever criminal can become a victim of deception. His story also conveys that good intentions or personal passions cannot justify dishonest actions.

Q: Describe how the young woman deceived Horace Danby.

A: The young woman deceived Horace through her confidence, intelligence and convincing performance. When she entered the drawing room, she behaved as if she were the owner of Shotover Grange. She spoke to the dog by its name and adjusted the ornaments near the fireplace, creating the impression that she belonged there. Instead of appearing frightened by the burglar, she calmly threatened to call the police. She then pretended to sympathise with him and suggested that she might allow him to escape. After recognising his fear of imprisonment, she manipulated him further by claiming that she needed her jewellery for a party but had forgotten the combination of the safe. Horace believed her story and offered to open the safe. He even removed his gloves to give her his cigarette lighter, leaving his fingerprints behind. Once he had handed over the jewellery, she allowed him to leave without revealing her deception. Later, Horace discovered the truth when he was arrested. The actual owner denied his story, exposing the young woman's trick. Her actions demonstrate that appearances can be deceptive and that criminals may also become victims of other criminals.

Q: Horace Danby was good and respectable but not completely honest. Explain.

A: The statement accurately describes the contradictory nature of Horace Danby's character. He was a successful businessman who manufactured locks and employed two assistants. He lived an apparently respectable life and was generally regarded as a good citizen. However, Horace had a dishonest habit. Every year, he carefully planned and carried out a robbery to obtain money for purchasing rare and expensive books. He believed that stealing from wealthy people was acceptable because he used the money to satisfy his passion rather than to support a life of luxury. His conduct reveals that respectability in public does not necessarily indicate honesty in private life. Although Horace possessed professional skills and planned his crimes carefully, he repeatedly violated the law. His eventual arrest reinforces the idea that a person's motives do not erase the wrongdoing involved in criminal acts. Therefore, Horace could be considered respectable in certain aspects of his life, but he could not truly be called an honest person.

Q: A Question of Trust is an appropriate title for the story. Justify.

A: The title A Question of Trust is appropriate because trust is the central issue around which the story develops. Horace Danby, a professional thief, believes that the young woman he meets at Shotover Grange is the owner of the house. He trusts her explanation that she needs the jewellery for a party and has forgotten the combination of the safe. The woman exploits this trust to achieve her own criminal objective. She persuades Horace to open the safe and hand over the jewellery, while concealing her real identity. Horace's misplaced trust eventually leads to his arrest because he removes his gloves and leaves fingerprints behind. The story also questions whether trust and honour can exist among criminals. Horace expects the woman to help him escape, but she betrays him. The irony lies in the fact that a thief who deceives others is himself deceived. Thus, the title highlights the dangers of misplaced trust and the irony of expecting honesty from someone who is participating in a dishonest act.

Q: Explain the irony in the story A Question of Trust.

A: The story is built around situational irony, in which events turn out contrary to expectations. Horace Danby is an experienced thief who carefully plans his robberies and takes precautions to avoid detection. He believes that he can successfully rob Shotover Grange and escape without consequences. However, he is deceived by a young woman who pretends to be the owner of the house. Instead of stealing the jewellery himself, he opens the safe and hands it over to her. His attempt to secure his freedom results in his arrest because he leaves fingerprints behind. The greatest irony is that Horace, a criminal who regularly deceives others, becomes the victim of another criminal's deception. He also expects honesty from the woman while engaging in an illegal activity himself. The story therefore exposes the contradiction in Horace's belief that thieves can trust one another and demonstrates that dishonesty often leads to unexpected consequences.

Q: How does the story prove that appearances can be deceptive?

A: The story demonstrates that appearances can be deceptive through the characters of Horace Danby and the young woman. Horace appears to be a respectable citizen and a successful businessman, but he secretly commits thefts every year. His outward respectability hides his criminal activities. Similarly, the young woman appears to be the owner of Shotover Grange. She behaves confidently, speaks to the dog by its name and moves around the house naturally. Her convincing performance leads Horace to believe her false identity. However, the woman is actually a thief who tricks Horace into opening the safe and giving her the jewellery. The real owner is an older woman who later denies Horace's explanation. Through these characters, the story shows that outward appearances do not always reveal a person's true nature. It warns readers against making assumptions without examining the facts.

Q: Horace Danby was a meticulous planner, yet he failed. Explain the reasons for his failure.

A: Horace Danby was an experienced thief who carefully planned his robberies. Before entering Shotover Grange, he spent two weeks studying the property, examining its rooms and surroundings, and identifying the location of the safe. He also chose a time when the servants were away. Despite these precautions, he failed for several reasons. First, he underestimated the possibility of an unexpected interruption. His hay fever caused him to sneeze repeatedly, attracting the attention of the young woman. Second, he trusted the woman without verifying her identity. Her confident behaviour convinced him that she was the owner of the house. Third, he allowed his fear of imprisonment and desire to escape to influence his judgement. He agreed to open the safe for the woman, believing that she would help him. Finally, he removed his gloves and left his fingerprints behind. These mistakes reveal that careful planning cannot guarantee success when a person becomes careless, overconfident or emotionally manipulated.

Q: What message does Victor Canning convey through the story?

A: Victor Canning conveys several important messages through A Question of Trust. The story highlights the consequences of dishonesty and the danger of misplaced trust. Horace Danby believes that his thefts are justified because he commits them to purchase rare books rather than to satisfy greed. However, the story shows that personal motives cannot justify breaking the law. The young woman's deception also demonstrates that appearances can be misleading and that people should not trust others blindly. Horace's overconfidence prevents him from recognising the danger he faces. Furthermore, the story challenges the belief that there is honour among thieves. Both Horace and the young woman engage in criminal activities, but the woman successfully deceives Horace. Ultimately, the story teaches that dishonesty, regardless of the motive, can lead to serious consequences and that intelligence without sound judgement can result in failure.

Q: Do you think Horace Danby deserved to be punished? Give reasons for your answer.

A: Yes, Horace Danby deserved to be punished because he deliberately committed theft despite being a successful businessman. His passion for rare books may explain his motivation, but it does not justify his actions. He carefully planned his robberies and knowingly violated the law. Although he was deceived by the young woman, his own dishonesty placed him in that situation. He trusted her because he believed she could help him escape punishment, not because he had verified her identity. His arrest demonstrates that criminal actions carry consequences even when the person committing them has an unusual or seemingly harmless motive. However, the young woman was also guilty of theft and deception. She should not escape responsibility merely because she outsmarted Horace. Therefore, Horace's punishment is justified, but the same legal and moral standards should apply to the woman as well.

Q: Do intentions justify actions? Explain with reference to Horace Danby.

A: Intentions do not automatically justify actions. Horace Danby believed that his thefts were acceptable because he used the money to purchase rare and expensive books rather than to fulfil ordinary material desires. However, his passion did not give him the right to steal from others. Although his ultimate objective was personal enjoyment through book collecting, his methods involved breaking the law and violating other people's property rights. The story demonstrates that even seemingly harmless goals cannot justify dishonest or illegal means. Horace's eventual arrest highlights the consequences of his decisions. His experience teaches us that people must pursue their interests through ethical and lawful methods. Good intentions may explain a person's behaviour, but they do not necessarily make that behaviour right.

Q: Compare Horace Danby and the young woman.

A: Horace Danby and the young woman are both intelligent and dishonest characters, but they differ in their methods and judgement. Horace is an experienced thief who carefully plans his robberies. He operates a successful lock-making business and steals once a year to purchase rare books. Although he is cautious and methodical, he becomes overconfident and trusts the young woman without checking her identity. The young woman is equally clever but more successful in manipulating others. She disguises her real intentions by pretending to be the owner of Shotover Grange. She understands Horace's fear of imprisonment and uses it to persuade him to open the safe. While Horace leaves fingerprints and is arrested, the young woman escapes with the jewellery. Their contrasting outcomes reveal that intelligence alone does not guarantee success. The story also exposes the irony of a thief becoming a victim of another thief.

Q: How does Horace's passion for books become the cause of his downfall?

A: Horace Danby's passion for rare and expensive books motivates him to commit theft every year. Although he earns a living through his lock-making business, he is unwilling to give up his criminal habit because he wants to purchase costly books. His desire to obtain money for three books motivates him to target Shotover Grange. He plans the robbery carefully but loses his judgement when the young woman interrupts him. Instead of leaving immediately, he trusts her claim that she owns the house and needs help opening the safe. He opens it and hands over the jewellery, believing that she will allow him to escape. His desire to avoid prison and continue pursuing his hobby makes him vulnerable to deception. Ultimately, his fingerprints lead to his arrest. Thus, his passion for books becomes indirectly responsible for his downfall because it motivates the crime that exposes his dishonesty and poor judgement.

### Section C: Competency-Based and Extract-Based Questions

Q: Why is Horace's belief that he steals for a good reason questionable?

A: Horace believes that his thefts are acceptable because he uses the money to purchase rare books rather than to satisfy greed. However, his reasoning is flawed because his personal passion does not justify violating the law or taking other people's property. His belief reflects his attempt to rationalise his dishonesty. The story demonstrates that a seemingly harmless motive does not make an unlawful action morally acceptable.

Q: What does the young woman's behaviour reveal about her personality?

A: The young woman's behaviour reveals that she is confident, observant, manipulative and quick-thinking. She remains calm when she encounters Horace and immediately understands how to exploit his fear of imprisonment. By pretending to be the owner of the house, she creates a convincing false identity. Her ability to deceive an experienced thief demonstrates her intelligence, although she uses it for dishonest purposes.

Q: Why was removing his gloves a crucial mistake for Horace?

A: Horace had carefully worn gloves to avoid leaving fingerprints during the robbery. However, when the young woman took a cigarette, he removed his gloves to give her his cigarette lighter. This careless act left his fingerprints in the room. The fingerprints provided evidence connecting him to the robbery and eventually led to his arrest. The incident highlights how a single lapse in judgement can undo extensive planning.

Q: What does the ending reveal about the relationship between intelligence and morality?

A: The ending reveals that intelligence does not necessarily indicate moral character. Both Horace and the young woman are clever enough to plan and execute thefts. However, they use their intelligence for dishonest purposes. The young woman's success does not make her actions right, just as Horace's passion for books does not justify his crimes. The story suggests that intelligence must be accompanied by sound judgement and ethical behaviour.

Q: Why is the saying honour among thieves relevant to the story?

A: The saying suggests that even criminals may follow an informal code of loyalty towards one another. Horace expects the young woman to help him because he believes she is willing to cooperate with him. However, she deceives him and escapes with the jewellery. His arrest exposes the weakness of his assumption and explains why he becomes angry whenever the saying is mentioned.

Q: What would have happened if Horace had not trusted the young woman?

A: If Horace had questioned the woman's identity or refused to open the safe for her, he might have avoided leaving the fingerprints that led to his arrest. However, he was already attempting to commit a robbery, so he could still have faced legal consequences. The incident demonstrates that caution might have prevented his immediate downfall, but it would not have made his original criminal intention acceptable.

### Section D: Important Value-Based and Higher-Order Thinking Questions

Q: Is Horace Danby a victim or a criminal? Justify your answer.

A: Horace Danby is primarily a criminal who becomes a victim of another criminal's deception. He deliberately plans to rob Shotover Grange and enters the house with the intention of stealing jewellery. Therefore, he cannot be considered innocent. However, the young woman's manipulation makes him a victim of deception. She pretends to be the owner and tricks him into opening the safe. Although her actions expose his poor judgement, they do not erase his original intention to commit theft. Thus, Horace occupies both roles: he is a criminal because of his own actions and a victim because another thief deceives him.

Q: How does the story challenge the assumption that criminals can always recognise one another?

A: The story challenges this assumption by showing that Horace, despite his experience as a thief, cannot recognise the young woman's dishonest intentions. He assumes that her confident behaviour indicates that she is the owner of the house. His overconfidence prevents him from considering that she might be a criminal like himself. The young woman successfully exploits his assumptions and escapes with the jewellery. This shows that experience in wrongdoing does not necessarily make a person better at judging others. Criminals can also be deceived, particularly when their own desires and fears influence their decisions.

Q: What role does overconfidence play in Horace's downfall?

A: Overconfidence plays a major role in Horace's downfall. Having successfully carried out robberies in previous years, he believes that he can rob Shotover Grange without difficulty. His familiarity with locks and safes makes him confident in his ability to complete the task. However, he fails to anticipate the young woman's deception. Instead of questioning her identity, he believes her story and agrees to open the safe. He also removes his gloves, leaving fingerprints behind. His confidence in his own intelligence prevents him from recognising the risks of the situation. The story demonstrates that overconfidence can weaken judgement and turn careful planning into failure.

Q: What is the significance of the contrast between Horace's professional expertise and his personal judgement?

A: Horace's professional expertise lies in making locks and understanding how safes work. He uses this knowledge to plan robberies carefully and overcome security measures. However, his technical competence does not extend to evaluating the young woman's claims. He is able to understand the mechanics of the safe but fails to understand the situation in which he finds himself. His fear of imprisonment and desire to escape make him vulnerable to manipulation. This contrast demonstrates that technical skill alone is insufficient for success. Good judgement, critical thinking and the ability to question assumptions are equally important.

Q: What lesson can students learn from Horace Danby's experience?

A: Students can learn that dishonesty has consequences, regardless of the reasons used to justify it. Horace believes that stealing is acceptable because he uses the money to purchase books. However, his actions eventually lead to imprisonment. His experience also teaches the importance of critical thinking and avoiding misplaced trust. He accepts the young woman's claims without verifying her identity and suffers because of his poor judgement. The story further demonstrates that intelligence and planning cannot compensate for unethical decisions. Students should pursue their goals through honest means and recognise that personal desires do not justify harming others.
