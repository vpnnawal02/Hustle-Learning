### Section A: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

Q: Who was Matilda Loisel? Why was she always unhappy?

A: Matilda Loisel was a beautiful young woman who belonged to a family of clerks and was married to a simple clerk in the office of the Board of Education. She was always unhappy because she believed that she deserved a life of luxury, wealth and social status. She disliked her modest home, ordinary clothes and lack of jewellery. Her dissatisfaction with her circumstances prevented her from appreciating what she had.

Q: Why was Matilda dissatisfied with her life despite having a caring husband?

A: Matilda was dissatisfied because she constantly dreamed of wealth, luxury and a high social position. She desired elegant dresses, expensive jewellery, grand dinners and beautiful surroundings. Her husband, however, was content with their modest life and tried to make her happy. Matilda's unrealistic expectations and constant comparison with wealthy people made her unable to appreciate her husband's affection and sacrifices.

Q: How did Matilda's husband try to make her happy?

A: Matilda's husband tried to make her happy by bringing home an invitation to a grand ball at the residence of the Minister of Public Instruction. When she complained that she had no suitable dress, he offered her four hundred francs, which he had saved to buy a gun. Later, he suggested that she borrow jewellery from her wealthy friend, Madame Forestier.

Q: Why did Matilda refuse to attend the ball initially?

A: Matilda initially refused to attend the ball because she did not have an elegant dress suitable for the occasion. She feared that she would appear poor and inferior among wealthy women. Although her husband had obtained an invitation to an important social event, she could not enjoy the opportunity because she was preoccupied with her appearance and social status.

Q: How did Matilda's husband arrange money for her dress?

A: When Matilda complained that she had no suitable dress, her husband asked how much it would cost. She estimated that four hundred francs would be sufficient. Although he had saved that money to buy a gun for the following summer's hunting parties, he willingly gave it to her so that she could purchase a dress for the ball.

Q: Why was Matilda still unhappy after getting a new dress?

A: Even after obtaining a new dress, Matilda remained unhappy because she had no jewellery to complement it. She feared that she would look poor among the wealthy women attending the ball. Her desire to appear rich and elegant made her feel that a beautiful dress alone was insufficient.

Q: How did Matilda solve the problem of not having jewellery?

A: Matilda's husband suggested that she borrow jewellery from her wealthy friend, Madame Forestier. Matilda visited her friend and was allowed to choose from a collection of ornaments. She selected a beautiful diamond necklace that perfectly complemented her dress and made her feel satisfied with her appearance.

Q: How did Matilda behave at the ball?

A: Matilda was extremely successful at the ball. She looked beautiful, elegant and graceful, attracting the attention of the men present. Many people admired her and wanted to be introduced to her. She danced enthusiastically and enjoyed the admiration she had always desired, forgetting her ordinary life for a few hours.

Q: What happened when Matilda returned home after the ball?

A: When Matilda returned home after the ball, she removed her wraps and looked at herself in the mirror for one final moment. Suddenly, she discovered that the diamond necklace was missing from her neck. She and her husband searched the folds of her dress, cloak and pockets but could not find it.

Q: What steps did the Loisels take to find the missing necklace?

A: Matilda's husband retraced the route they had walked after leaving the ball, hoping to find the necklace. He also contacted the police and the cab offices and placed an advertisement in newspapers offering a reward. Despite these efforts, the necklace could not be found. Eventually, the couple decided to purchase a replacement.

Q: How did the Loisels arrange money to buy the replacement necklace?

A: The Loisels found a necklace that appeared identical to the lost one and cost thirty-six thousand francs. Matilda's husband used the eighteen thousand francs inherited from his father and borrowed the remaining amount from moneylenders. He accepted harsh financial terms and made ruinous promises to obtain the money.

Q: Why did the Loisels not tell Madame Forestier about the loss?

A: The Loisels feared the consequences of admitting that they had lost the necklace. They were worried that Madame Forestier might suspect them of theft or demand compensation. To avoid revealing the truth, they decided to replace the necklace with an identical-looking one and return it without explaining what had happened.

Q: How did the loss of the necklace change the lives of the Loisels?

A: The loss of the necklace forced the Loisels to borrow a large amount of money to purchase a replacement. They dismissed their maid, moved into a small attic room and adopted an extremely difficult lifestyle. Matilda performed exhausting household chores, while her husband worked extra hours and took on additional work. They spent ten years repaying their debt.

Q: How did Matilda's appearance change after ten years of hardship?

A: After ten years of hard work and poverty, Matilda became a strong, rough and prematurely aged woman. Her hair was poorly arranged, her clothes were untidy and her hands became rough from household work. She no longer resembled the delicate and beautiful woman who had once attracted admiration at the ball.

Q: What did Madame Forestier reveal to Matilda at the end of the story?

A: When Matilda finally confessed that she had lost the original necklace and spent ten years repaying the cost of its replacement, Madame Forestier was shocked. She revealed that her original necklace was imitation jewellery worth no more than five hundred francs. The revelation exposed the unnecessary suffering endured by the Loisels.

Q: Why is the ending of The Necklace ironic?

A: The ending is ironic because Matilda and her husband spend ten years living in poverty and repaying a debt of thirty-six thousand francs to replace a necklace. At the end, they discover that the original necklace was imitation jewellery worth only five hundred francs. Their suffering could have been avoided if Matilda had told her friend the truth immediately.

Q: What role does Madame Forestier play in the story?

A: Madame Forestier is Matilda's wealthy friend who lends her the necklace for the ball. Her generosity gives Matilda the opportunity to experience the luxurious lifestyle she desires. However, the necklace's loss changes the course of Matilda's life. Madame Forestier's final revelation that the necklace was imitation jewellery provides the story with its unexpected ending.

Q: What does the story teach us about honesty?

A: The story teaches that honesty is essential, especially when mistakes occur. Instead of admitting that she had lost the necklace, Matilda and her husband chose to replace it secretly. Their fear of telling the truth resulted in ten years of unnecessary hardship. The story demonstrates that honesty could have prevented their suffering and that concealing the truth may create much greater problems.

### Section B: Long Answer Questions (5-6 Marks)

Q: Write a character sketch of Matilda Loisel.

A: Matilda Loisel is the central character of Guy de Maupassant's story The Necklace. She is a beautiful woman who belongs to a modest family and is married to a clerk. However, she is dissatisfied with her ordinary life and constantly dreams of wealth, luxury and social recognition. Matilda is materialistic and vain. She believes that she deserves expensive jewellery, elegant dresses and a luxurious lifestyle. Her desire to appear wealthy prevents her from appreciating her husband's affection and sacrifices. Even after receiving an invitation to a grand ball, she becomes upset because she lacks suitable clothes and jewellery. At the ball, Matilda enjoys the admiration of others and feels that she has finally achieved the social recognition she desires. However, losing the necklace changes her life dramatically. Instead of confessing the truth, she spends ten years working hard to repay the cost of its replacement. Her experience demonstrates how vanity, dissatisfaction and poor judgement can lead to suffering. Matilda's story teaches the importance of contentment, honesty and sensible decision-making.

Q: Describe the role of Matilda's husband in the story.

A: Matilda's husband is a simple, caring and hardworking clerk who tries to keep his wife happy despite their modest financial circumstances. Unlike Matilda, he is satisfied with ordinary pleasures and does not constantly dream of wealth or luxury. When he receives an invitation to the Minister's ball, he is delighted because he believes it will make his wife happy. When Matilda complains about her clothes, he sacrifices the money he had saved to buy a gun and gives her four hundred francs for a dress. He also suggests that she borrow jewellery from Madame Forestier. After the necklace disappears, he makes every possible effort to find it. When these attempts fail, he takes responsibility for replacing it and borrows a substantial amount of money. He works evenings and nights to repay the debt. His character demonstrates generosity, patience, responsibility and sacrifice. However, his decision to conceal the loss rather than insist on telling Madame Forestier the truth contributes to the couple's prolonged suffering.

Q: How did the necklace change the course of the Loisels' lives?

A: The necklace became the turning point in the lives of Matilda and her husband. Before the ball, they lived a modest but manageable life. Matilda was dissatisfied with their circumstances, but her husband worked steadily and provided for their needs. After borrowing a diamond necklace from Madame Forestier, Matilda attended the ball and enjoyed the admiration of others. However, when she discovered that the necklace was missing, the couple's lives changed dramatically. Unable to find it, they purchased a replacement for thirty-six thousand francs. Matilda's husband borrowed a large sum of money, and the couple had to adopt an extremely difficult lifestyle to repay the debt. They dismissed their maid, moved into a small attic room and worked hard for ten years. Finally, they repaid the debt, but Matilda had become prematurely aged and physically exhausted. The final revelation that the original necklace was worth only five hundred francs made their suffering even more tragic. Thus, the necklace transformed their lives from modest comfort into prolonged hardship.

Q: Matilda's vanity and dissatisfaction were responsible for her downfall. Justify the statement.

A: Matilda's vanity and dissatisfaction played a major role in her downfall. Although she had a caring husband and a modest home, she constantly dreamed of wealth, luxury and social recognition. She believed that her beauty entitled her to a more glamorous life. When her husband brought home an invitation to the ball, she became upset because she lacked an elegant dress. After receiving money for a new dress, she demanded jewellery because she feared appearing poor among wealthy women. At the ball, she enjoyed the admiration she had always desired. However, after losing the borrowed necklace, she and her husband decided to replace it without telling Madame Forestier the truth. Their decision forced them into ten years of poverty and exhausting work. The revelation that the original necklace was imitation jewellery showed how unnecessary their suffering had been. Therefore, Matilda's vanity, dissatisfaction and reluctance to be honest contributed significantly to her downfall.

Q: What would have happened if Matilda had confessed to Madame Forestier that she had lost the necklace?

A: If Matilda had confessed to Madame Forestier immediately, the consequences might have been very different. Madame Forestier could have explained the actual value of the necklace, which was only five hundred francs. Matilda and her husband might then have replaced it at a much lower cost or compensated her for its actual value. They would probably have avoided borrowing thirty-six thousand francs and spending ten years in poverty. Matilda would not have been forced to perform exhausting household chores, and her husband would not have needed to work extra hours and nights to repay the debt. The story demonstrates that Matilda's fear of admitting her mistake made the situation much worse. An honest conversation could have prevented years of unnecessary suffering.

Q: Explain the significance of the title The Necklace.

A: The title The Necklace is appropriate because the necklace is the central object around which the entire story revolves. Matilda borrows it from Madame Forestier to appear wealthy and elegant at the ball. The necklace initially represents beauty, luxury and social status in Matilda's eyes. However, its disappearance transforms it into the cause of financial hardship and suffering. The Loisels spend ten years repaying the debt incurred to replace it. The final revelation that the original necklace was imitation jewellery creates the story's central irony. The object that Matilda considered essential to her appearance was not genuinely valuable, yet its loss cost the couple ten years of their lives. Thus, the necklace symbolises vanity, materialism, deception and the unforeseen consequences of seemingly small events.

Q: What is the central theme of The Necklace?

A: The central theme of The Necklace is the danger of vanity, materialism and dissatisfaction with one's circumstances. Matilda's constant desire for wealth and social recognition prevents her from appreciating her modest but stable life. Her obsession with appearing rich leads her to borrow a necklace for a grand ball. When she loses it, she and her husband conceal the truth and spend ten years repaying the cost of its replacement. The story also highlights the importance of honesty. If Matilda had admitted her mistake to Madame Forestier, the couple might have avoided years of hardship. The unexpected ending reinforces the idea that appearances can be deceptive. What seemed to be an expensive diamond necklace was actually imitation jewellery. Ultimately, the story teaches readers to value honesty, contentment and sensible decision-making over material possessions and social approval.

Q: Compare Matilda's life before and after the loss of the necklace.

A: Before losing the necklace, Matilda lived a modest life with her husband. Although she was dissatisfied with their circumstances, she had a home, financial stability and a caring husband who tried to make her happy. She dreamed of wealth and luxury but had not yet experienced the hardships that followed. After losing the necklace, her life changed completely. The couple borrowed a large sum of money to purchase a replacement and moved into a small attic room. Matilda performed exhausting household chores, carried water, washed clothes and bargained for groceries. Her husband worked evenings and nights to repay their debt. After ten years, Matilda had become rough, tired and prematurely aged. Although the couple eventually repaid the entire debt, they discovered that the original necklace was worth only five hundred francs. The contrast highlights the devastating consequences of their decision to conceal the truth.

Q: How does Guy de Maupassant use irony in the story?

A: Guy de Maupassant uses situational irony to create the story's unexpected and powerful ending. Matilda believes that wearing an expensive diamond necklace will help her appear wealthy and gain social recognition. However, the necklace becomes the cause of ten years of hardship. After losing it, Matilda and her husband purchase a replacement for thirty-six thousand francs. They sacrifice their comfort, work relentlessly and spend a decade repaying the debt. At the end of the story, Madame Forestier reveals that the original necklace was imitation jewellery worth no more than five hundred francs. This revelation shows that the couple's suffering was unnecessary. The irony lies in the enormous difference between the necklace's perceived value and its actual worth. It reinforces the themes of vanity, appearances and the consequences of dishonesty.

Q: We should be content with what life gives us. Explain with reference to the story.

A: The story The Necklace illustrates the importance of contentment through Matilda Loisel's experience. Although she has a home and a caring husband, she is constantly unhappy because she dreams of wealth, luxury and social recognition. Her dissatisfaction leads her to borrow a necklace so that she can appear wealthy at a grand ball. When she loses it, she and her husband conceal the truth and purchase an expensive replacement. Their decision forces them to live in poverty and work hard for ten years. At the end, they discover that the original necklace was imitation jewellery worth only five hundred francs. Matilda's suffering demonstrates how dissatisfaction and the desire to impress others can destroy peace and happiness. The story teaches that people should appreciate what they have rather than sacrifice their well-being to maintain appearances.

### Section C: Higher-Order Thinking Questions

Q: Was Matilda a victim of circumstances or of her own choices? Explain.

A: Matilda was affected by her modest financial circumstances, but her own choices played a major role in her suffering. She was unhappy because she desired a luxurious lifestyle that her family could not afford. However, her husband tried to make her happy by sacrificing his savings for her dress. Matilda then borrowed a necklace because she wanted to appear wealthy at the ball. After losing it, she and her husband decided to replace it without telling Madame Forestier the truth. Their decision to conceal the loss resulted in ten years of hardship. Had Matilda admitted her mistake, the couple might have avoided the enormous debt. Therefore, although her circumstances contributed to her dissatisfaction, her vanity, poor judgement and dishonesty were major causes of her downfall.

Q: Do you think Matilda's husband was responsible for the couple's suffering?

A: Matilda's husband was not primarily responsible for their suffering. He was caring, generous and willing to sacrifice his own wishes to make his wife happy. He gave her the money he had saved for a gun so that she could buy a suitable dress. After the necklace disappeared, he also made considerable efforts to find it and arranged the money needed to replace it. However, his decision to support the plan to conceal the loss contributed to the couple's difficulties. Instead of telling Madame Forestier the truth, they purchased a replacement necklace at a very high cost. Thus, Matilda's husband was largely a victim of the circumstances, although their shared decision to conceal the truth prolonged their suffering.

Q: What does the story suggest about the difference between appearance and reality?

A: The story demonstrates that outward appearances can be misleading. Matilda believes that wearing beautiful clothes and expensive jewellery will make her appear wealthy and socially important. At the ball, she receives admiration because of her elegant appearance. However, the necklace she considers a symbol of wealth is actually imitation jewellery. Its apparent value differs greatly from its real worth. Similarly, Matilda's desire to maintain appearances prevents her from admitting the truth when she loses the necklace. Her attempt to protect her reputation ultimately causes ten years of hardship. The story teaches that appearances do not always reflect reality and that people should not sacrifice honesty, peace or financial security merely to impress others.

Q: If Matilda had been honest from the beginning, how might the story have ended differently?

A: If Matilda had been honest from the beginning, she could have informed Madame Forestier that she had lost the necklace. Madame Forestier might then have revealed that the jewellery was imitation and worth only five hundred francs. Even if Matilda had needed to compensate her friend, the amount would probably have been far less than the thirty-six thousand francs spent on the replacement. The couple could have avoided borrowing a large sum of money, moving into an attic and spending ten years repaying their debt. This alternative ending highlights the importance of honesty and open communication. It demonstrates that admitting a mistake may be uncomfortable initially but can prevent much greater problems later.

Q: Why is the final revelation important to the story?

A: The final revelation is important because it changes the reader's understanding of the entire story. Until the end, Matilda and her husband appear to have made a tremendous sacrifice to replace a valuable diamond necklace. After ten years of hardship, Matilda finally tells Madame Forestier the truth. Madame Forestier then reveals that the original necklace was imitation jewellery worth no more than five hundred francs. This discovery exposes the unnecessary nature of the couple's suffering and highlights the irony of the plot. It also reinforces the themes of vanity, appearances, honesty and poor judgement. The ending leaves readers reflecting on how a small mistake, combined with fear and dishonesty, can lead to enormous consequences.
