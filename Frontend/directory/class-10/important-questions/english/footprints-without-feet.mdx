### Section A: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

Q: Who was Griffin? What extraordinary discovery did he make?

A: Griffin was a brilliant scientist who discovered how to make the human body invisible. After conducting numerous experiments, he swallowed certain rare drugs that made his body transparent like glass while keeping it solid. However, instead of using his discovery responsibly, he misused it for selfish and criminal purposes.

Q: How did Griffin become invisible?

A: Griffin conducted several experiments to prove that the human body could become invisible. Finally, he swallowed certain rare drugs that made his body transparent like a sheet of glass. Although he became invisible, his body remained solid.

Q: Why did Griffin set fire to his landlord's house?

A: Griffin's landlord disliked him and tried to evict him. To take revenge, Griffin set fire to the house. He then removed his clothes to escape without being seen, becoming invisible and homeless.

Q: Why was Griffin wandering the streets of London without clothes?

A: After setting fire to his landlord's house, Griffin removed his clothes to become invisible and escape. Consequently, he became homeless, without money or clothing. However, the cold winter weather made it difficult for him to remain outdoors without clothes.

Q: How did Griffin first become visible after becoming invisible?

A: Griffin entered a large London departmental store to protect himself from the winter cold. After the store closed, he stole clothes, shoes, an overcoat and a hat. Once dressed, he became visible because his clothes could be seen even though his body was invisible.

Q: Why did Griffin enter the London departmental store?

A: Griffin entered the departmental store because it was the middle of winter, and the weather was bitterly cold. Since he was invisible but naked, he needed clothing and warmth. After the store closed, he stole clothes and food and spent the night sleeping on a pile of quilts.

Q: Why did Griffin have to escape from the departmental store the following morning?

A: Griffin overslept and did not wake up before the store assistants arrived. When he saw them approaching, he panicked and ran away. He escaped only by removing the clothes he had stolen, becoming invisible again in the cold January air.

Q: How did Griffin disguise himself at the theatrical shop?

A: Griffin visited a theatrical shop in Drury Lane, hoping to find clothes and items to conceal his invisible body. He wore bandages around his forehead, dark glasses, a false nose, large bushy side-whiskers and a large hat. This disguise made him appear like an unusual but visible person.

Q: How did Griffin obtain money after visiting the theatrical shop?

A: Griffin attacked the shopkeeper from behind and robbed him of all the money he could find. He used his invisibility to approach the shopkeeper without being noticed, demonstrating how he misused his scientific discovery for criminal purposes.

Q: Why did Griffin travel to the village of Iping?

A: Griffin wanted to escape the crowded streets of London and find a quiet place to live. Therefore, he travelled by train to the village of Iping and booked two rooms at the local inn. He hoped to remain undisturbed while continuing his work.

Q: Why did Mrs Hall find Griffin eccentric?

A: Mrs Hall found Griffin eccentric because of his unusual appearance, secretive behaviour, irritable temper and refusal to interact with others. He wore bandages, dark glasses and other items to conceal his invisible body. He also insisted that he wanted solitude and did not wish to be disturbed.

Q: What explanation did Griffin give Mrs Hall for his unusual appearance?

A: Griffin told Mrs Hall that an accident had affected his face. He also explained that he had come to Iping because he wanted solitude and did not wish to be disturbed while working. Mrs Hall accepted his explanation and excused his strange behaviour because he had paid in advance.

Q: How did Griffin steal money from the clergyman's house?

A: Griffin entered the clergyman's house invisibly and stole money from the desk in the study. The clergyman and his wife heard noises and rushed downstairs, but they found the room empty. Since Griffin was invisible, they could not identify the thief.

Q: Why was the clergyman surprised when he entered his study?

A: The clergyman and his wife had been awakened by noises in their study. When they entered the room, they discovered that money had been taken from the desk, but there was no sign of anyone. They searched the room carefully but could not find the intruder, leaving the clergyman astonished.

Q: What extraordinary things happened in Mrs Hall's house?

A: Mrs Hall and her husband entered Griffin's room when they found its door open. Although the room appeared empty, the furniture suddenly behaved strangely. A hat flew at Mrs Hall's face, and a chair sprang into the air and pushed both of them out of the room. The door then appeared to close and lock itself, frightening them.

Q: Why did Mrs Hall believe that Griffin's room was haunted?

A: Mrs Hall believed that Griffin's room was haunted because objects appeared to move without anyone touching them. A hat flew at her face, and a chair attacked her and her husband, forcing them out of the room. Unable to explain these events, she believed that supernatural forces were responsible.

Q: Why did the villagers suspect Griffin of the burglary?

A: The villagers suspected Griffin because he behaved strangely and had recently admitted that he had no money. Shortly after the clergyman's money disappeared, Griffin suddenly produced some cash. This suspicious change, combined with his unusual behaviour, strengthened their belief that he was responsible for the burglary.

Q: How did Griffin reveal his invisibility to the people at the inn?

A: When Mrs Hall confronted Griffin about the strange incidents in his room, he became furious. He shouted that she did not understand who or what he was and decided to demonstrate his identity. He removed his bandages, whiskers, spectacles and false nose, revealing a headless-looking figure and shocking everyone.

Q: Who was Mr Jaffers? Why did he come to the inn?

A: Mr Jaffers was the village constable. He arrived at the inn to arrest Griffin after the villagers suspected him of involvement in the clergyman's burglary. However, Griffin's invisibility made the arrest extremely difficult because the constable could not see him.

Q: How did Griffin escape from the constable?

A: When Mr Jaffers attempted to arrest Griffin, the scientist began removing his clothes one by one. As each garment was removed, Griffin became increasingly invisible. Eventually, he became completely invisible and fought back against those trying to catch him. He knocked Jaffers unconscious and escaped.

### Section B: Long Answer Questions (5-6 Marks)

Q: Write a character sketch of Griffin.

A: Griffin is the central character of H. G. Wells's story Footprints Without Feet. He is an exceptionally brilliant scientist who discovers how to make the human body invisible. His scientific achievement demonstrates his intelligence, determination and experimental ability. However, Griffin is also lawless, selfish and irresponsible. When his landlord attempts to evict him, he sets fire to the house out of revenge. He steals clothes, food and money to satisfy his needs and attacks a shopkeeper to obtain cash. He also burgles the clergyman's house and frightens the people at the inn by misusing his invisibility. Griffin's quick temper and lack of consideration for others make him dangerous. Rather than using his discovery for the benefit of humanity, he uses it to commit crimes and escape punishment. Thus, Griffin represents the danger of scientific knowledge when it is not accompanied by morality, responsibility and respect for the law.

Q: Griffin was a brilliant scientist but a lawless person. Explain.

A: Griffin was a brilliant scientist who successfully discovered a method of making the human body invisible. Through repeated experiments, he developed a way to make his body transparent while retaining its solidity. This achievement demonstrates his scientific intelligence and determination. However, Griffin's behaviour reveals a complete lack of moral responsibility. When his landlord attempted to evict him, he set fire to the house in revenge. He stole clothing and food from a departmental store, robbed a theatrical shopkeeper and stole money from a clergyman's house. He also misused his invisibility to frighten Mrs Hall and her husband by making furniture appear to move on its own. When the constable attempted to arrest him, Griffin fought back and escaped. These incidents show that scientific brilliance alone does not make someone admirable. Griffin's discovery could have been valuable, but his selfishness and criminal behaviour turned it into a means of harming others.

Q: How did Griffin misuse his scientific discovery?

A: Griffin misused his discovery of invisibility for personal gain and criminal activities. Instead of using his scientific achievement responsibly, he repeatedly broke the law. After becoming invisible, he set fire to his landlord's house in revenge. He entered a departmental store and stole clothes, food and other necessities. Later, he visited a theatrical shop, disguised himself with bandages and other accessories, attacked the shopkeeper and robbed him. Griffin then travelled to Iping, where he stole money from a clergyman's house. He also used his invisibility to frighten Mrs Hall and her husband by making objects appear to move mysteriously. When the villagers became suspicious, he fought against the constable and escaped. These incidents demonstrate that Griffin used his scientific knowledge to commit crimes rather than to benefit society. The story highlights the importance of ethical responsibility in scientific research and warns against the misuse of knowledge.

Q: Describe Griffin's adventures in London.

A: After becoming invisible, Griffin found himself homeless, without money or clothes. He wandered through London during the bitterly cold winter. To protect himself from the cold, he entered a large departmental store and hid until it closed. Once the store was empty, he stole clothes, shoes, an overcoat and a hat. He also obtained food from the store and nearby grocery shop before sleeping on a pile of quilts. However, Griffin overslept and was discovered by the assistants the following morning. He escaped by removing his clothes, becoming invisible again. Still needing clothing and money, Griffin visited a theatrical shop in Drury Lane. There, he obtained bandages, dark glasses, a false nose, side-whiskers and a large hat to disguise his appearance. Before leaving, he attacked the shopkeeper from behind and stole his money. These adventures reveal Griffin's resourcefulness but also his dishonesty and willingness to harm others to meet his needs.

Q: Describe the strange incidents that took place at the inn in Iping.

A: Griffin's arrival at the inn in Iping created curiosity because of his unusual appearance and secretive behaviour. Mrs Hall initially tried to be friendly, but Griffin insisted that he wanted solitude. Soon afterwards, a strange incident occurred at the clergyman's house. Money was stolen from his study, yet the room appeared empty when he and his wife investigated. Later, Mrs Hall and her husband entered Griffin's room when they found its door open. Although nobody appeared to be present, the furniture suddenly behaved strangely. A hat flew at Mrs Hall's face, and a chair sprang into the air and pushed both of them out of the room. Mrs Hall became convinced that the room was haunted. The villagers grew suspicious of Griffin, especially when he suddenly produced money after claiming to have none. When Mrs Hall confronted him, Griffin removed his disguise and revealed his invisible body. The constable arrived to arrest him, but Griffin became completely invisible and escaped.

Q: How did Griffin's behaviour lead to suspicion among the villagers of Iping?

A: Griffin's unusual appearance and secretive behaviour immediately attracted attention in Iping. He wore bandages, dark glasses, a false nose and other items to conceal his invisible body. He refused to interact with people and became angry when anyone disturbed him. Initially, Mrs Hall tolerated his behaviour because he had paid in advance. However, suspicion increased when money was stolen from the clergyman's house and Griffin suddenly produced cash despite having recently claimed that he had none. The mysterious movement of furniture in Griffin's room further frightened the villagers. Mrs Hall confronted him about these incidents, and Griffin responded angrily by removing his disguise and revealing his invisible body. The villagers' suspicion eventually led to the arrival of Constable Jaffers. However, Griffin used his invisibility to fight back and escape arrest.

Q: What message does H. G. Wells convey through Footprints Without Feet?

A: H. G. Wells conveys the message that scientific knowledge must be accompanied by morality and responsibility. Griffin's discovery of invisibility demonstrates extraordinary scientific achievement, but he uses it for selfish and criminal purposes. Instead of benefiting society, Griffin steals clothes, food and money, attacks a shopkeeper and frightens innocent people. He believes that invisibility allows him to escape the consequences of his actions. However, his behaviour creates fear and suspicion wherever he goes. His misuse of science eventually turns his discovery into a source of trouble for himself and others. The story warns that scientific progress without ethical judgement can be dangerous. It also emphasises the importance of respecting the law and considering the consequences of one's actions.

Q: How does the story show that intelligence without morality can be dangerous?

A: Griffin's character illustrates the dangers of intelligence without morality. He possesses exceptional scientific ability and successfully discovers how to make himself invisible. However, he lacks the ethical judgement needed to use this discovery responsibly. He uses invisibility to commit thefts, take revenge on his landlord and frighten innocent people. His scientific knowledge gives him opportunities to avoid detection, but he uses those opportunities for selfish purposes. His behaviour creates fear among the villagers and leads to a confrontation with the constable. Instead of using his intelligence to improve human life, Griffin makes himself a threat to society. The story therefore suggests that knowledge and ability must be guided by ethical principles. Intelligence becomes dangerous when it is used without consideration for others.

### Section C: Important Higher-Order Thinking Questions

Q: Why is the title Footprints Without Feet appropriate?

A: The title Footprints Without Feet is appropriate because it refers to the mysterious footprints left by Griffin while he is invisible. At the beginning of the story, two boys notice muddy footprints appearing on the steps of a house and continuing down the street, even though no person is visible. The footprints are created when Griffin, an invisible scientist, steps into mud. Although his body cannot be seen, the marks left by his feet reveal his presence. The title captures the mystery surrounding Griffin's scientific discovery and introduces the unusual events that follow. It also reflects the central idea of the story: invisibility can conceal a person's body but does not necessarily conceal every trace of their presence.

Q: Was Griffin a victim of circumstances, or was he responsible for his own problems?

A: Although Griffin faced difficulties after becoming invisible, he was largely responsible for his own problems. His landlord's attempt to evict him angered him, but setting fire to the house was a deliberate act of revenge. Similarly, his lack of clothing and money forced him to seek shelter, but he chose to steal from shops and attack a shopkeeper. Griffin repeatedly used his scientific discovery to commit crimes instead of seeking lawful ways to meet his needs. His behaviour at the inn also created fear and suspicion among the villagers. Therefore, while his circumstances were difficult, his decisions made the situation worse. The story suggests that people must accept responsibility for their actions rather than use difficult circumstances to justify wrongdoing.

Q: How does Griffin's invisibility help him, and how does it create difficulties for him?

A: Griffin's invisibility helps him escape detection, enter places unnoticed and commit thefts. It allows him to leave his landlord's house after setting it on fire and steal money from the clergyman's study without being seen. However, invisibility also creates serious difficulties. Griffin cannot remain invisible without removing his clothes, which leaves him exposed to the cold. He struggles to obtain food, clothing and shelter without attracting attention. He must also find ways to disguise himself when he wants to interact with others. His strange behaviour and criminal activities create suspicion among the villagers. Ultimately, although invisibility helps him escape the constable, it also makes his life difficult and isolates him from society.

Q: What would have happened if Griffin had used his discovery responsibly?

A: If Griffin had used his discovery responsibly, his scientific achievement might have benefited society. He could have continued his research, documented his experiments and explored the scientific possibilities of invisibility without harming others. Instead of stealing clothes, food and money, he could have sought lawful assistance and protection. He would also have avoided frightening the villagers and creating suspicion through criminal behaviour. Responsible conduct might have allowed him to earn recognition for his scientific work rather than become known for theft and violence. The story suggests that scientific discoveries can have positive consequences when guided by ethical principles. Griffin's experience demonstrates how selfishness and irresponsibility can undermine even an extraordinary achievement.

### Section D: Questions Based on the NCERT Textbook

Q: Griffin was rather a lawless person. Comment.

A: Griffin was a lawless person because he repeatedly violated the law and showed little concern for the consequences of his actions. He set fire to his landlord's house in revenge, stole clothes and food from a departmental store, robbed a shopkeeper and stole money from a clergyman's house. He also used his invisibility to frighten Mrs Hall and her husband. When the constable tried to arrest him, Griffin fought back and escaped. These actions demonstrate that Griffin lacked respect for the law and the rights of others. Although he was a brilliant scientist, his criminal behaviour made him a threat to society.

Q: How would you assess Griffin as a scientist?

A: Griffin was an exceptionally brilliant and determined scientist. Through repeated experiments, he discovered a way to make the human body transparent while retaining its solidity. This extraordinary achievement demonstrates his scientific curiosity and experimental ability. However, Griffin lacked ethical responsibility. Rather than using his discovery for constructive purposes, he used it to commit crimes and avoid detection. Therefore, Griffin can be admired for his scientific intelligence and achievement, but his irresponsible behaviour cannot be justified. The story emphasises that scientific excellence must be accompanied by moral judgement.

Q: What advantages and disadvantages would invisibility offer?

A: Invisibility could offer advantages such as observing situations without being noticed, conducting certain scientific investigations and carrying out rescue operations in dangerous environments. However, it could also create serious disadvantages. An invisible person might struggle to obtain food, clothing and shelter without attracting suspicion. Invisibility could also encourage privacy violations, theft and other crimes. Griffin's experience illustrates these difficulties. Although invisibility allows him to escape detection, he struggles with the cold, needs clothing and becomes involved in criminal activities. Therefore, invisibility might offer unusual possibilities, but its effects would depend on how responsibly it was used.
