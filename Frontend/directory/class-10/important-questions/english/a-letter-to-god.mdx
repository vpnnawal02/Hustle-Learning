### Section A: 1-Mark Questions

Q: What was Lencho waiting for to ensure a good harvest?

A: Lencho was waiting for rain to ensure a good harvest.

Q: Why did Lencho compare the raindrops to new coins?

A: Lencho compared the raindrops to new coins because he believed the rain would help his crops grow and bring him a good income.

Q: What destroyed Lencho's crops?

A: A severe hailstorm destroyed Lencho's crops.

Q: How much money did Lencho ask God to send him?

A: Lencho asked God to send him one hundred pesos.

Q: What did Lencho write on the envelope of his letter?

A: Lencho wrote To God on the envelope.

Q: Who first read Lencho's letter addressed to God?

A: A post office employee who was also a postman first read Lencho's letter.

Q: Why did the postmaster decide to help Lencho?

A: The postmaster decided to help Lencho because he was impressed by the farmer's unwavering faith in God.

Q: How much money did Lencho receive in response to his letter?

A: Lencho received seventy pesos.

Q: What did Lencho believe about God's ability to make mistakes?

A: Lencho believed that God could neither make a mistake nor deny him what he had requested.

Q: Why did Lencho become angry after counting the money?

A: Lencho became angry because he had received only seventy pesos instead of the hundred pesos he had requested.

Q: What did Lencho call the post office employees in his second letter?

A: Lencho called the post office employees a bunch of crooks.

Q: What does the word conscience mean in the context of the story?

A: Conscience refers to a person's inner sense of right and wrong.

Q: Identify the literary device used when Lencho compares raindrops to coins.

A: The literary device is a metaphor, as Lencho compares raindrops to coins.

Q: What does the expression an ox of a man suggest about Lencho?

A: The expression suggests that Lencho was a strong, hardworking man who worked tirelessly in his fields.

Q: Which two major conflicts are presented in the story?

A: The two major conflicts are the conflict between human beings and nature and the conflict between human beings themselves.

Q: Why did Lencho need money even after receiving some help?

A: Lencho needed money to sow his field again and support his family until the next crop was ready.

Q: What was the occupation of Lencho?

A: Lencho was a farmer.

Q: Who contributed money to help Lencho?

A: The postmaster, his employees, and several friends contributed money to help Lencho.

Q: Why did the postmaster sign the reply letter as God?

A: The postmaster signed the letter as God to preserve Lencho's faith in God.

Q: What is the irony at the end of the story?

A: The irony is that the post office employees who helped Lencho were accused by him of stealing the money.

### Section B: 2-Mark Questions

Q: Why was Lencho eagerly looking at the sky before the rain arrived?

A: Lencho was eagerly watching the sky because his crops needed rain to grow properly. He expected a good harvest and knew that sufficient rainfall was essential for his family's livelihood.

Q: How did the hailstorm change Lencho's expectations for the year?

A: Initially, Lencho expected the rain to ensure a good harvest. However, the rain turned into a hailstorm that destroyed his entire crop. His hopes of earning an income disappeared, and he feared that his family would go hungry.

Q: Why did Lencho believe that God would help his family?

A: Lencho had complete faith in God's power and believed that God watched over everyone. Since his family faced starvation after the destruction of their crops, he trusted God to provide the money they needed.

Q: How did Lencho react when the hailstorm ended?

A: Lencho was devastated when he saw that his crops had been completely destroyed. He realised that his family would have no harvest that year and feared that they would suffer from hunger.

Q: Why did Lencho consider the hailstones to be silver coins initially?

A: Lencho initially compared the raindrops to coins because he expected the rain to bring him a good harvest and financial prosperity. The hailstones also resembled silver coins, but they ultimately destroyed his crops.

Q: What made the postmaster admire Lencho's faith?

A: The postmaster was deeply impressed by Lencho's absolute confidence that God would answer his letter. He admired this extraordinary faith and decided to help the farmer rather than allow him to lose hope.

Q: How did the postmaster arrange the money for Lencho?

A: The postmaster contributed part of his own salary and collected money from his employees and friends. Although he could not arrange the full hundred pesos, he managed to send Lencho seventy pesos.

Q: Why was the postmaster unable to send the entire amount requested by Lencho?

A: The postmaster could not collect the entire hundred pesos despite his efforts. He therefore sent seventy pesos to Lencho, hoping that the contribution would provide some relief to the farmer.

Q: Why did Lencho show no surprise when he received the envelope containing money?

A: Lencho was completely confident that God would answer his letter. Therefore, receiving the money did not surprise him because he considered God's response natural and expected.

Q: Why did Lencho refuse to believe that the money sent by God could be incomplete?

A: Lencho believed that God was perfect and could neither make mistakes nor deny anyone what they deserved. Consequently, he assumed that someone else must have taken the missing money.

Q: What does the story reveal about Lencho's attitude towards God?

A: The story reveals that Lencho had unwavering and unquestioning faith in God. He believed that God could see everyone's needs and would always provide help in times of difficulty.

Q: Why did Lencho suspect the post office employees of taking the missing money?

A: Lencho believed that God would never send less money than requested. Since he received only seventy pesos, he concluded that the post office employees must have stolen the remaining thirty pesos.

Q: How did the postmaster's actions demonstrate his kindness?

A: The postmaster collected money from his employees and friends and contributed part of his own salary to help a stranger. His actions demonstrated generosity, empathy, and concern for someone in distress.

Q: Explain the significance of the title A Letter to God.

A: The title refers to Lencho's letter requesting financial assistance from God after his crops are destroyed. It highlights his extraordinary faith and introduces the central themes of belief, kindness, and irony.

Q: How did nature become an obstacle in Lencho's life?

A: Lencho depended on his crops for survival. Although the rain initially appeared beneficial, it turned into a destructive hailstorm that ruined his entire harvest and threatened his family's livelihood.

Q: Why can Lencho's behaviour at the end be considered ungrateful?

A: Lencho received money collected by the postmaster and his colleagues, who had made a sincere effort to help him. Instead of appreciating their generosity, he accused them of stealing the remaining money.

Q: What does the story suggest about the importance of faith during difficult times?

A: The story suggests that faith can provide hope and emotional strength during difficult circumstances. Lencho's belief in God encouraged him to seek help despite his family's desperate situation.

Q: How did the postmaster attempt to preserve Lencho's faith in God?

A: The postmaster arranged financial assistance and sent Lencho a letter signed God. He wanted Lencho to believe that his prayer had been answered and that his faith had not been misplaced.

Q: Why did Lencho believe that only God could help his family?

A: After the hailstorm destroyed his crops, Lencho saw no immediate way to support his family. Because of his deep religious faith, he believed that God was the only source of reliable assistance.

Q: What contrast is presented between Lencho's faith and his understanding of human kindness?

A: Lencho trusted God completely but failed to recognise the kindness of the people who helped him. The postmaster and his colleagues acted generously, yet Lencho suspected them of dishonesty.

His kindness creates the central irony because Lencho later accuses the post office employees of stealing the missing money.

Although they collect money to help him, Lencho believes that they have stolen part of it. His suspicion creates an imaginary conflict between himself and the very people who have supported him. This demonstrates how mistrust can prevent people from recognising genuine kindness.

However, Lencho receives less than he expected and accuses the post office employees of stealing the difference. The people who help him are treated as dishonest, while his faith in God remains unquestioned.

The postmaster reads the letter and organises a collection among his employees and friends. They manage to collect seventy pesos, which are sent to Lencho in an envelope signed God.

A: The postmaster was moved by Lencho's faith and decided to help him financially. He contributed part of his salary and persuaded his colleagues and friends to donate money. Although Lencho was a stranger, they made a sincere effort to support him. Their actions demonstrate generosity, empathy, and a willingness to help someone in need.

However, his unquestioning belief prevents him from considering other explanations when he receives less money than requested. He immediately accuses the post office employees of theft. Thus, his faith provides hope but also contributes to his misunderstanding of human kindness.

A: Lencho believed that God would fulfil his request completely. When he received only seventy pesos, he assumed that God had sent the full amount and that someone had taken the remaining thirty pesos. Because he never suspected that the money had been collected by ordinary people, he wrongly accused the post office employees.

A: Lencho expects the rain to produce a good harvest, but the hailstorm destroys his crops. He then expects God to send one hundred pesos, but receives only seventy. In reality, the money comes from the generosity of the postmaster and his colleagues. Lencho fails to recognise their contribution and instead believes that they have stolen his money.

Although he spends most of his time performing demanding agricultural work, he possesses the literacy needed to write a letter. This ability allows him to communicate his desperate situation to God and request financial assistance.

However, Lencho assumes that God sent the money and accuses the employees of stealing the remainder. His reaction demonstrates that people may fail to recognise kindness when their expectations and assumptions influence their judgement.

A: The destruction of the crops caused severe economic hardship because Lencho depended on his harvest for income and food. Without a crop, he could not support his family or sow his field again. Emotionally, the family experienced sorrow, anxiety, and hopelessness. Lencho's concern about starvation eventually led him to seek help from God.

Q: What qualities of the postmaster make him a sympathetic character?

A: The postmaster is compassionate, generous, and empathetic. Rather than dismissing Lencho's unusual letter, he admires the farmer's faith and decides to help him. He contributes his own salary and collects money from others. His willingness to assist a stranger makes him a sympathetic character.

A: The author contrasts the promising rain with the destructive hailstorm, Lencho's faith in God with his distrust of human beings, and the postmaster's generosity with Lencho's accusation. These contrasts create emotional tension and strengthen the irony of the ending. They also highlight the difference between good intentions and how others interpret them.

His accusation against the post office employees demonstrates his limited understanding of their intentions.

His behaviour demonstrates how unquestioning assumptions can damage human relationships and prevent gratitude.

Q: Explain how the title reflects the central theme of the story.

A: The title focuses on Lencho's letter to God, which represents his absolute faith and desperate need for assistance. The letter sets the story's events in motion and leads to an unexpected act of human kindness. Its outcome highlights faith, generosity, misunderstanding, and irony.

A: The statement accurately captures the central irony of the story. After a hailstorm destroys his crops, Lencho writes to God requesting one hundred pesos. He accuses the post office employees of being dishonest. The irony lies in the fact that the people who help Lencho become the targets of his suspicion. Their kindness goes unrecognised because Lencho refuses to consider their contribution. The story demonstrates that genuine generosity may be misunderstood when people make assumptions without understanding the truth.

Q: Lencho's faith is admirable, but his inability to trust other human beings leads to a misunderstanding. Evaluate by analysing the major events of the story.

Instead, he writes a letter to God requesting one hundred pesos to support his family and sow his field again. His belief encourages him to seek assistance, and the postmaster responds with compassion. He collects money from colleagues and friends and sends Lencho seventy pesos. Q: How does G. L. Fuentes use characterisation, contrast, and irony to communicate the central message of A Letter to God?

A: G. L. Fuentes develops the story's message through characterisation, contrast, and irony. Lencho is portrayed as a hardworking farmer with absolute faith in God. His character represents hope and determination, but his suspicion of the post office employees also reveals his naivety. The postmaster is presented as compassionate and generous. He is moved by Lencho's faith and arranges financial assistance despite having no personal relationship with the farmer. The author uses contrast to highlight the difference between Lencho's trust in God and his distrust of human beings. Another contrast appears between the destructive hailstorm and the kindness shown by the postmaster. Irony becomes most apparent at the end. The post office employees, who collect money to help Lencho, are accused of stealing it. Through these techniques, the author highlights the importance of faith, generosity, gratitude, and understanding human intentions.
