Chapter revision of The Thief's Story by Ruskin Bond. Practise short, long, competency-based, extract-based and additional questions. Tap any question to reveal its answer.

### Section A: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

Q: Why did Hari Singh approach Anil?

A: Hari Singh approached Anil with the intention of gaining his trust and eventually robbing him. He considered Anil an easy target because he appeared kind, simple and easy-going. Hari used flattery to initiate a conversation and convince Anil to give him work.

Q: Why did Hari Singh change his name every month?

A: Hari Singh changed his name every month to avoid being caught by the police and recognised by his former employers. As an experienced thief, he used different identities to escape detection and continue his dishonest activities.

Q: How did Anil react when Hari Singh asked him for work?

A: Anil told Hari that he could not pay him. However, when Hari claimed that he could cook, Anil agreed to provide him with food and shelter in exchange for his services. Later, Anil also offered to teach him cooking, reading, writing and arithmetic.

Q: Why did Anil decide to teach Hari Singh how to read and write?

A: Anil decided to teach Hari Singh because he was willing to help the young boy improve his life. He offered to teach him to write his name and gradually progress to complete sentences and arithmetic. This education gave Hari hope for a better future.

Q: How did Hari Singh make money while working for Anil?

A: Hari Singh made money by cheating while purchasing daily supplies. He usually kept approximately one rupee from the money given to him for shopping. Although Anil seemed aware of this dishonesty, he did not confront Hari about it.

Q: Why was it difficult for Hari Singh to rob Anil?

A: It was difficult for Hari Singh to rob Anil because Anil was a trusting and generous person. Unlike a greedy person, Anil did not constantly worry about his possessions. Hari also realised that stealing from Anil would mean betraying someone who had offered him shelter, trust and an opportunity to receive an education.

Q: Why did Hari Singh steal Anil's money?

A: Hari Singh was tempted by the six hundred rupees Anil had received after selling a book to a publisher. He believed that the money would allow him to live comfortably for a week or two. He also tried to justify the theft by thinking that Anil did not pay him a regular salary and might waste the money on his friends.

Q: Why did Hari Singh fail to board the Lucknow Express?

A: Hari Singh reached the railway station just as the Lucknow Express was leaving. Although he could have jumped into one of the carriages, he hesitated for reasons he could not explain and missed the opportunity to escape. His hesitation reflected his growing inner conflict and attachment to Anil.

Q: What made Hari Singh return to Anil after stealing the money?

A: Hari Singh's conscience and his desire to receive an education made him return to Anil. He realised that the stolen money would provide only temporary comfort, whereas learning to read and write could help him build a better future. He also understood that Anil's trust was more valuable than the money.

Q: Why did Hari Singh put the money back under the mattress?

A: Hari Singh returned the money because he regretted betraying Anil's trust. He realised that stealing would not help him become a respected and successful person in the long run. He wanted to preserve his relationship with Anil and continue learning from him.

Q: How did Anil react after discovering the theft?

A: Anil did not openly confront Hari Singh or hand him over to the police. Instead, he gave Hari a fifty-rupee note and told him that he would now be paid regularly. Although Anil understood what had happened, he chose to forgive Hari and continued his education.

Q: What is the significance of the wet fifty-rupee note?

A: The wet fifty-rupee note suggests that Anil knew Hari had stolen the money and later returned it in the rain. Anil's decision to give him the note without confrontation shows his understanding and forgiveness. It also indicates that he wants Hari to reform rather than face punishment.

Q: What change comes over Hari Singh by the end of the story?

A: By the end of the story, Hari Singh begins to move away from dishonesty towards a more responsible life. Initially, he sees Anil merely as someone he can rob. However, Anil's kindness and his offer of education awaken Hari's conscience. Hari returns the stolen money and decides to continue learning.

Q: Why did Anil not hand Hari Singh over to the police?

A: Anil understood that Hari Singh was a young boy who still had the opportunity to change. Rather than punishing him, Anil chose to give him another chance through kindness, trust and education. His response suggests that he believed reform was more valuable than punishment.

### Section B: Long Answer Questions (5-6 Marks)

Q: Write a character sketch of Hari Singh.

A: Hari Singh is a fifteen-year-old boy who has become an experienced thief despite his young age. He is clever, observant and manipulative, as he uses flattery to gain Anil's confidence. He frequently changes his name to avoid being caught by the police and his former employers. When he begins living with Anil, he continues his dishonest behaviour by making small profits while shopping. However, Hari is not entirely without a conscience. Anil's kindness and willingness to teach him how to read and write gradually influence him. Although he steals six hundred rupees, he begins to feel guilty and realises that education could provide him with a better future than theft. He eventually returns the money and decides to continue learning. Thus, Hari Singh is a complex character who struggles between dishonesty and the desire to reform. His transformation demonstrates that trust, education and compassion can encourage a person to change.

Q: Write a character sketch of Anil.

A: Anil is a twenty-five-year-old writer who is kind-hearted, generous, simple and trusting. When Hari Singh approaches him, Anil agrees to provide him with food and shelter despite being unable to pay him a regular salary initially. He also offers to teach Hari cooking, reading, writing and arithmetic, thereby giving the young boy an opportunity to improve his future. Anil is trusting enough to allow Hari to come and go freely and does not appear to object when Hari makes small profits while shopping. Even after Hari steals his money, Anil does not confront him or report him to the police. Instead, he gives Hari a fifty-rupee note and promises to pay him regularly. Anil's forgiving nature and faith in the possibility of reform make him an important influence in Hari's life. His character highlights the power of kindness, trust and education in bringing about positive change.

Q: Describe the circumstances that led Hari Singh to return the stolen money.

A: After stealing six hundred rupees from Anil, Hari Singh rushes towards the railway station to catch the Lucknow Express. However, he hesitates as the train begins to leave and misses his chance to escape. He then wanders through the streets in the rain, thinking about the consequences of his actions. Hari realises that Anil would be hurt not simply by the loss of money but by the betrayal of trust. He also understands that by leaving Anil, he would lose the opportunity to learn how to read and write. Education could help him become a clever, respected and successful person, whereas theft would offer only temporary gains. These thoughts awaken his conscience. Consequently, Hari returns to Anil's room and quietly places the damp money back under the mattress. His decision marks an important step towards honesty and personal reform.

Q: Education can transform a person's life. Explain with reference to The Thief's Story.

A: The story The Thief's Story demonstrates that education can provide people with opportunities to improve their lives. Hari Singh is initially an experienced thief who believes that stealing is an easy way to earn money. However, his relationship with Anil introduces him to the possibility of a different future. Anil offers to teach Hari how to read, write and perform basic calculations. Hari recognises that literacy could help him achieve far more than the money he earns through theft. When he steals Anil's savings, he initially plans to escape, but eventually realises that leaving would mean sacrificing his chance to receive an education. He therefore returns the stolen money and chooses to remain with Anil. His decision shows that education can encourage people to think beyond immediate gains and work towards a more secure and respectable future.

Q: Trust and kindness can bring about a positive change in a person's life. Justify this statement with reference to the story.

A: Trust and kindness play a significant role in transforming Hari Singh's character. Initially, Hari approaches Anil with the intention of robbing him. However, Anil treats him generously, offers him food and shelter, and promises to teach him how to read and write. Even when Hari steals six hundred rupees, Anil does not punish him or report him to the police. Instead, he gives Hari another opportunity by offering him regular payment and continuing his education. Anil's behaviour makes Hari realise the value of trust and the opportunities he might lose through dishonesty. As a result, Hari returns the stolen money and decides to continue learning. The story demonstrates that compassion and trust can encourage a person to recognise their mistakes and choose a better path. However, such change also requires the individual to respond positively to the opportunity offered.

Q: Justify the title The Thief's Story.

A: The title The Thief's Story is appropriate because the narrative revolves around Hari Singh, a young thief, and his gradual transformation. The story is narrated from Hari's perspective and describes his attempt to gain Anil's trust, his decision to steal six hundred rupees and his subsequent struggle with guilt. Initially, Hari considers Anil merely a target for theft. However, Anil's kindness and offer of education gradually influence his thinking. After stealing the money, Hari realises that education and trust are more valuable than temporary financial gains. He returns the money and chooses to remain with Anil. Thus, the title reflects both the central character's identity as a thief and the important moral change that forms the heart of the story.

Q: How does Ruskin Bond highlight the conflict between greed and conscience in the story?

A: Ruskin Bond presents the conflict between greed and conscience through Hari Singh's decision to steal Anil's money and his subsequent regret. When Hari discovers six hundred rupees under Anil's mattress, his greed encourages him to take the money and escape to Lucknow. Initially, he justifies the theft by thinking that Anil does not pay him and may waste the money. However, after reaching the railway station, Hari begins to reconsider his decision. He realises that Anil's trust and his opportunity to receive an education are more valuable than the stolen money. His conscience eventually overcomes his greed, and he returns the money. Through this internal conflict, the author shows that moral awareness and the desire for a better future can lead a person away from dishonesty.

### Section C: Competency-Based and Value-Based Questions

Q: Why does Hari Singh consider education more valuable than the stolen money?

A: Hari Singh realises that the stolen money could provide only temporary comfort, whereas education could help him achieve lasting success. He understands that learning to read and write would give him opportunities to become a respected and independent person. This realisation motivates him to return the money and continue learning from Anil.

Q: What does Anil's reaction to the theft reveal about his character?

A: Anil's reaction reveals that he is understanding, forgiving and compassionate. Although he realises that Hari has stolen and returned the money, he does not confront or punish him. Instead, he offers Hari regular payment and continues teaching him. His response suggests that he believes in giving people opportunities to correct their mistakes.

Q: Do you think Hari Singh's decision to return the money was an act of weakness? Give reasons.

A: No, Hari Singh's decision was not an act of weakness but a sign of growing maturity and moral awareness. Returning the money required him to overcome his greed and acknowledge the value of Anil's trust. He realised that education could help him build a better future than theft. His decision reflects courage, self-control and the willingness to change.

Q: What might have happened if Anil had punished Hari Singh or handed him over to the police?

A: If Anil had punished Hari or handed him over to the police, Hari might have lost the opportunity to receive an education and improve his life. Punishment could have made him more distrustful and less willing to reform. Although the outcome cannot be known with certainty, Anil's compassionate response creates an opportunity for Hari to change his behaviour and pursue a better future.

Q: What does the story teach us about forgiveness?

A: The story teaches that forgiveness can give people an opportunity to recognise their mistakes and improve themselves. Anil does not openly confront Hari after discovering the theft. Instead, he continues to trust him and offers him education and regular payment. This response encourages Hari to value honesty and reform his behaviour. The story suggests that forgiveness can be constructive when it is accompanied by a genuine opportunity to change.

### Section D: Important Extract-Based Questions

Q: Who is the speaker referring to in the extract about robbing a greedy man versus a careless man? Why does Hari find it difficult to rob Anil?

A: The speaker, Hari Singh, is referring to Anil. Hari finds it difficult because Anil is trusting and does not constantly worry about his possessions. Hari also recognises that Anil's kindness makes betraying him more difficult.

Q: What does the greedy-man statement reveal about Hari's attitude towards theft? What change does Hari eventually undergo?

A: It reveals that Hari initially views theft as a profession and evaluates potential victims according to their behaviour and financial circumstances. Hari begins to value Anil's trust and the opportunity to receive an education. He returns the stolen money, showing the beginning of a moral transformation.

Q: What does the speaker mean by whole sentences? Why does Hari consider this ability valuable?

A: The phrase refers to Hari's ability to read and write complete sentences, an important part of the education Anil has promised to provide. Hari believes that literacy could help him achieve greater success and become a respected person, providing more lasting benefits than stolen money.

Q: How does the whole-sentences thought affect his decision? What central theme does this extract highlight?

A: It encourages Hari to return the stolen money and remain with Anil so that he can continue learning. The extract highlights the transformative power of education and the importance of long-term growth over immediate financial gain.

Q: What did Anil know in the extract He knew. But neither his lips nor his eyes showed anything? How did Anil respond to Hari after the incident?

A: Anil knew that Hari had stolen the money and later returned it. Anil did not confront Hari. Instead, he gave him a fifty-rupee note, promised to pay him regularly and continued teaching him.

Q: Why did Anil choose not to confront Hari openly? What does this incident reveal about Anil's character?

A: Anil appears to have chosen forgiveness over punishment, giving Hari an opportunity to correct his behaviour and reform himself. It reveals his compassion, understanding and faith in Hari's ability to change.

### Section E: Additional Important Questions

Q: Why did Hari Singh initially consider Anil an easy target?

A: Hari considered Anil an easy target because he appeared simple, kind and easy-going. Hari believed that he could gain Anil's confidence through flattery and eventually rob him.

Q: How did Hari Singh benefit from living with Anil?

A: Hari received food and shelter and was given an opportunity to learn cooking, reading, writing and arithmetic. He also experienced the trust and kindness of someone who was willing to help him improve his life. These opportunities gradually influenced his thinking and encouraged him to abandon theft.

Q: Why did Hari feel that he could no longer cook Anil's meals or learn to write after stealing the money?

A: Hari realised that escaping with the stolen money would mean losing his relationship with Anil and the opportunity to learn valuable skills. He understood that education could provide him with a better future, whereas the stolen money would offer only temporary benefits.

Q: How does the ending of the story convey hope?

A: The ending conveys hope through Anil's decision to forgive Hari and continue teaching him. Hari's return of the stolen money indicates that he has begun to change. Anil's willingness to give him another chance suggests that trust and education can help him build a better future.

Q: What role does the first-person narration play in the story?

A: The story is narrated by Hari Singh, allowing readers to understand his thoughts, motives and internal conflict. His narration reveals how he initially plans to exploit Anil but gradually begins to value his kindness and the opportunity to receive an education. This perspective makes Hari's moral transformation central to the narrative.
