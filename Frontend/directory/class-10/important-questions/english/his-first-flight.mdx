### Section A: Objective-Type Questions (1 Mark Each)

Q: Why was the young seagull afraid to fly?
(a) He had injured his wings.
(b) He thought his wings would not support him.
(c) His parents had forbidden him to fly.

### Section B: Very Short-Answer Questions (2 Marks Each)

Q: Why did the young seagull's siblings manage to fly while he remained on the ledge?

A: His siblings had the courage to attempt flying and trusted their ability to use their wings. The young seagull, however, lacked confidence and imagined that his wings would fail to support him.

Q: How did the young seagull's parents react to his refusal to fly?

A: His parents called to him, scolded him and threatened to leave him hungry on the ledge. They attempted to make him understand that he needed to overcome his fear and learn to fly independently.

Q: What had the young seagull observed his parents teaching his siblings?

A: He had watched his parents teach his siblings how to skim the waves, dive for fish and fly confidently. He had also witnessed his older brother catching and eating his first herring.

Q: Why did the young seagull pretend to sleep?

A: He stood near the edge of the ledge and pretended to sleep while hoping to attract his family's attention. However, his family continued with its activities without immediately responding to him.

Q: Why did the young seagull cry out to his mother?

A: He was extremely hungry and wanted his mother to bring him some food. When he saw her eating a piece of fish, his hunger intensified and he begged her to feed him.

Q: Why did the mother stop before reaching the young seagull?

A: She stopped just beyond his reach while holding the fish in her beak. This tempted him to move towards the food, eventually forcing him to make the leap that initiated his first flight.

Q: Describe the young seagull's reaction immediately after he plunged into the air.

A: He was seized by overwhelming terror and initially believed that he was falling helplessly. However, he soon felt his wings spread out and realised that the air was supporting him.

Q: How did the young seagull's feelings change during his first flight?

A: Initially, he experienced intense fear and uncertainty. As he felt the air supporting his wings, his fear gradually disappeared, and he began to enjoy flying, soaring and diving through the air.

Q: What did the young seagull see when he flew over the ocean?

A: He saw a vast green sea beneath him, with small ridges moving across its surface. He also noticed his family ahead of him, resting on the water and calling to him.

Q: Why was the young seagull unable to rise after touching the sea?

A: He was exhausted and weakened by hunger after his first flight. Consequently, he could not immediately lift himself from the water, although he attempted to rise by flapping his wings.

Q: What is the significance of the young seagull's family offering him scraps of fish?

A: The gesture represents the family's happiness and approval of his achievement. It also shows that he has successfully overcome his fear and taken an important step towards independence.

Q: How does the story demonstrate that necessity can overcome fear?

A: The young seagull's intense hunger eventually becomes stronger than his fear of flying. His desperate attempt to reach the fish makes him leap into the air, where he discovers his natural ability to fly.

### Section C: Short-Answer Questions (3 Marks Each)

Q: Explain why the young seagull was unable to gather the courage to fly despite watching his siblings succeed.

A: The young seagull lacked confidence in his own abilities. Although he watched his brothers and sister take their first flights successfully, he believed that his wings would not support him. The vast distance between the ledge and the sea frightened him. His fear prevented him from making an attempt, even though he possessed the physical ability to fly.

Q: Describe the young seagull's life during the twenty-four hours before his first flight.

A: The young seagull remained alone on the ledge while his family flew about and practised flying with his siblings. He watched them learn to catch fish and witnessed his older brother's success. His parents repeatedly encouraged him to fly, but he refused. As the hours passed without food or company, his hunger and isolation increased.

Q: How did the young seagull's mother use his hunger to encourage him to fly?

A: His mother noticed his desperate hunger and picked up a piece of fish. She flew towards him but stopped just beyond the reach of his beak. Driven by hunger, the young seagull leaned forward and dived towards the food. In doing so, he left the ledge and instinctively began using his wings to fly.

Q: Describe the physical sensations experienced by the young seagull during his first flight.

A: Initially, the young seagull felt terrified as he plunged into the air. Soon, he felt his wings spread outwards and the wind rush against his breast, stomach and wings. He sensed his wing tips cutting through the air. As he began soaring and flapping his wings, he felt increasingly confident and excited.

Q: How did the young seagull's family celebrate his first flight?

A: His mother swooped past him, making a loud noise with her wings, while his father flew over him screaming. His brothers and sister flew around him, performing different aerial movements. Later, the family landed on the sea, called to him and offered him pieces of fish, celebrating his achievement.

Q: Why is the young seagull's first flight an important milestone in his life?

A: His first flight marks the transition from dependence to independence. Until then, his fear had prevented him from using his natural ability. Once he made the attempt, he discovered that he could fly successfully. The experience increased his confidence and demonstrated that overcoming fear is essential for learning and personal growth.

Q: What role do the young seagull's parents play in his development?

A: His parents play an important role by teaching his siblings to fly and encouraging him to do the same. Although they scold and threaten him, their actions are intended to make him independent. His mother uses the fish to motivate him to take the necessary leap, helping him discover his ability.

Q: Explain the significance of the title His First Flight.

A: The title refers to the young seagull's first successful experience of flying. More importantly, it represents his journey from fear and self-doubt to courage and confidence. His first flight is both a physical achievement and a symbol of personal growth, showing the importance of taking risks to overcome limitations.

Q: How does the author create suspense before the young seagull takes his first flight?

A: The author describes the great distance between the ledge and the sea, the young seagull's fear and his family's refusal to bring him food. His growing hunger creates tension, while his mother's approach with a piece of fish builds anticipation. The moment he dives towards the food becomes the turning point of the story.

Q: What does the story teach us about the importance of self-confidence?

A: The story teaches us that fear and self-doubt can prevent us from using our abilities. The young seagull believes that he cannot fly until he actually attempts it. Once he takes the leap, he discovers his capability and gains confidence. The story encourages us to face challenges instead of allowing fear to control our decisions.

### Section D: Long-Answer Questions (6 Marks Each)

Q: Fear is often an obstacle to success, but taking the first step can change everything. Explain this statement with reference to His First Flight.

A: The story His First Flight illustrates how fear can prevent individuals from discovering their abilities. The young seagull possesses wings like his siblings, but he refuses to fly because he believes they cannot support him. His fear makes him remain isolated on the ledge, even as his family learns to fly successfully. His hunger eventually forces him to move beyond his comfort zone. When his mother holds a piece of fish just beyond his reach, he dives towards it and falls into the air. Initially terrified, he soon discovers that his wings can support him. He begins flying confidently and enjoys the experience. The story demonstrates that taking the first step is often the most difficult part of overcoming a challenge. Confidence develops through experience, and success becomes possible when fear no longer prevents action.

Q: Describe the young seagull's transformation from a frightened bird into a confident flyer.

A: At the beginning of the story, the young seagull is frightened and lacks confidence. Although his siblings have already flown away, he remains on the ledge because he fears that his wings will not support him. His parents attempt to encourage him, but their efforts initially fail. As time passes, his hunger becomes unbearable. His mother approaches him with a piece of fish but stops just beyond his reach. Driven by hunger, he dives towards the food and falls into the air. His initial terror soon gives way to relief when he feels his wings supporting him. He gradually learns to control his movements, flaps his wings and begins soaring confidently. By the end, he is flying over the sea and celebrating his achievement with his family. His transformation demonstrates the power of experience, courage and self-discovery.

Q: Discuss the role of the young seagull's mother in helping him overcome his fear. Was her approach effective? Give reasons for your answer.

A: The young seagull's mother plays a decisive role in helping him overcome his fear. While his father and siblings attempt to encourage him through calls and demonstrations, he remains unwilling to fly. His mother recognises that merely calling him is insufficient to make him act. She picks up a piece of fish and flies towards him. However, she stops just beyond his reach, creating a situation in which he must choose between remaining hungry and attempting to obtain the food. Driven by hunger, he dives towards the fish and begins flying instinctively. Her approach proves effective because it motivates him to act rather than simply telling him what to do. Once he experiences flight, he discovers his ability and gains confidence. Thus, his mother's actions demonstrate that encouragement sometimes requires creating an opportunity for individuals to overcome their fears through direct experience.

Q: The young seagull's first flight represents more than learning to fly. Discuss the deeper meaning of the story.

A: The young seagull's first flight represents a journey towards independence, courage and self-confidence. Although he possesses the natural ability to fly, his fear prevents him from using it. His experience reflects the way people sometimes underestimate themselves and avoid challenges because they fear failure. His first flight begins when hunger compels him to take a risk. As he discovers that his wings can support him, his fear gradually disappears. He learns through experience that his imagined limitations were not as powerful as he had believed. The story also highlights the importance of facing unfamiliar situations. Personal growth often requires individuals to leave their comfort zones and attempt difficult tasks. The young seagull's achievement shows that abilities may remain undiscovered until they are tested. Ultimately, the story conveys that courage does not necessarily mean having no fear; it means acting despite fear and learning from the experience.

Q: Compare the young seagull's behaviour before and after his first flight. What factors bring about this change?

A: Before his first flight, the young seagull is frightened, hesitant and dependent on his family. He watches his siblings fly successfully but refuses to attempt the same because he doubts his wings' ability to support him. His fear isolates him on the ledge, while his hunger makes his situation increasingly uncomfortable. After his first flight, his behaviour changes dramatically. Once he discovers that he can fly, he begins flapping his wings confidently, soaring, diving and enjoying the movement of the air. He no longer appears paralysed by fear and joins his family over the sea. Several factors contribute to this transformation. His hunger motivates him to take the initial risk, while the physical experience of flying proves that his fears were unfounded. His family's presence and approval further reinforce his confidence. The change demonstrates the importance of experience in overcoming self-doubt.

Q: How does the story present the relationship between parental discipline and independence?

A: The story presents parental discipline as a means of preparing young individuals for independence. The young seagull's parents know that he must learn to fly to survive. They therefore encourage him to leave the ledge and join his siblings. When he refuses, they scold him and threaten to leave him without food. Although their behaviour may initially appear harsh, it reflects their understanding that he must overcome his fear rather than remain dependent on them. His mother eventually creates an opportunity for him to act by holding food just beyond his reach. Once the young seagull takes the leap, he discovers his natural ability and learns to fly. His family then celebrates his success. The story suggests that effective parenting involves providing guidance and encouragement while allowing children to face challenges, develop confidence and become independent.

### Section E: Competency-Based and Inference Questions (4 Marks Each)

Q: The young seagull believed that his wings would never support him, although his siblings had already flown successfully. What does this reveal about the difference between reality and perception?

A: The young seagull's belief is based on fear rather than evidence. His siblings' successful flights demonstrate that flying is possible, yet he continues to doubt his own ability. His perception of danger prevents him from recognising his natural capability. Once he actually attempts to fly, he discovers that his wings support him. The episode shows that our fears can distort our understanding of reality and that practical experience can challenge negative assumptions.

Q: Suppose the young seagull's mother had immediately given him the fish without encouraging him to move towards it. How might this have affected his development?

A: If his mother had immediately given him the fish, the young seagull might have satisfied his hunger without attempting to fly. His fear could have continued, delaying his independence. By holding the fish just beyond his reach, his mother created a situation that compelled him to act. This suggests that although support is important, individuals sometimes need to face challenges themselves to discover their abilities and develop confidence.

Q: The young seagull's family had been calling to him for a long time, but he did not respond until hunger became unbearable. What does this suggest about motivation?

A: The episode suggests that motivation can arise from both external encouragement and immediate personal needs. The young seagull's family repeatedly calls to him, but these efforts do not overcome his fear. His hunger eventually creates a stronger motivation to act. When the opportunity to obtain food appears, his desire becomes greater than his fear. The incident illustrates that motivation becomes effective when it encourages an individual to take concrete action.

Q: Experience is a better teacher than imagination. Justify this statement with reference to the story.

A: The young seagull imagines that his wings will fail to support him, and this belief prevents him from flying. However, his actual experience proves that his fear is unfounded. When he dives towards the fish, he discovers that his wings can keep him in the air. He gradually learns to control his movements and begins enjoying flight. The story demonstrates that imagined difficulties may appear overwhelming, but direct experience can replace uncertainty with understanding and confidence.

Q: How does the author's description of the wind and the movement of the young seagull's wings help the reader understand his changing emotions?

A: The descriptions of the wind rushing against the seagull's body and his wings cutting through the air make his first flight vivid and realistic. Initially, his experience is associated with terror and the feeling of falling. As he senses the air supporting him, his fear begins to disappear. His successful movements through the air reflect his growing confidence. The physical descriptions therefore communicate both the mechanics of flight and the young seagull's emotional transformation.

Q: What would have been the likely consequences if the young seagull had continued to refuse to fly? Explain using evidence from the story.

A: If the young seagull had continued refusing to fly, he would have remained isolated on the ledge and dependent on his family. His parents had already warned him that they would leave him without food, and he had not eaten since the previous evening. Continued refusal could therefore have threatened his survival. His siblings were learning to obtain food independently, while he remained unable to do so. The situation emphasises that overcoming fear was essential for his development and survival.

### Section F: Character-Based Questions (3-4 Marks Each)

Q: Write a character sketch of the young seagull.

A: The young seagull is initially timid, hesitant and lacking in self-confidence. Although he possesses the physical ability to fly, his fear prevents him from attempting it. He depends on his family and becomes distressed when he is left alone without food. However, his intense hunger eventually motivates him to take a risk. Once he discovers that he can fly, he becomes confident and enthusiastic. His transformation shows that fear can be overcome through action and experience.

Q: Describe the character of the young seagull's mother.

A: The young seagull's mother is caring, observant and determined to help her offspring become independent. She recognises that he must learn to fly despite his fear. Rather than simply bringing him food, she holds a piece of fish just beyond his reach, encouraging him to move towards it. Her approach proves effective because it compels him to take his first flight. Her behaviour demonstrates a combination of parental care, discipline and practical guidance.

Q: How do the young seagull's siblings contribute to the development of the story?

A: The young seagull's siblings provide a contrast to his fearful behaviour. They have already learned to fly, catch fish and move confidently through the air. Their success highlights his lack of confidence and increases the pressure on him to become independent. Later, they fly around him during his first flight, helping create a sense of celebration and family support. Their role reinforces the idea that learning and achievement are natural parts of growing up.

### Section H: Value-Based and Theme-Based Questions (3-4 Marks Each)

Q: What lesson does the story teach about stepping outside one's comfort zone?

A: The story teaches that remaining within one's comfort zone may prevent personal growth. The young seagull remains on the ledge because he fears flying, even though he possesses the ability to do so. Only when he takes the risk does he discover his capabilities. The story encourages readers to face unfamiliar situations and learn through experience.

Q: Do you think fear is always harmful? Explain with reference to the story.

A: Fear is not always harmful because it can warn individuals about danger. However, excessive fear can prevent them from taking necessary actions. The young seagull's fear initially prevents him from learning to fly, even though flying is essential for his survival. His experience shows that fear must be balanced with judgment and that unnecessary self-doubt should not prevent personal development.

Q: How does the story demonstrate the importance of perseverance?

A: The young seagull initially fails to overcome his fear despite repeated encouragement from his family. However, his situation eventually forces him to attempt flight. Once he begins, he continues flapping his wings and learns to control his movements. His experience demonstrates that progress often requires facing difficulties and making an effort despite uncertainty.

Q: What does the story suggest about the relationship between independence and responsibility?

A: The story suggests that independence requires individuals to develop the skills necessary to meet their own needs. The young seagull depends on his family for food and support because he has not learned to fly. His parents encourage him to overcome this dependence by learning to fly like his siblings. Once he succeeds, he gains greater confidence and begins participating in the family's activities. The story shows that independence develops when individuals take responsibility for learning essential skills and overcoming their limitations.
