Practice set of exam-pattern questions from Minerals and Energy Resources. Read each question, frame your answer, then tap it to compare against the structured answer below.

Q: Define mineral.

A: A mineral is a homogenous, naturally occurring substance with a definable internal structure.

Q: Name the hardest and softest mineral.

A: The hardest mineral is diamond and the softest mineral is talc.

Q: Name the iron ore which is the most important industrial iron ore in terms of the quantity used.

A: Hematite is the most important industrial iron ore in terms of the quantity used.

Q: How do minerals occur in sedimentary rocks?

A: In sedimentary rocks, minerals occur in beds or layers. They have been formed as a result of deposition, accumulation and concentration in horizontal strata.

Q: Name any two minerals formed due to evaporation, especially in arid regions.

A: Gypsum, potash salt and sodium salt are formed due to evaporation, especially in arid regions.

Q: Explain three factors that make mineral extraction commercially viable.

A: The concentration of the mineral in the ore decides how much metal can be extracted, the ease of extraction decides the effort and cost involved, and closeness to the market decides transport cost — together these three factors make mineral extraction commercially viable.

Q: Differentiate between ferrous and non-ferrous minerals.

A: Ferrous minerals contain iron and help in the development of metallurgical industries, with examples like iron ore and manganese. Non-ferrous minerals do not contain iron and play an important role in a number of engineering and electrical industries, with examples like lead, zinc and gold.

Q: Which three places are connected with the HVJ gas pipeline?

A: Hazira, Vijaipur and Jagdishpur are connected by the HVJ gas pipeline.

Q: Explain the reason why minerals are found in varied forms.

A: A particular mineral that will be formed from a certain combination of elements depends upon the physical and chemical conditions under which the material forms, so the same elements can crystallise into different minerals under different conditions.

Q: What are placer deposits? Give examples of minerals found in such deposits.

A: Certain minerals may occur as alluvial deposits in the sands of valley floors and the base of hills, and these deposits are called placer deposits. Gold, silver, tin and platinum are found in such deposits.

Q: Distinguish between metallic minerals and non-metallic minerals.

A: Metallic minerals provide metal after processing, such as iron, copper and aluminium: they are often hard, generally associated with igneous rocks, have a lustre or shine of their own, and are ductile and malleable. Non-metallic minerals do not provide metals, such as mica, potash, coal and limestone: they are not so hard, are generally associated with sedimentary rocks, have no shine, and are not ductile and malleable.

Q: Give one point of difference between magnetite and hematite.

A: Magnetite is the finest iron ore with up to 70 per cent iron content, while hematite has a slightly lower iron content of 50 to 60 per cent.

Q: Describe any three characteristics of the Odisha-Jharkhand belt of iron ore in India.

A: In Odisha, high-grade hematite ore is found, occurring in the Badampahar mines in the Mayurbhanj and Kendujhar districts. In the adjoining Singhbhum district of Jharkhand, hematite ore is mined in Gua and Noamundi.

Q: Mention any four uses of manganese ore.

A: Manganese ore is used in the manufacturing of steel and ferromanganese alloys, and in the manufacture of bleaching powder, insecticides and paints.

Q: Swamy visited a coal field at Neyveli with his classmates as a school project. Name the type of coal found there.

A: Lignite coal is found at Neyveli.

Q: How is bauxite formed? Give its uses. Discuss its distribution in India.

A: **Formation:** bauxite deposits are formed by the decomposition of a wide variety of rocks rich in aluminium silicates. **Uses:** bauxite is a clay-like substance from which alumina and later aluminium are obtained, and aluminium is an important metal because it combines the strength of metals such as iron with extreme lightness and also with good conductivity and great malleability. **Distribution:** bauxite is found in the Amarkantak plateau, Maikal hills and the plateau region of Bilaspur-Katni, and Odisha is the largest bauxite-producing state in India.

Q: Why is mica considered the most important mineral in electrical and electronic industries?

A: Mica is considered the most important mineral in electrical and electronic industries because of its excellent dielectric strength, low power loss factor, insulating properties and resistance to high voltage.

Q: How has mining become a problem for workers and the environment? OR Analyse the impact of mining activities on the local environment and the health of the surrounding communities.

A: **Problems for miners:** the miners have to work under tough conditions where no natural light is available, there is always a risk of collapse of the mine roof, inundation with water and fire, and miners are at great risk of getting afflicted with pulmonary disorders. **Environmental damage:** the water sources in the region get contaminated due to mining, and dumping of waste and slurry leads to degradation of land and soil and an increase in stream and river pollution.

Q: What are the two main ways of generating electricity? How are they different from each other?

A: The two main ways of generating electricity are hydroelectricity and thermal electricity. Thermal electricity is obtained by using coal, petroleum and natural gas, is a non-renewable resource, causes pollution and is expensive in the long run. Hydroelectricity is produced from water, is renewable, does not cause pollution and is cheaper in the long run.

Q: Why should we conserve minerals? Suggest three ways to conserve minerals.

A: **Why conserve:** industry and agriculture strongly depend upon mineral deposits and the substances manufactured from them, the geological processes of mineral formation are very slow, mineral resources are finite and non-renewable, and continued extraction of ores leads to increasing costs as minerals are extracted from greater depths along with a decrease in quality. **Ways to conserve:** make a concerted effort to use mineral resources in a planned and sustainable manner, constantly evolve improved technologies to allow the use of low-grade ores at low costs, and recycle metals using scrap metals and other substitutes.

Q: Explain the difference between conventional and non-conventional sources of energy. Name the two most common conventional resources used in rural India. Why is the use of these conventional sources discouraged?

A: Conventional sources are the traditional sources of energy produced from coal, petroleum and natural gas: they are non-renewable, their generation is expensive, they pollute the atmosphere on a large scale, and examples are coal, petroleum and natural gas. Non-conventional sources are developed recently from the sun, wind and tide: they are renewable, their generation is cheaper, they are pollution-free or low-pollution sources, and examples are solar energy, wind energy and tidal power. Firewood and cattle dung cake are the most common conventional resources in rural India, meeting more than 70 per cent of rural household energy needs. Continual use of firewood is increasingly difficult due to decreasing forest area, and using dung cake is discouraged because it consumes the most valuable manure which could be used in agriculture.

Q: Which minerals are used to obtain nuclear energy? How is nuclear energy obtained? Mention the names of the six nuclear power stations in India.

A: Uranium and thorium are used to obtain nuclear energy. Nuclear energy is obtained by altering the structure of atoms: when such an alteration is made, much energy is released in the form of heat, and this is used to generate electric power. The six nuclear power stations are Narora in Uttar Pradesh, Rawat Bhata in Rajasthan, Kakrapara in Gujarat, Tarapur in Maharashtra, Kaiga in Karnataka and Kalpakkam in Tamil Nadu.

Q: How can solar energy solve the energy problem to some extent in India?

A: India is a tropical country and therefore receives sunlight in abundance throughout the year, and solar plants can be easily established in rural and remote areas. Solar energy will minimise the dependence of rural households on firewood and dung cakes, which in turn will contribute to environmental conservation and an adequate quantity of manure.

Q: Define geothermal energy. Also mention two projects which have been set up in India to harness geothermal energy.

A: Geothermal energy refers to the heat and electricity produced by using the heat from the interior of the Earth, and it exists because the Earth grows progressively hotter with increasing depth. Two experimental projects have been set up in India: one is located in the Parvati valley near Manikarn in Himachal Pradesh, and the other is located in the Puga valley in Ladakh.

Q: There is a pressing need to use non-conventional energy sources in India. Analyse the statement with reasons. OR Non-conventional resources are the best option to conserve the natural resources. Substantiate this statement with examples.

A: Because of the growing consumption of energy, the country is becoming increasingly dependent on fossil fuels. Rising prices of oil and gas and their potential shortages have raised uncertainties about the security of the energy supply in the future, and these can have serious repercussions on the growth of the national economy. Increasing the use of fossil fuels also causes environmental problems, so non-conventional sources like solar, wind, tidal and biogas energy are the best option to conserve natural resources.

Q: Name the most abundantly available fossil fuel in India. Evaluate the importance of its different forms.

A: The most abundantly available fossil fuel in India is coal. **Peat** has low carbon and high moisture content and low heating capacity. **Lignite** is a low-grade brown coal that is soft with high moisture content and is used for generating electricity. **Bituminous** is the most popular coal for commercial use and has special value for smelting iron in blast furnaces. **Anthracite** is the highest quality hard coal.

Q: Highlight the importance of petroleum. Explain the occurrence of petroleum in India.

A: **Importance:** petroleum provides fuel for heat and lighting, lubricants for machinery and raw materials for a number of manufacturing industries, and petroleum refineries act as a nodal industry for synthetic textiles, fertilisers and numerous chemical industries. **Occurrence:** most petroleum occurrences in India are associated with anticlines and fault traps in the rock formations of the tertiary age; in regions of folding, anticlines or domes, oil occurs where it is trapped in the crest of the upfold, and petroleum is also found in fault traps between porous and non-porous rocks.

Q: Which are the potential sources of biogas? State any four benefits of biogas.

A: **Sources:** shrubs, farm waste, animal waste and human waste. **Benefits:** biogas has high thermal efficiency in comparison to kerosene, dung cake and charcoal, it burns without smoke causing no pollution, it is the most efficient use of cattle dung, and it improves the quality of manure and prevents the loss of trees and manure due to the burning of fuel wood and cow dung cakes.

Q: Gobar gas plants in rural India provide which twin benefits to the farmers?

A: Gobar gas plants provide energy and improved quality of manure — the twin benefits to farmers.

Q: Distinguish between natural gas and biogas.

A: Natural gas is a conventional source of energy used as a raw material in the petrochemical industry and mainly in urban areas: it is non-renewable and not environmentally friendly. Biogas is a non-conventional source obtained from the decomposition of shrubs, farm waste and animal and human wastes, used as fuel and for lighting homes mainly in rural areas: it is renewable and environmentally friendly.

Q: Explain the need to adopt a cautious approach for the judicious use of our limited energy resources.

A: Energy is a basic requirement for economic development, since every sector of the national economy like agriculture, industry, transport, commercial and domestic needs inputs of energy. India is presently one of the least energy-efficient countries in the world, so there is an urgent need to develop a sustainable path of energy development. Various development plans require increasing amounts of energy to remain operational, and the success of economic development plans depends on energy resources.

Q: Suggest ways to conserve energy resources in India.

A: Adopt a cautious approach to the judicious use of our limited energy resources, use public transport systems instead of individual vehicles, switch off electricity when not in use, use power-saving devices, and use non-conventional sources of energy.

Q: How is tidal energy generated?

A: Floodgate dams are built across inlets: the water flows into the inlet during high tide and gets trapped when the gate is closed. Once the tide recedes, the gates are opened so that water can flow back to the sea or ocean, and the flow of water is used to run the turbine to generate electricity.

Q: Which are the two planks of sustainable energy?

A: Promotion of energy conservation and increased use of renewable energy resources are the two planks of sustainable energy.
