Q: Manufacturing sector is considered the backbone of general and economic development. Examine the statement in the context of India.

A: Manufacturing industries help in modernising agriculture and reduce the heavy dependence of people on agricultural income by providing jobs in secondary and tertiary sectors. They help eradicate unemployment and poverty, reduce regional disparities by establishing industries in tribal and backward areas, and expand trade and commerce through exports of manufactured goods that bring foreign exchange. Countries with large numbers of manufacturing industries are prosperous.

Q: Agricultural sector and manufacturing sector move hand in hand. Justify with suitable examples. OR Explain with examples how industries give a boost to the agriculture sector.

A: Agriculture and manufacturing are not exclusive of each other but quite interconnected. Agro-industries in India have given a major boost to agriculture by raising its productivity and efficiency: they add value to agricultural produce, intermediates and residues by processing, improving storability or linking the farm to the market. Industries depend on agriculture for raw materials and sell farmers irrigation pumps, fertilisers, insecticides, pesticides, PVC pipes, machines and tools. So the development and competitiveness of manufacturing industries have not only helped farmers increase production but also made production processes very efficient and far better.

Q: Industrialisation and urbanisation go hand in hand. Validate the statement.

A: After an industrial activity starts in a town, urbanisation follows: the industry provides employment, population migrates from rural hinterlands to seek jobs, and housing and transport facilities are developed to accommodate these people. Other infrastructural developments lead to the growth of the town into a city. Sometimes industries locate in or near cities, since cities provide markets and services such as banking, insurance, transport, labour, consultants and financial advice. Thus industrialisation and urbanisation go hand in hand.

Q: What are agglomeration economies?

A: When many industries tend to come together to make use of the advantages offered by an urban centre, like banking, insurance, transport, labour, consultants and financial advice, it is known as agglomeration economies.

Q: Describe the various physical and human factors responsible for the location of industries.

A: **Physical factors:** availability of raw materials, most important for heavy industries like iron and steel and cement; availability of power resources, since proximity to coal and electricity attracts more industries; availability of water, since almost all industries require huge quantities of it; and availability of a favourable climate. **Human factors:** availability of skilled and unskilled labourers; proximity to market, which reduces transport costs and delays; infrastructural facilities like banking, transport and communication; and government policy, since tax benefits and subsidies on inputs play an important role in industrial location.

Q: Determining an ideal industrial location is a challenging undertaking. Examine the statement and elucidate.

A: Industrial locations are complex, influenced by the availability of raw materials, labour, capital, power and market. Manufacturing locates where all these factors are either available or can be arranged at lower cost, which is the key determining factor. Industrialisation and urbanisation go hand in hand, with cities providing markets and services like banking, insurance, transport, labour and consultants. Before 1947, most industries were located near ports for overseas trade, and government policies and specialised labour also influence industrial location.

Q: Classify industries on the basis of the source of raw materials.

A: On the basis of raw materials, industries are agro-based industries like cotton, woollen, jute and silk, and mineral-based industries like iron and steel, cement and aluminium.

Q: Classify industries on the basis of their main role. How are they different from each other?

A: Basic or key industries supply their products as raw materials to manufacture other goods, such as the iron and steel industry, copper smelting and aluminium smelting. Consumer industries produce goods for direct use by consumers, such as sugar, paper, toothpaste and fans.

Q: What are cooperative sector industries? Which industry, due to its seasonal nature, is ideally suited to the cooperative sector?

A: Cooperative sector industries are owned and operated by the producers or suppliers of raw materials, workers or both, who pool resources and share profits or losses proportionately. The sugar industry, due to its seasonal nature, is ideally suited to the cooperative sector.

Q: Classify industries on the basis of ownership.

A: Public sector industries are owned and operated by government agencies, for example BHEL and SAIL. Private sector industries are owned and operated by individuals or a group of individuals, for example TISCO, Bajaj Auto Ltd. and Dabur Industries. Joint sector industries are jointly run by the state and individuals, for example Oil India Limited. Cooperative sector industries are owned and operated by producers, suppliers or workers sharing profits or losses proportionately, for example the sugar and coir industries.

Q: Why is aluminium smelting the second most important metallurgical industry in India? Explain.

A: Aluminium is light, resistant to corrosion, a good conductor of heat, malleable, and becomes strong when mixed with other metals. It is used to manufacture aircraft, utensils and wires, and it has gained popularity as a substitute for steel, copper, zinc and lead in a number of industries.

Q: Name the two important requirements of the aluminium smelting industry.

A: An assured source of raw material (bauxite) and a regular supply of electricity are the two important requirements of the aluminium smelting industry.

Q: India does not have any reserves of which significant fertiliser element used in the fertiliser industry, which is entirely imported?

A: Potash, or potassium compounds, are entirely imported since India has no reserves of them.

Q: Namit's elder sister is employed in the IT industry in the electronic capital of India. Name the city.

A: Bengaluru is the electronic capital of India.

Q: Why is copper mainly used in electrical cables and electronic industries?

A: Copper is used because it is malleable and ductile and a good conductor of heat and electricity.

Q: Examine the multi-pronged aspects of the Information Technology and Electronics industry.

A: The electronics industry covers products from transistor sets to television, telephones, cellular telecom, telephone exchanges, radars and computers. Bengaluru has emerged as the electronic capital of India, with other centres at Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Kolkata, Lucknow and Coimbatore. Eighteen software technology parks provide single-window service and high data communication facilities to software experts. A major impact has been employment generation, with 30 per cent of employees being women. The industry has been a major foreign exchange earner through the fast-growing Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) sector, and continuing growth in hardware and software is the key to the success of the IT industry in India.

Q: Describe how industries are responsible for environmental degradation.

A: **Air pollution:** a high proportion of carbon dioxide, sulphur dioxide and carbon monoxide creates air pollution, and suspended particulate matter adds to the problem. **Water pollution:** organic and inorganic industrial wastes and effluents pollute water, with paper, pulp, chemicals, textiles, dyeing and petroleum refineries as the main culprits. **Thermal pollution:** hot water from factories and thermal plants is drained into rivers and ponds before cooling. **Radioactive waste:** wastes from nuclear power plants cause cancer, birth defects and miscarriages. **Noise pollution:** results in irritation, hypertension and hearing impairment.

Q: Suggest ways through which the problem of environmental degradation by industries can be resolved. OR Environmental degradation has been everywhere. Explain the ways to prevent environmental degradation.

A: Water should be reused and recycled in industry to minimise fresh water use, rainwater harvesting should be promoted, and hot water and effluents should be treated before release into rivers and ponds. Overdrawing of groundwater needs legal regulation. Particulate matter can be reduced by fitting smoke stacks with electrostatic precipitators and fabric filters, smoke can be reduced by using oil or gas instead of coal, and machinery and generators should be fitted with silencers.

Q: The challenge of sustainable development requires control over industrial pollution. Substantiate the statement with examples.

A: Industries drive extensive growth and expansion but also cause environmental degeneration through air and water pollution, so a sustainable model is increasingly required. Industries must produce eco-friendly products and dump waste responsibly, and the latest technology can help control pollution towards sustainable operation. Industries can follow the reuse-recycle-refuse approach, and where necessary dump treated waste in specified places away from land and water sources, making it useful for other than drinking purposes, and stop using thermal plants in some locations.

Q: What steps have been taken by NTPC toward environmental protection?

A: NTPC has adopted optimum utilisation of equipment with the latest techniques and upgrades, minimised waste generation by maximising ash utilisation, and provided green belts for ecological balance with afforestation. It reduces pollution through ash pond management, ash water recycling and liquid waste management, with ecological monitoring, reviews and online database management for all its power stations.
