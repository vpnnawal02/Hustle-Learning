Q: Define resources.

A: Everything available in our environment which can be used to satisfy our needs, provided it is technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable, can be termed as a resource.

Q: Explain the classification of resources on the basis of origin.

A: On the basis of origin, resources are classified as biotic resources, which are obtained from the biosphere and have life, and abiotic resources, which are composed of non-living things.

Q: Classify the resources on the basis of exhaustibility.

A: On the basis of exhaustibility, resources are classified as renewable resources, which can be renewed or reproduced, and non-renewable resources, which occur over a very long geological time and may get exhausted.

Q: Provide a suitable classification of resources on the basis of ownership.

A: On the basis of ownership, resources are classified as individual resources owned privately by individuals, community-owned resources accessible to all members of a community, national resources belonging to the nation, and international resources regulated by international institutions.

Q: Examine the major problems created as a result of the indiscriminate utilisation of natural resources. How can this situation be averted?

A: **Problems:** depletion of resources at a faster rate for satisfying the greed of a few individuals, accumulation of resources in a few hands dividing society into haves and have-nots or rich and poor, and an increase in global ecological crises such as global warming, ozone layer depletion, environmental pollution and land degradation. **Ways to avert:** judicious use of resources keeping in mind the needs of the present and future, equal distribution and sharing of resources so that everyone gets the desired benefits, proper resource planning, and diversion towards the use of non-conventional sources.

Q: What is Agenda 21? List its two principles.

A: Agenda 21 is the declaration signed by world leaders in 1992 at the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED) held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, and it aims at achieving global sustainable development. Its two principles are to combat environmental damage, poverty and disease through global cooperation on common interests, mutual needs and shared responsibilities, and that every local government should draw its own local Agenda 21.

Q: Why is resource planning essential? OR What is the need of doing resource planning in a country like India?

A: Resource planning is a widely accepted strategy for the judicious use of resources. Resources in India are not evenly distributed: some parts are rich in one resource but deficient in others — for example, Rajasthan is rich in solar and wind energy but lacks water resources, while Jharkhand is rich in minerals and coal deposits but lacks industrialisation. Most resources in our environment are limited, so if they are not preserved or used rationally we will be in great trouble. Resource planning also reduces regional disparity, saves for future generations and avoids wastage or over-utilisation of resources.

Q: What are the three stages of resource planning?

A: The three stages are identification and inventory of resources across the regions of the country, involving surveying, mapping and qualitative and quantitative estimation and measurement; evolving a planning structure endowed with appropriate technology, skill and institutional setup for implementing resource development plans; and matching the resource development plans with overall national development plans.

Q: Write any three human activities which are responsible for land degradation in India.

A: Mining is a major cause, since the expansion of mining and quarrying has caused severe land degradation with deforestation in Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha. Over-grazing is one of the main reasons for land degradation in Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra. Over-irrigation in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh causes land degradation through waterlogging leading to increased salinity and alkalinity in the soil. In addition, mineral processing like grinding limestone for cement and calcite and soapstone for ceramics generates huge quantities of dust, which settles on land and retards the infiltration of water into the soil.

Q: Suggest and explain ways to protect the land from degradation in various states of India. OR Describe any three measures of controlling land degradation.

A: Afforestation and proper management of grazing, planting of shelter belts of plants with control over grazing, and stabilisation of sand dunes by growing thorny bushes protect the land. Further, mining activities should be controlled with proper management of wastelands, and industrial effluents and wastes should be properly discharged and disposed of after treatment.

Q: Explain the various purposes of land use.

A: Land resources are used for forests; for land not available for cultivation, namely barren and wasteland and land put to non-agricultural uses like buildings, roads and factories; for other uncultivated lands excluding fallow land, namely permanent pastures and grazing land, land under miscellaneous tree crops and groves, and culturable wasteland left uncultivated for more than five agricultural years; for fallow lands, namely current fallow left without cultivation for one or less than one agricultural year and fallow other than current fallow left uncultivated for one to five agricultural years; and for gross cropped area, which is the area sown more than once in an agricultural year plus the net sown area.

Q: What type of soil is found in the river deltas of the eastern coast? Give four main features of this type of soil.

A: Alluvial soil is found in the river deltas of the eastern coast and across the entire northern plain, and it is the most widely spread soil in India. It is formed by the sediments deposited by river water and consists of various proportions of sand, silt and clay. It is very fertile, containing an adequate proportion of potash, phosphoric acid and lime, and it is ideal for sugarcane, paddy, wheat and other cereals. Due to its high fertility, regions of alluvial soils are intensively cultivated and densely populated.

Q: Classify alluvial soil on the basis of age and differentiate between them. OR Distinguish between Khadar and Bangar soil.

A: According to age, alluvial soils are classified as old alluvial (Bangar) and new alluvial (Khadar). Khadar is a new alluvial soil with a lower concentration of kankar nodules, more fine particles and greater fertility. Bangar is an old alluvial soil with a higher concentration of kankar nodules, fewer fine particles and lower fertility.

Q: Write the features of regur soil.

A: Regur soil, also known as black soil, is made up of extremely fine clayey material well known for its capacity to hold moisture. It is rich in soil nutrients such as calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash and lime, but generally poor in phosphoric content. These soils develop deep cracks during hot weather, which helps in the proper aeration of the soil, and the soil is ideal for growing cotton, so it is also known as black cotton soil.

Q: Distinguish between red and laterite soils.

A: Red soil is formed due to the weathering of igneous and metamorphic rocks and develops in areas of low rainfall; it is red in colour due to the presence of iron, is less crystalline, and is highly porous and less fertile, though fertile where deep. Laterite soil is formed by the leaching process and develops in areas of high rainfall; it is red in colour due to little clay and much gravel of red sandstones, is crystalline, and is less fertile with only grass growing in abundance.

Q: Define soil erosion. Explain the two types of soil erosion mostly observed in India. What are the causes of soil erosion in India?

A: Soil erosion is the displacement of the upper layer of soil by agents such as water, wind and human activities, leading to loss of fertile land, reduced crop yields and increased risk of landslides and flooding. **Types:** in gully erosion, running water cuts through clayey soil and makes deep channels or gullies, and the resulting unfit land is called bad land or ravines; in sheet erosion, water flows as a sheet over large areas down a slope and the topsoil is washed away. **Causes:** human activities like deforestation, over-grazing, construction and mining; natural forces like wind, glaciers and water, including wind erosion of loose soil off flat or sloping land; and defective farming methods such as ploughing up and down the slope, which forms channels for the quick flow of water.

Q: What are the factors that have contributed to the development of various types of soils in India? Suggest any three methods through which soil erosion can be prevented. OR Suggest any three methods of soil conservation suitable to Indian conditions. OR What steps can be taken to control soil erosion in hilly areas?

A: India has varied relief features, landforms, climatic regimes and vegetation types, which have contributed to the development of various types of soils. **Prevention measures:** contour ploughing along contour lines decreases the speed of water flow down slopes; terrace cultivation in steps on slopes restricts erosion, as developed in the Western and Central Himalayas; strip cropping divides large fields into strips with grass grown between crops to break the force of the wind; and shelter belts of rows of trees have stabilised sand dunes in the desert of western India.
