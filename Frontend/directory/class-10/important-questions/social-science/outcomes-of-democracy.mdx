Practice set of exam-pattern questions from Outcomes of Democracy. Read each question, frame your answer, then tap it to compare against the structured answer below.

Q: If a government provides its citizens a right and means to examine the process of decision-making, it is blank.

A: If a government provides its citizens a right and means to examine the process of decision-making, it is a transparent government.

Q: More than economic development, it is reasonable to expect democracies to reduce economic blank.

A: More than economic development, it is reasonable to expect democracies to reduce economic disparities.

Q: Explain the common features of democracies.

A: **Regular elections:** in a democratic government, general elections are held to elect a new government. **Right to vote:** all citizens of and above a certain age have the right to vote. **Political parties:** parties are an essential part of democracy and put up candidates who contest elections. **Rule of law:** law is supreme and all citizens are equal in the eyes of the law, with no one above it.

Q: What should be the most basic outcome of democracy?

A: The most basic outcomes of democracy should be an accountable government and a responsive government.

Q: Democracy is based on the idea of deliberation and negotiation. Examine the statement.

A: Democracy gives importance to deliberation and public opinion, and a democratic government takes more time to follow procedures before arriving at a decision. Its decisions may be more acceptable to the people and more effective, since decision-making is based on norms and procedures with transparency — persons have the right and means to examine the process. It follows procedures, is accountable to the people, develops mechanisms for citizens to hold it accountable, and enables citizens to take part in decision-making whenever they think fit.

Q: What is meant by transparency?

A: The right or means to examine the process of decision-making is called transparency.

Q: A democratic government is efficient and effective. Analyse the statement. OR The cost of time that democracy pays is perhaps worth it. Justify.

A: Imagine a government that takes decisions very fast but takes decisions not accepted by the people, and therefore faces problems. In contrast, a democratic government takes more time to follow procedures before arriving at a decision. However, because it has followed procedure, its decisions may be more acceptable to the people and more effective — so the cost of time that democracy pays is perhaps worth it.

Q: What are the different ways in which a democracy ensures accountable, responsive and legitimate government?

A: A democratic government develops mechanisms for citizens to take part in decision-making: free and fair elections, open debate on major policies and legislation, and the right to information exist in good democracies, making them accountable governments. Democracy is responsive to the needs and expectations of citizens and makes policies for their welfare. It is legitimate because it is elected by the people, and people wish to be ruled by representatives elected by them.

Q: Why is a democratic government referred to as a legitimate government?

A: A democratic government is legitimate because it is the people's own government, there is great popular support for democracy all over the world, people wish to be ruled by representatives elected by them, and the government is attentive to the needs and demands of the people.

Q: Democracy's ability to generate its own support is itself an outcome that cannot be ignored. Support the statement with examples.

A: Democracy ensures decision-making based on norms and procedures, and every citizen has the right and means to examine the process. Democratic governments are accountable, legitimate and transparent; people have the right to choose their rulers and the right to information about the government and its functioning. A democratic government is the people's own government, run by the people.

Q: How can we measure democracy based on expected outcomes?

A: We observe practices and institutions: there should be free and fair elections, open criticism and debate on major policies and legislation, and citizens should have the right to information about the functioning of government.

Q: What is the relationship between democracy and the economic growth of a country?

A: During the last fifty years, dictatorships have shown a slightly higher rate of economic growth compared to democracies. But many other factors determine economic growth, like population size, the global situation, cooperation from other countries and economic priorities adopted by the country. So even with a nominal difference in growth rates, it is better to prefer democracy, since it has other positive outcomes like the dignity and freedom of citizens.

Q: Explain the relationship between democracy and development.

A: Democracies are expected to produce development, but development depends upon many factors like population size, the global situation and cooperation from other countries. In democracies, time is taken to discuss and reach a decision, so it is slow — but it is not unjust or inappropriate.

Q: There is overwhelming support for the idea of democracy in South Asia. Support the statement with examples.

A: Democratic government is the people's own government, and South Asian countries want democratic rights, to elect their own representatives, and the dignity, freedom and accommodation of diversity that democracy provides, since democracy rests on discussion and negotiation — for example in India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Pakistan.

Q: Differentiate between democratic and non-democratic government.

A: Democratic governments are transparent, legitimate and accountable, whereas non-democratic governments are selected and formed at their own discretion. Democratic governments provide dignity and freedom to all without discrimination, resolve conflicts through debate, discussion and negotiation, and rest on minority-majority cooperation, enhancing the dignity of all.

Q: How is democracy a better form of government when compared with dictatorship or any other alternative government?

A: Democracy promotes equality among citizens, enhances the dignity of the individual, improves the quality of decision-making, provides a method to resolve conflicts, and allows room to correct mistakes.

Q: Are democracies based on political and economic equality? Explain. OR Does democracy lead to a reduction of inequality and poverty? Explain.

A: Democracies are based on political equality, with all individuals having an equal role in electing representatives. However, alongside political inclusion we find growing economic inequalities: a small number of ultra-rich enjoy a highly disproportionate share of wealth and incomes while the poor struggle for food, shelter and clothing. The poor form a high proportion of voters whom no party wants to lose, yet democratically elected governments do not appear keen to address poverty as people may expect.

Q: Democracy accommodates social diversities. Support the statement with examples.

A: Democracies develop procedures to conduct competition, reducing the possibility of tensions turning explosive or violent. No society can fully and permanently resolve group conflicts, but we can learn to respect differences and evolve mechanisms to negotiate them. The ability to handle social differences, divisions and conflicts is a definite plus of democratic regimes — for example, Belgium has successfully negotiated differences among its ethnic population, reducing tensions.

Q: Do democracies lead to peaceful and harmonious life among citizens? Clarify.

A: Non-democratic regimes often ignore or suppress internal social differences, while handling such differences is a definite plus of democracies. But Sri Lanka's example shows democracy must fulfil two conditions for this outcome: democracy is not simply rule by majority opinion, since the majority must work with the minority so governments represent the general view; and rule by the majority must not become rule by the majority community in religion, race or language.

Q: What are the two conditions necessary to ensure that democracy accommodates social diversity?

A: The majority must work with the minority so that governments function to represent the general view rather than mere majority opinion, and rule by the majority must not become rule by the majority community in religion, race or linguistic group. Rule by majority means that for every decision or election, different persons and groups may and can form a majority.

Q: Respect and equal treatment of women are necessary ingredients of a democratic society. Examine the statement.

A: Economic independence or access to inherited or self-generated income is a major means of empowering women, along with equal political and civil rights, autonomy and freedom, and distributive justice to reduce inequalities in resources and power. Love, universal brotherhood, sincerity, honesty, integrity of character and a firm attitude of rendering help matter, as does removing social evils including violence, torture and humiliation.

Q: How does democracy assure the dignity of women and prevent caste inequalities?

A: Most societies were historically male-dominated, but long struggles by women have created sensitivity that respect for and equal treatment of women are necessary ingredients of a democratic society. Women are still not always treated with respect, yet once the democratic principle is recognised, it becomes easier to struggle against what is now unacceptable legally and morally. Democracy in India has strengthened the claim of disadvantaged and discriminated castes for equal status and opportunity, and though caste-based inequalities and atrocities persist, they lack moral and legal foundations.

Q: Explain any four areas where democracy has failed.

A: Many democracies did not produce the expected development, and the inability to achieve higher economic development worries us. Even with economic growth, wealth is not distributed so that all citizens share a better life — the bitter truth of persistent disparities. Democracy has failed on poverty reduction, with a small ultra-rich minority holding a highly disproportionate share while those at the bottom have very little. Democracies often frustrate people's needs, ignore majority demands, and routine talk of corruption shows democracy is not free of evil.

Q: What are the reasonable outcomes that one may expect from democracy?

A: An accountable, responsive and legitimate government; accommodation of social diversity with peace and harmony; and promotion of the dignity and freedom of the individual.

Q: Constant complaints by people can be seen as a testimony to the success of democracy. Explain.

A: Complaints highlight people's awareness and their expectations of the system in place. They show citizens are not overawed by those in power and can objectively examine the difference made, and they measure participation in public debates. Public dissatisfaction with democracy shows the success of the democratic project, proving people have transformed from subjects into citizens.

Q: A think tank must design an outline to measure how successful democracy has been in a country. Discuss the key indicators and why they are crucial.

A: **Free and fair elections** represent a healthy democratic process. **Citizens' right to information** matters, since only informed citizens can hold the government accountable. **Protection of minority rights** shows commitment to democratic principles by enabling minorities to join decision-making. **Poverty reduction** over the years indicates successful implementation of democracy. **Rule of law** through institutions like an independent judiciary upholds fairness, justice and equal treatment.
