Q: Define the term federalism.

A: Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and various constituent units of the country. Both levels of government, the central and the state, enjoy their powers independently of each other.

Q: How is a federal government different from a unitary form of government? Why are federations preferred these days? OR Distinguish between the unitary and federal systems of government.

A: A unitary government has only one level of government, its sub-units are subordinate to the centre, and they are answerable to the central government. A federal government has two or more levels, the central government cannot order the state government about, and both are separately answerable to the people rather than to each other. A federation is preferred because it makes administration effective and efficient and helps accommodate all diverse groups.

Q: State the main features of federalism.

A: There are two or more levels of government, and different tiers govern the same citizens with each tier's own jurisdiction in legislation, taxation and administration. The powers and functions of each tier are specified and guaranteed by the Constitution, and fundamental provisions cannot be unilaterally changed by one level — changes need both levels' consent. Revenue sources of different levels are specified by the Constitution, and courts interpret the Constitution and the powers of each level, with the highest court acting as umpire in disputes.

Q: Differentiate between the Coming Together Federation and the Holding Together Federation with examples.

A: Coming Together federations form when independent states join to make a bigger state: state governments are strong compared to the centre, all states have equal power, and examples are the USA, Switzerland and Australia. Holding Together federations form when a large country divides itself into sub-units: the central government is strong compared to the states, states very often have unequal powers, and examples are India, Spain and Belgium.

Q: Which provisions of the Indian Constitution make India a full-fledged federation? OR Mention any five main features which make India a federal country.

A: **Two or more levels:** India has three levels — Centre, State and Local. **Three lists:** powers divide through the Union, State and Concurrent lists. **Rigid Constitution:** fundamental provisions cannot be changed unilaterally by one level. **Financial autonomy:** revenue sources of Centre and States are clearly defined. **Independent judiciary:** the Supreme Court settles disputes between levels of government.

Q: Explain the process of power-sharing among different organs of the government in India. OR Describe the threefold distribution of legislative powers and who legislates on leftover subjects. OR Describe the division of powers between the Central and State Governments.

A: The Constitution gives a threefold distribution: the Union List has subjects of national importance like foreign affairs and defence, on which only the Union Government legislates; the State List has state and local subjects like police and trade, on which only States legislate; and the Concurrent List has common-interest subjects like education, forests and agriculture, on which both can legislate, with Union law prevailing in disputes. Subjects outside these lists, like computer software that emerged later, are called residuary subjects, on which the Union Government legislates.

Q: Indian Constitution has a unitary bias. Support the statement with examples.

A: Power-sharing between Union and States is federal, but the system turns unitary since residuary powers rest with the Union. The Constitution never uses the word Federation, yet divides powers into a three-tier system of central, state and local governments. Each level has its own legislative jurisdiction, but ours is a centralised federation where the Union carries more powers — it has 97 subjects while the State and Concurrent lists have fewer.

Q: Independence of the judiciary is the key to the success of federalism. Examine the statement.

A: The judiciary, with the Supreme Court at the apex, is the sole interpreter of the Indian Constitution. It oversees implementation of constitutional provisions and procedures, and in any dispute about division of powers the High Courts and the Supreme Court decide.

Q: Why has federalism succeeded in India? Which policies adopted by India ensured this success? Explain.

A: Federalism succeeded because of the nature of democratic politics, which made the spirit of federalism, respect for diversity and desire for living together shared ideals. **Linguistic states:** old states vanished and new ones formed so same-language people live together, with some states recognising culture, ethnicity or geography. **Language policy:** no national language was imposed; Hindi is the official language while 21 other Scheduled Languages are recognised. **Centre-State relations:** after 1990, regional parties and coalition governments created power-sharing and respect for state autonomy. **Decentralisation:** the 1992 amendment strengthened the third tier of Panchayats and municipalities.

Q: Write a brief note on the language policy adopted in India. OR Our Constitution has taken measures to safeguard different languages. Explain.

A: No language was given national-language status; Hindi was identified as the official language, though only about 40 per cent of Indians have Hindi as mother tongue. Besides Hindi, 21 other languages are recognised as Scheduled Languages, and candidates for central government examinations may opt for any of them. States have their own official languages, and government work takes place in the state's official language.

Q: What is decentralisation in democracy? What are the advantages of decentralisation in democracy?

A: When power is taken away from central and state governments and given to local government, it is called decentralisation. Its advantages are that local problems are best settled locally by people with better knowledge of them, people know better where to spend money and manage things efficiently, and direct participation in decision-making inculcates democratic habits.

Q: Explain the provisions made towards decentralisation in India after the 1992 Constitutional Amendment. OR How has the third tier been made more effective and powerful by the 1992 Amendment?

A: The Constitution mandates regular elections for local bodies; reserves seats and executive heads for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Other Backward Classes; reserves at least one-third of all positions for women; creates an independent State Election Commission in each state for panchayat and municipal elections; and requires state governments to share powers and revenue with local bodies.

Q: What is a Gram Sabha? Write the functions of a Gram Sabha.

A: All the voters in a village constitute a Gram Sabha. It approves the Gram Panchayat's annual budget, reviews the Panchayat's performance, and elects the members of the Gram Panchayat.

Q: Local governments have made a significant impact on Indian democracy. At the same time, there are many difficulties. Explain.

A: **Impact:** constitutional status for local government has deepened democracy and increased women's representation and voice. **Difficulties:** elections are not held regularly and enthusiastically, Gram Sabhas are not held regularly, most state governments have not transferred significant powers to local governments, and local governments lack adequate resources.
