Q: Which party is only allowed to rule in China?

A: Only the Communist Party is allowed to rule in China.

Q: What is a political party? What are the main components of political parties?

A: A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. They agree on policies and programmes for society to promote the collective good, persuade people that their policies are better than others, and seek to implement them by winning popular support through elections. Its main components are the leaders, the active members and the followers.

Q: Give one special feature that distinguishes a pressure group from a political party.

A: Pressure groups do not seek to get into power, while political parties do — this distinguishes them.

Q: Examine the role of political parties in a democratic country. OR Modern democracies cannot exist without political parties. Evaluate with relevant arguments. OR Political parties fill political offices and exercise political power by performing important functions. Explain any five of them.

A: Parties contest elections, put forward different policies and programmes for voters to choose from, play a decisive role in making laws, and form and run governments. Parties that lose play the opposition, voicing different views and criticising the government for its failures or wrong policies. Parties shape public opinion by raising and highlighting issues, and they provide people access to government machinery and welfare schemes.

Q: Explain the functions of the opposition parties.

A: They oppose the government by voicing different views, criticise it for failures and wrong policies, and mobilise opposition to it. Opposition parties safeguard the liberty and rights of people by monitoring the ruling party, keeping a check on its dictatorial behaviour.

Q: How do political parties shape public opinion?

A: They raise and highlight issues, form pressure groups as an extension, launch movements for resolving people's problems, and public opinion is often shaped by the stand parties take.

Q: The rise of political parties is directly linked to the emergence of representative democracy. Justify the statement.

A: As societies become large and complex, they need an agency to gather different views on issues and present them to the government, a way to bring representatives together to form a responsible government, and a mechanism to support or restrain the government, make policies and justify or oppose them. Political parties fulfil these needs of every representative government and are thus a necessary condition for democracy.

Q: Differentiate between one-party and two-party systems.

A: In a one-party system only one party is allowed to control and run the government, as in China where only the Communist Party rules — this is not a democratic option. In a two-party system only two main parties contest elections, as in the United States of America and the United Kingdom — this is a democratic option.

Q: Why is a one-party political system not considered a good option in a democratic system?

A: Voters are offered no choice at voting time, which is not democratic. Any democratic system must allow at least two parties to compete in elections and give competing parties a fair chance to come to power.

Q: What is a multiparty system? Give reasons why the multiparty system has evolved in India.

A: If several parties compete for power and more than two parties have a reasonable chance of coming to power on their own or in alliance, it is a multiparty system. It evolved in India because India's tremendous social and geographical diversity cannot be accommodated by two or three parties, and because a variety of interests and opinions thereby enjoy political representation.

Q: In a hypothetical democratic nation with a religiously homogenous population, should there still be multiple political parties? Justify in about 40 words.

A: Yes, because despite religious homogeneity, multiple parties help represent diverse political perspectives, help in policy formation, help avoid authoritarianism and provide voters an alternative.

Q: Analyse the role of a multiparty system in a democratic country like India.

A: Multiparty politics represents the diverse interests and aspirations of citizens, creating a competitive and dynamic environment where parties with different ideologies compete for the electorate. Multiple parties check any one party from becoming too powerful and dominant, let a variety of interests enjoy representation with a choice of candidates, and keep power from concentrating in one party — a system India adopted for its vast diversity and plurality.

Q: Why is it not possible for all countries to have the same party system, that is, single, two or multiparty?

A: A party system is not something any country can choose: it evolves over a long time depending on the nature of society, its social and regional divisions, its history of politics and its system of elections. Each country develops a party system conditioned by special circumstances, and no system is ideal for all countries and situations.

Q: What is meant by a national political party? State the conditions required to be one. How many national parties are there in India? Name them.

A: National political parties are country-wide parties with units in various states, all following the same policies, programmes and strategy decided at the national level. The conditions are securing at least 6 per cent of total votes in Lok Sabha or Assembly elections in four states, and winning at least four Lok Sabha seats. There were six recognised national parties in India in 2023: the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP), Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP), Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), Communist Party of India–Marxist (CPI-M), Indian National Congress (INC) and Nationalist Congress Party (NCP).

Q: What is meant by a regional political party? State the conditions required to be recognised as one.

A: A regional party is present in only some states and is commonly called a State party. The conditions are securing at least 6 per cent of total votes in a State Legislative Assembly election and winning at least two Assembly seats.

Q: Evaluate the significant distinction between national and regional parties.

A: National parties are powerful across the nation and deal with national issues, preferring national issues over regional problems. Regional parties are confined to a specific region or state, discussing only its issues and demands, with operations limited to the state.

Q: Write about the main features of the BJP.

A: **Founded:** 1980, tracing origins to the Bharatiya Jana Sangh formed by Syama Prasad Mukherjee in 1951. **Ideals:** a strong and modern India rooted in Indian culture, cultural nationalism (Hindutva), integral humanism and Antyodaya, the upliftment of the marginalised. **Policies:** complete integration of Jammu and Kashmir with India, a uniform civil code and a ban on religious conversions. **Reach:** once limited to northern and urban areas, now substantial across rural, southern and eastern India. **Governments:** leads the National Democratic Alliance (NDA) and holds power at the Centre with 303 seats in the 2019 Lok Sabha elections.

Q: Name the national political party which espouses secularism and the welfare of weaker sections and minorities. Mention any four features of that party.

A: The Indian National Congress (INC) espouses secularism and the welfare of weaker sections and minorities. **Founded:** 1885, one of the oldest parties globally. **Ideals:** centrist, supporting secularism and weaker-section welfare. **Role in history:** key player in independence and post-independence governance, especially under Jawaharlal Nehru. **Policies:** economic reforms with a human face and focus on social welfare. **Governments:** led the United Progressive Alliance (UPA) from 2004 to 2019, winning 19.5 per cent of votes and 52 seats in 2019.

Q: Identify the following political parties as national or regional: National Conference, Shiromani Akali Dal, DMK, Nationalist Congress Party, Bharatiya Janata Party, Shiv Sena, Forward Block, Janata Dal (U), Rashtriya Janata Dal, Samajwadi Party.

A: Regional: National Conference, Shiromani Akali Dal, DMK, Shiv Sena, Forward Block, Janata Dal (U), Rashtriya Janata Dal and Samajwadi Party. National: Nationalist Congress Party and Bharatiya Janata Party.

Q: Political parties need to face and overcome challenges to remain effective instruments of democracy. Write about such challenges. OR All over the world, people express dissatisfaction with parties' failure to perform. Analyse with arguments.

A: The challenges are lack of internal democracy within parties, dynastic succession, the growing role of money and muscle power especially during elections, and parties not offering voters a meaningful choice.

Q: Illustrate the situations which display a lack of internal democracy within a political party.

A: Parties do not keep membership registers, hold organisational meetings or conduct internal elections regularly. Ordinary members get insufficient information about inner workings and lack means to influence decisions, so leaders assume greater power in the party's name. With one or few leaders exercising paramount power, dissenters struggle to continue, and personal loyalty to the leader matters more than loyalty to principles and policies.

Q: Dynastic succession is one of the most serious challenges before political parties. Analyse the statement.

A: Most parties lack open, transparent procedures, so ordinary workers rarely rise to the top. Top leaders favour close associates or family members, and in many parties top positions stay within one family. This is unfair to other members and bad for democracy, since people without adequate experience or popular support occupy positions of power.

Q: Examine the role of money and muscle power among political parties during elections.

A: Focused only on winning, parties use shortcuts and nominate candidates who have or can raise lots of money. Rich donors and companies funding parties influence their policies and decisions. In some cases parties support criminals who can win elections. Democrats worldwide worry about the increasing role of the rich and big companies in politics.

Q: A decline in ideological differences among parties has been seen. Which challenge is this? Enumerate its features.

A: This is the lack of meaningful choice for voters. Its features are declining ideological differences worldwide, agreement on fundamentals with differences only in policy details, no option for those wanting really different policies, and inability to elect very different leaders since the same leaders keep shifting between parties.

Q: Elucidate recent efforts in India to reform political parties and their leaders.

A: The Constitution was amended so defecting MLAs and MPs lose their seats, curbing defections for ministerial posts or cash. The Supreme Court ordered candidates to file affidavits of property and pending criminal cases, reducing money and criminal influence. The Election Commission ordered parties to hold organisational elections and file income tax returns.

Q: What do you mean by defection? What measures were adopted to prevent it?

A: Defection means changing party allegiance from the party on which a person was elected to a legislature to a different party. The Constitution was amended so defecting MLAs and MPs lose their seats, since many defected for ministries or cash rewards. However, this has made dissent harder, as MPs and MLAs must accept whatever party leaders decide.

Q: Suggest and explain any five ways to reform political parties in India.

A: **Regulate internal affairs:** a law making parties maintain membership registers, follow their constitution and have an independent authority. **Ensure women's participation:** mandatory minimum tickets, about one-third, for women candidates. **State funding:** government money for election expenses to avoid corruption. **Pressure of public opinion:** petitions, propaganda and agitations, with pressure groups and media playing a role. **Public participation:** those wanting improvement should join parties, since democracy's quality depends on public participation.

Q: Suggest broad guidelines for devising political reforms in India.

A: Any legal change must consider its results on politics, since results may be counter-productive. Reforms should focus on strengthening democratic practice. Any proposal should consider not only the good solution but also who will implement it and how.
