Q: What is the ethnic composition of Sri Lanka?

A: Sinhala speakers are 74 per cent and Tamil speakers are 18 per cent, and among Tamils there are two subgroups — Sri Lankan Tamils and Indian Tamils.

Q: Sharing of powers makes a country more powerful and united. Do you agree and why? OR Power-sharing is the very spirit of democracy. Justify with three suitable points. OR How far do you agree that power-sharing keeps with the spirit of democracy?

A: Power-sharing ensures that all people have a stake in government. It ensures maximum participation, upholds the concept of people's rule, always brings better outcomes in democracy, and ensures people are consulted on how they are governed. It accommodates diverse groups and helps reduce the possibility of conflict between social groups.

Q: Explain any three consequences of the majoritarian policies adopted by the Sri Lankan government.

A: Tamils felt the government was insensitive to the Tamil language and culture, discriminatory in jobs and education, and practising religious discrimination. Relations between Tamils and Sinhalese became strained. Several political organisations formed demanding an independent Tamil Eelam in the north and east. Distrust turned into civil war, killing thousands from both communities, and caused a terrible setback to the country's social, cultural and economic life.

Q: Differentiate between the horizontal and vertical division of powers. OR Describe horizontal and vertical power-sharing in modern democracies.

A: In the horizontal division, power is shared among different organs of government such as the executive, legislature and judiciary; different organs exercise different powers; and this specifies checks and balances against unlimited power. In the vertical division, power is shared among governments at different levels like union, state and local, involving higher and lower levels; the constitution lays down each level's powers; and there are no checks and balances since powers flow from higher to lower levels by the constitution.

Q: Which three demands of the Sri Lankan Tamils, if accepted and met, could have settled the ethnic conflict in Sri Lanka for good? Explain.

A: Recognition of Tamil as an official language, equal opportunities for Tamils in government jobs and educational institutions, and provincial autonomy for Tamil-dominated provinces.

Q: What is power-sharing? Give reasons why power-sharing is desirable. OR Why is power-sharing desirable? Explain with prudential and moral reasons.

A: When power does not rest with any one organ of the state but is shared among different levels or organs of government, it is called power-sharing. **Prudential reasons:** it reduces the possibility of conflict between social groups, such stability of political order is ensured, and it promotes national unity. **Moral reasons:** power-sharing is the very spirit of democracy, since democratic rule involves sharing power with those affected by it; people have a right to be consulted on how they are governed; and it produces a legitimate government in which citizens acquire a stake through participation.

Q: Bring out any two sharp contrasts between Belgian and Sri Lankan democracies.

A: Both are democratic with diverse social setups but adopted opposite approaches. Belgium accommodated social and ethnic divisions while Sri Lanka followed majoritarian policies. Under the Belgian model power was shared between two ethnic groups, while Sri Lanka favoured the majority Sinhala community. In Belgium both groups had an equal share in government, while in Sri Lanka the minority was isolated. Belgium gave equal representation plus community governments at the local level, while Sri Lanka had no such arrangement. Belgium amended its constitution four times to prevent civil strife, while majoritarianism led Sri Lanka into a twenty-year civil war.

Q: What are the advantages of horizontal power-sharing? Explain with an example.

A: Power is shared equally among different organs of government, placing the legislature, executive and judiciary at the same level, so no organ can exercise unlimited powers — hence the system of checks and balances. For example, in India ministers and officials exercise power but are responsible to Parliament or state legislatures, while judges appointed by the executive can check the executive's functioning and laws made by legislatures.

Q: Describe any three majoritarian measures adopted by the Sri Lankan government to establish Sinhala supremacy. OR Describe any three provisions of the 1956 Act. OR Why did Sri Lankan Tamils feel alienated?

A: The government adopted majoritarian measures: in 1956 an Act recognised Sinhala as the only official language, disregarding Tamil. It followed preferential politics favouring Sinhala applicants for university positions and government jobs. A new Constitution stipulated that the state shall protect and foster Buddhism.

Q: Describe the power-sharing arrangement made by Belgium. OR Explain any three elements of the Belgian model. OR What changes were done to the Belgian constitution?

A: The number of Dutch and French-speaking ministers in the central government is equal. Some special laws require majority support from each linguistic group. State governments are not subordinate to the central government. Brussels has a separate government with equal representation of both communities. Apart from central and state governments, there is a community government elected by people of one language community — Dutch, French or German-speaking.

Q: Explain the major forms of power-sharing in modern democracies.

A: Power-sharing among different organs of government (horizontal); among governments at different levels; among different social groups; among political parties, pressure groups and movements; and through interest groups such as businessmen, farmers and industrial workers in a democracy.

1. The Bombay High Court ordered the Maharashtra state government to immediately take action and improve living conditions for the 2,000-odd children at seven children's homes in Mumbai.
2. The government of Ontario state in Canada has agreed to a land claim settlement with the aboriginal community. The Minister responsible for Native Affairs announced that the government will work with aboriginal people in a spirit of mutual respect and cooperation.
3. Russia's two influential political parties, the Union of Right Forces and the Liberal Yabloko Movement agreed to unite their organizations into a strong right-wing coalition. They propose to have a common list of candidates in the next parliamentary elections.
4. The finance ministers of various states in Nigeria got together and demanded that the federal government declare its sources of income. They also wanted to know the formula by which the revenue is distributed to various state governments.

Q: Here are some examples of power-sharing. Which of the four types do these represent? Who is sharing power with whom?

A: **First:** horizontal power-sharing between organs at the same level — the judiciary and the state legislature in this case. **Second:** power shared among different social and ethnic groups. **Third:** a power-sharing arrangement between two political parties which, if elected, form a coalition government. **Fourth:** the vertical form, with power shared between the union government and the states.
