One-paragraph revision of From the Diary of Anne Frank: why a lonely girl names her diary Kitty and charms her strict teacher.

## Summary

In this lesson, Anne talks about being lonely and having no one to talk to despite being surrounded by family and around thirty people whom she called her friends. Thus, she takes the decision of writing her feelings in a diary. It is unusual for her to write in a diary but she is really in need of a true friend. Thus, she decides to name her diary Kitty and unlike other people, she is not going to mention just facts in it.

She starts with writing her background, to make it easier to understand for anyone who reads it later. She describes her family and early childhood when they all migrated, and her early schooling. After giving a brief account of her background, she comes to the present day where she tells us about the result day. Everyone in the class is nervous about their result and boys are even making bets.

Anne was quite sure about herself and her friends. She had a good equation with all the teachers except her maths professor, who was constantly annoyed at her for her talking habits. Thus, he started giving her punishments in the form of essays on weird topics such as Chatterbox, to which she replied with full wit. She wrote essays that were funny and explained that this was a trait that she inherited from her mother and not much could be done about it. He kept on giving her essays until she wrote a satire. After that third and last assignment, Mr Keesing never pointed her out for talking.

```keys
- Anne names her diary Kitty because she needs a true friend to confide her feelings in, not just record facts.
- Her witty essays turn Mr Keesing's punishments for talking into a victory that ends his scolding for good.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: Why does Anne decide to write a diary?

A: Though surrounded by family and friends, she feels lonely with no true confidant, so she starts a diary as the friend she can tell everything to.

Q: How does Anne deal with Mr Keesing's punishments?

A: She answers each essay topic with humour, arguing talkativeness is inherited, and her final satire amuses him so much he stops punishing her.
