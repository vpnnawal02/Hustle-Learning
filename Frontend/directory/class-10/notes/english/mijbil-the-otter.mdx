## Summary

The chapter is an interesting account of the author's experiences with an unusual pet otter. After the death of his dog Jonnie, Maxwell felt lonely and decided to keep an otter instead of another dog. In 1956, while travelling to Southern Iraq, he learned that otters were commonly found in the marshes of the Tigris region and could be tamed. When he reached Basra, two Arabs brought him an otter sent by his friend.

Maxwell named the otter Mijbil, or Mij, and soon discovered that it belonged to a species previously unknown to science, which was later named Lutrogale perspicillata maxwelli in his honour. Initially, Mij was aloof and indifferent, but he gradually became comfortable with Maxwell and developed a playful and affectionate nature. He particularly loved water and spent hours splashing, rolling, and swimming in the bathtub. Mij also displayed remarkable intelligence and curiosity by learning to operate the bathroom tap, following Maxwell without a lead, responding to his name, and playing with marbles and a rubber ball.

However, transporting Mij from Iraq to England proved difficult because the airline required him to be carried in a small box. Mij struggled violently inside the box and injured himself while trying to escape. During the flight, he managed to get out and ran through the aircraft, frightening passengers who mistook him for a rat. Eventually, he returned to Maxwell and affectionately nuzzled his face and neck.

After reaching London, Mij continued to entertain Maxwell with his playful behaviour and invented games, including rolling a ping-pong ball down a sloping suitcase and catching it at the other end. During their walks, he developed unusual habits, such as running along a low wall near a school. Since most Londoners had never seen an otter, they made amusing guesses about his identity, mistaking him for animals such as a seal, squirrel, walrus, or even a hippopotamus.
