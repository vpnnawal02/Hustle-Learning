## Stanza 1

```stanza
He stalks in his vivid stripes
The few steps of his cage
On pads of velvet quiet
In his quiet rage
```

**Line 1: "He stalks in his vivid stripes"** The poet describes a tiger walking slowly and majestically inside a cage. The word stalks means to walk slowly, carefully and silently, often while hunting prey. It reflects the tiger's natural behaviour as a powerful predator. The phrase vivid stripes refers to the bright and clearly visible black stripes on the tiger's body. These stripes make the tiger look beautiful, distinctive and majestic. Although the tiger possesses a powerful body and an intimidating appearance, he is now confined to a zoo. His movements are restricted, and he cannot roam freely in the forest.

**Line 2: "The few steps of his cage"** The tiger can walk only a few steps inside his cage. In the forest, he would have a vast area to explore, hunt and move around freely. However, in the zoo, he is restricted to a small enclosure. The phrase few steps emphasises the limited space available to the tiger. It also highlights the contrast between his natural freedom and his present condition of captivity. The poet wants readers to understand that a tiger's natural habitat is the forest, not a small cage.

**Line 3: "On pads of velvet quiet"** The tiger walks silently on his soft paws. The poet compares the softness of the tiger's paws to velvet, a smooth and soft fabric. The word pads refers to the soft, cushioned undersides of the tiger's paws. These padded paws help the tiger move quietly while hunting, allowing him to approach his prey without making much noise. The expression also highlights the tiger's natural grace and stealth. Despite being a large and powerful animal, he can move with remarkable silence. However, in the zoo, this natural ability to stalk prey has little purpose because the tiger cannot hunt freely.

**Line 4: "In his quiet rage."** The tiger is angry and frustrated because he has been deprived of his freedom. However, he cannot express his anger openly and remains silent as he walks inside his cage. The phrase quiet rage suggests intense anger that is not expressed through loud roars or aggressive actions. The tiger's frustration remains suppressed within him. The poet presents a contrast between the tiger's powerful physical appearance and his helpless situation. Although he is naturally strong and dangerous, he cannot escape the cage or live according to his instincts.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 1:** The poet describes a beautiful and powerful tiger walking silently inside a small cage. His movements are restricted to a few steps, and his soft paws allow him to walk without making a sound. Although he possesses immense strength and natural hunting abilities, he is frustrated by his captivity. His quiet rage reflects his suppressed anger at losing his freedom.

## Stanza 2

```stanza
He should be lurking in shadow
Sliding through long grass
Near the water hole
Where plump deer pass
```

**Line 1: "He should be lurking in shadow"** The poet now describes how the tiger ought to live in his natural habitat. The expression should be indicates that the tiger belongs in the forest rather than in a cage. The poet imagines him hiding in the shadows of trees and bushes, waiting patiently for an opportunity to hunt. The word lurking means hiding or remaining concealed, usually while waiting to catch prey. Tigers naturally use the thick vegetation and shadows of the forest to hide themselves from other animals. By describing the tiger in this way, the poet reminds readers that hunting and moving freely are natural activities for a tiger.

**Line 2: "Sliding through long grass"** The tiger should be moving smoothly and silently through the tall grass of the forest. The word sliding suggests graceful, effortless and nearly noiseless movement. The tiger would move carefully through the grass so that its prey would not notice its presence. The tall grass provides natural cover, allowing the tiger to remain hidden while approaching its prey. This line highlights the tiger's agility and ability to move stealthily in the forest. Unlike the restricted movements of the zoo, the tiger would be free to move wherever it wished.

**Line 3: "Near the water hole"** The tiger should be moving near a water hole, which is a place where animals gather to drink water. In a forest, water holes attract many animals, especially during hot and dry conditions. Deer and other herbivores frequently visit these places to satisfy their thirst. Therefore, a water hole can also become a suitable hunting location for a tiger because prey animals are likely to visit it. The poet uses this setting to describe the tiger's natural surroundings and the activities he would perform if he were free.

**Line 4: "Where plump deer pass."** The poet imagines the tiger waiting near the water hole where healthy, well-fed deer come to drink water. The word plump means well-fed or relatively fat. It describes the deer that pass near the water hole. The tiger is a carnivorous animal, which means that it feeds on other animals. Hunting deer is a natural part of its life in the forest. The poet presents a picture of the tiger preparing to hunt its prey in its natural environment. This is very different from its life in the zoo, where food is provided and the tiger does not have to hunt.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 2:** The poet imagines the tiger living freely in the forest. It should hide in the shadows, move silently through tall grass and wait near a water hole where deer come to drink. These activities represent the tiger's natural behaviour as a wild predator. The stanza emphasises that the forest provides the freedom, space and opportunities necessary for the tiger to live according to its instincts.

## Stanza 3

```stanza
He should be snarling around houses
At the jungle's edge
Baring his white fangs, his claws
Terrorising the village!
```

**Line 1: "He should be snarling around houses"** The poet continues to imagine the tiger in its natural environment, particularly near the boundary between the forest and human settlements. The word snarling means making an aggressive, threatening growling sound, often while showing the teeth. The tiger should be free to roam through the forest and its surrounding areas rather than being confined to a cage. The poet imagines the tiger moving near houses located close to the forest. This description emphasises the tiger's natural power and intimidating presence. Unlike its silent and restricted movements in the zoo, the tiger in the forest can behave according to its instincts.

**Line 2: "At the jungle's edge,"** The phrase jungle's edge refers to the boundary of the forest, where the forest meets open land or human settlements. People sometimes build houses and villages near forests. As a result, wild animals may occasionally come close to these settlements while searching for food or moving through their natural habitat. The poet imagines the tiger roaming freely near this boundary. This line also establishes the setting for the next two lines, in which the tiger's sharp teeth and claws are described.

**Line 3: "Baring his white fangs, his claws,"** The word baring means exposing or showing something. Fangs are the tiger's long, sharp canine teeth, while claws are the sharp, curved nails on its paws. The poet imagines the tiger exposing its sharp teeth and claws, making its powerful and threatening appearance evident. These features are essential to the tiger's life as a predator. Its fangs help it catch and kill prey, while its claws help it seize prey and defend itself. The description emphasises the tiger's natural strength and ability to inspire fear. In the zoo, however, the tiger cannot fully express its natural behaviour because it is confined behind bars.

**Line 4: "Terrorising the village!"** The word terrorising means frightening or causing great fear. The poet imagines the tiger frightening people living in a village near the forest. Its powerful body, sharp teeth and claws make it an intimidating animal. The exclamation mark emphasises the intensity of the situation and the fear that the tiger could create among the villagers. However, the poet is not necessarily suggesting that the tiger should attack humans. Rather, he is highlighting the tiger's natural strength and freedom in contrast with its helplessness in captivity.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 3:** The poet imagines the tiger roaming freely near a village situated at the edge of a forest. It snarls, exposes its sharp teeth and claws, and creates fear among the villagers. These descriptions emphasise its natural power and wild instincts. The stanza contrasts the tiger's ability to behave freely in its natural environment with its restricted existence inside the zoo.

## Stanza 4

```stanza
But he's locked in a concrete cell
His strength behind bars
Stalking the length of his cage
Ignoring visitors
```

**Line 1: "But he's locked in a concrete cell"** The word but marks a significant change in the poem. Until this point, the poet has described how the tiger should live in its natural habitat. Now, he returns to the tiger's actual condition in the zoo. The tiger is locked inside a concrete enclosure. The word cell is commonly associated with a prison, where a prisoner is confined and deprived of freedom. By using this word, the poet compares the tiger's cage to a prison. The tiger is not free to roam through the forest, hunt prey or explore its surroundings. The phrase concrete cell creates an image of a cold, enclosed and restrictive space. It highlights the unnatural conditions in which the tiger is forced to live.

**Line 2: "His strength behind bars"** The tiger is physically powerful, but his strength is restricted by the bars of his cage. Under natural conditions, the tiger's muscular body, sharp teeth and claws help it hunt and survive in the forest. However, inside the zoo, it cannot use these abilities freely. The phrase behind bars suggests imprisonment and the loss of freedom. The tiger may possess tremendous physical strength, but this strength cannot help him escape his enclosure. The line also conveys a deeper idea: even the strongest creature can become helpless when its freedom is taken away.

**Line 3: "Stalking the length of his cage"** The tiger walks repeatedly from one end of the cage to the other. The word stalking refers to the tiger's slow, deliberate movement. However, instead of stalking prey in the forest, the tiger is merely pacing inside its enclosure. The expression the length of his cage emphasises the limited space in which the tiger can move. The tiger's repeated movements suggest frustration and restlessness. It has no opportunity to roam freely or perform its natural activities, so it continues walking within the small space available to it. This behaviour highlights the difference between the tiger's natural life and its existence in captivity.

**Line 4: "Ignoring visitors."** People visit the zoo to look at the tiger, but the tiger pays no attention to them. The visitors may consider the tiger an interesting attraction, but they do not understand the frustration of living inside a cage. The tiger appears uninterested in the people watching it. His attention remains focused on his own existence rather than on entertaining the visitors. This line may also suggest the tiger's emotional withdrawal and dissatisfaction with captivity. The poet draws attention to the difference between how humans view animals in zoos and what captivity means for the animals themselves.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 4:** The poet returns to reality and describes the tiger's life in the zoo. The tiger is imprisoned in a concrete enclosure, where the bars prevent it from using its physical strength freely. It repeatedly walks from one end of the cage to the other and ignores the visitors watching it. The stanza highlights the tiger's helplessness, frustration and loss of freedom.

## Stanza 5

```stanza
He hears the last voice at night
The patrolling cars
And stares with his brilliant eyes
At the brilliant stars
```

**Line 1: "He hears the last voice at night"** The poet describes the tiger during the night, when the zoo becomes quiet and most visitors have left. The phrase the last voice refers to the final human voice the tiger hears before the zoo becomes silent for the night. During the day, the tiger may hear people talking, children shouting and visitors moving around its enclosure. At night, these sounds gradually disappear. The line creates a sense of loneliness. The tiger is left alone in its cage, separated from the natural environment in which it should be living. The silence of the night also draws attention to the tiger's isolation and the unnatural conditions of captivity.

**Line 2: "The patrolling cars"** The tiger can hear cars moving around the zoo as security personnel patrol the premises. The word patrolling means moving around an area to keep it under observation and maintain security. The presence of patrolling cars suggests that the zoo is guarded and that the tiger is securely confined within its enclosure. These vehicles are a reminder that the tiger is not free to leave the zoo. Human-made security measures ensure that it remains within the boundaries set for it. The line reinforces the idea that the tiger's freedom has been taken away.

**Line 3: "And stares with his brilliant eyes"** The tiger looks intensely at the world around him with his bright, shining eyes. The word brilliant means bright or shining. It emphasises the beauty and intensity of the tiger's eyes. The verb stares suggests that the tiger is looking fixedly at something rather than glancing at it casually. Although the tiger is physically confined, it can still observe the world outside its cage. Its gaze may suggest a longing for freedom or an awareness of the vast world beyond the enclosure. The tiger's brilliant eyes also remind readers of its natural beauty and strength, which remain present despite its imprisonment.

**Line 4: "At the brilliant stars."** The tiger looks at the stars shining in the night sky. The repetition of the word brilliant connects the brightness of the tiger's eyes with the brightness of the stars. The stars appear in the vast, open sky, which contrasts with the small and restricted enclosure in which the tiger lives. The tiger can see the stars, but it cannot roam freely beneath the open sky. This contrast emphasises the difference between the freedom of the natural world and the limitations of captivity. The stars may symbolise freedom, the vastness of nature and the world that remains beyond the tiger's reach. The poem ends with this image rather than with an escape or a change in the tiger's situation. The tiger remains confined, and its freedom remains out of reach.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 5:** At night, the tiger hears the final human voice and the sounds of patrolling cars around the zoo. It then gazes at the shining stars with its bright eyes. The quiet surroundings emphasise its isolation, while the vast sky contrasts with its restricted enclosure. The tiger's gaze suggests a longing for the freedom it has lost, bringing the poem to a thoughtful and moving conclusion.
