A stanza-by-stanza explanation of **A Tiger in the Zoo** by Leslie Norris. Read each stanza slowly, then read its explanation.

> [!TIP] Track the two alternating pictures — tiger caged vs tiger free — and every stanza falls into place.

## Stanza 1 — The caged tiger

```stanza
He stalks in his vivid stripes
The few steps of his cage
On pads of velvet quiet
In his quiet rage
```

The tiger paces a few steps inside his cage on soft, velvet-quiet paws. **Stalks** (slow, silent hunting walk), **vivid stripes** and **pads** show his natural grace — but here they serve nothing. His **quiet rage** is suppressed anger at lost freedom: powerful, yet helpless.

## Stanza 2 — The forest he should haunt

```stanza
He should be lurking in shadow
Sliding through long grass
Near the water hole
Where plump deer pass
```

The poet imagines the tiger where he belongs: **lurking** (hiding) in shadows, **sliding** silently through long grass near the **water hole**, where **plump** (well-fed) deer come to drink. Hunting, hiding, roaming free — the wild predator's true life.

## Stanza 3 — Terror at the jungle's edge

```stanza
He should be snarling around houses
At the jungle's edge
Baring his white fangs, his claws
Terrorising the village!
```

Free, the tiger would snarl near forest-edge houses, **baring** (exposing) white **fangs** and claws, terrifying the village. The poet is not asking tigers to attack people — he is measuring the tiger's natural power against its caged helplessness.

## Stanza 4 — Locked in concrete

```stanza
But he's locked in a concrete cell
His strength behind bars
Stalking the length of his cage
Ignoring visitors
```

**But** snaps us back to reality: a **concrete cell** — a prison word for a cage. Muscle, fangs and claws mean nothing **behind bars**. He paces the cage's length, restless and frustrated, **ignoring visitors** who see entertainment where he feels imprisonment.

## Stanza 5 — Night and stars

```stanza
He hears the last voice at night
The patrolling cars
And stares with his brilliant eyes
At the brilliant stars
```

At night the zoo falls silent except the **last voice** and **patrolling cars** guarding his prison. He stares with **brilliant eyes** at the **brilliant stars** — the repeated word links his shining gaze to the vast open sky he cannot roam. Freedom glitters above; the cage holds below.

**Overall meaning:** A magnificent predator belongs to the forest, not a concrete cell. The poem contrasts natural power with captive frustration, ending on a caged animal longing at unreachable stars.

```keys
- Cage stanzas (1, 4, 5): few steps, concrete cell, ignored visitors, patrolling cars, distant stars.
- Freedom stanzas (2, 3): shadows, long grass, water hole, snarling, bared fangs.
- Devices: contrast throughout, prison imagery (cell, bars), repeated brilliant linking eyes to stars.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the central idea of A Tiger in the Zoo?

A: Wild animals suffer in captivity — the tiger's strength and instincts belong to the forest, not a zoo cage.
