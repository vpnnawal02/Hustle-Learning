One-paragraph revision of The Thief's Story by Ruskin Bond: how trust and education reform a young thief.

## Summary

The story revolves around a fifteen-year-old thief named Hari Singh and his relationship with a kind-hearted young man named Anil. Hari is an experienced thief who frequently changes his name to avoid being caught by the police. One day, he meets Anil at a wrestling match and befriends him with the intention of robbing him. When Hari asks Anil for work, Anil agrees to provide him with food and shelter in exchange for his services.

Although Hari initially lies about his cooking skills, Anil forgives him and offers to teach him how to cook, read, write and perform basic calculations. Hari is delighted by the opportunity to learn, as he realises that education can help him build a better future. While living with Anil, Hari occasionally makes small profits by cheating during shopping, but Anil appears to overlook his dishonesty.

One evening, Anil returns home with six hundred rupees after selling a book to a publisher and keeps the money under his mattress. Tempted by the opportunity, Hari steals the money while Anil is asleep and heads towards the railway station to catch a train to Lucknow. However, just as the train begins to leave, Hari hesitates and misses his chance to escape. As he wanders through the streets in the rain, he begins to feel guilty and realises that by stealing the money, he would lose Anil's trust and the opportunity to receive an education.

He understands that learning to read and write could help him achieve far more than the money he has stolen. Consequently, Hari returns to Anil's room and quietly places the damp notes back under the mattress. The next morning, Anil gives Hari a fifty-rupee note and tells him that he will now be paid regularly. Hari realises that Anil knows about the theft, but Anil neither confronts him nor punishes him. Instead, he continues to teach Hari, beginning with writing complete sentences.

## Frequently asked questions

Q: Why does Hari Singh return the stolen money?

A: His conscience awakens: education and Anil's trust promise a better future than six hundred rupees spent in a week or two.

Q: How does Anil show he knows about the theft?

A: He says nothing but hands Hari a damp fifty-rupee note, starts paying him regularly, and continues the writing lessons.
