## Stanza 1

```stanza
Some say the world will end in fire
Some say in ice
From what I've tasted of desire
I hold with those who favour fire
```

**Explanation:** The poet begins by presenting two different opinions about how the world might end. Some people believe that fire will destroy the world, while others think ice will be responsible. The poet then relates these possibilities to human emotions. Based on his personal experience of desire, he agrees with those who believe that fire will destroy the world. Here, fire symbolises uncontrolled desire, greed, passion and excessive ambition. Just as fire can spread rapidly and destroy everything in its path, uncontrolled desires can lead to destruction. The poet suggests that if human beings allow their desires to become excessive, these desires may ultimately destroy the world. The poet believes that uncontrolled desire is powerful enough to destroy the world, just as fire destroys everything it touches.

**In simple words:** A crow shakes snow off a hemlock tree, and the falling snow lands on the poet.

## Stanza 2

```stanza
But if it had to perish twice
I think I know enough of hate
To say that for destruction ice
Is also great
And would suffice
```

**Explanation:** In the second stanza, the poet considers another possibility. If the world had to be destroyed a second time, he believes that ice would be equally capable of causing its destruction. Here, ice symbolises hatred, coldness, cruelty and emotional indifference. Just as ice can freeze and destroy life, hatred can make human beings insensitive towards others and lead to conflict and destruction. The poet has experienced enough hatred to understand its destructive power. He therefore concludes that hatred is just as dangerous as desire and is sufficient to destroy the world. The poet's message is that both excessive desire and hatred can cause immense destruction. While desire may burn like fire, hatred can freeze relationships and destroy humanity from within.

**In simple words:** The falling snow changes the poet's mood and saves a part of his otherwise unhappy day.

## Central Idea of the Poem

The poem highlights the destructive potential of two powerful human emotions: desire and hatred. Fire represents uncontrolled desire, greed and passion, whereas ice represents hatred, cruelty and emotional coldness. The poet initially favours fire as the force that could destroy the world. However, he acknowledges that ice is equally capable of causing destruction. Through this comparison, Robert Frost warns humanity against allowing negative emotions to control human behaviour. The poem's message is that uncontrolled desire and hatred can destroy not only individuals and relationships but also society and the world.

## Poetic Devices

| Poetic Device | Example | Explanation |
| ------------- | ------- | ----------- |
| Symbolism | Fire and ice | Fire represents desire, while ice represents hatred. |
| Alliteration | Some say | The initial s sound is repeated. |
| Alliteration | I hold with those who favour fire | The h sound is repeated in "hold" and "who" only approximately depending on pronunciation; this is not the strongest example of alliteration in the poem. |
| Enjambment | To say that for destruction ice / Is also great | The sentence continues from one line to the next without a full stop. |
| Rhyme scheme | Fire, desire, twice, ice, suffice | The poem follows the rhyme scheme ABAABCBCB. |
