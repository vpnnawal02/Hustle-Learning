A stanza-by-stanza explanation of **How to Tell Wild Animals** by Carolyn Wells. Every stanza identifies one animal through dark, playful humour.

> [!TIP] Each stanza follows one formula — funny clue plus fatal encounter. Learn the clue per animal.

## Stanza 1 — The Asian Lion

```stanza
If ever you should go by chance
To jungles in the east
And if there should to you advance
A large and tawny beast
If he roars at you as you're dyin'
You'll know it is the Asian Lion
```

Wander **by chance** into eastern jungles, meet a large **tawny** (yellow-brown) beast — and if it **roars at you as you're dyin'**, it's the Asian Lion. The joke: you identify it at the exact moment it kills you.

## Stanza 2 — The Bengal Tiger

```stanza
Or if some time when roaming round
A noble wild beast greets you
With black stripes on a yellow ground
This may be the Bengal Tiger
```

A **noble** beast **greets** you — politely! — wearing **black stripes on a yellow ground**. The humour is the mismatch: deadly predator, courteous vocabulary.

## Stanza 3 — The Leopard

```stanza
If strolling forth, a beast you view
Whose hide with spots is peppered
As soon as he has leapt on you
You'll know it is the Leopard
```

A **hide peppered** (sprinkled) with spots — and you'll know it for certain **as soon as he has leapt on you**. Identification by attack: the poem's running gag at full strength.

## Stanza 4 — The Bear

```stanza
If when you're walking round your yard
You meet a creature there
Who hugs you very, very hard
Be sure it is a Bear
```

> [!NOTE] The explanation follows the standard NCERT version above, commonly used in CBSE examinations.

A backyard meeting with a creature that **hugs you very, very hard** can only be a bear. The deadly grab becomes an affectionate hug — menace rewritten as manners.

## Stanza 5 — Crocodile vs Hyena, introduced

```stanza
Though to distinguish beasts of prey
A novice might nonplus
The Crocodile you always may
Tell from the Hyena thus:
```

Telling predators apart will **nonplus** (bewilder) a **novice** (beginner) — so here is a foolproof method, coming next.

## Stanza 6 — Smiles vs tears

```stanza
Hyenas come with merry smiles
But if they weep they're Crocodiles
```

> [!NOTE] The explanation follows the standard NCERT version above.

Hyenas **smile** (their cackle sounds like laughter); weeping means **crocodile** — famous for **crocodile tears**, grief that isn't genuine. Human expressions pasted onto predators: pure comedy, zero field-guide value.

## Stanza 7 — The Chameleon

```stanza
The true Chameleon is small
A lizard sort of thing
He hasn't any ears at all
And not a single wing
```

After all the exaggeration, a plain description: small, **lizard sort of thing**, no ears, no wings. The joke lands by contrast — the only animal described accurately is the harmless one.

**Overall meaning:** A mock field guide where every identification method is fatal, funny, or both — entertainment dressed as instruction.

```keys
- One joke engine: identify the beast by the attack it is currently making on you.
- Signature clues: tawny roar, striped greeting, spotted leap, crushing hug, smiling hyena, weeping crocodile.
- Tone: polite words (greets, smiles, hugs) for violent animals throughout.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the central idea of How to Tell Wild Animals?

A: Humour through mock instruction — dangerous beasts identified by absurd, often fatal, clues.
