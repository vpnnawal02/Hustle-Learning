## Stanza 1

```stanza
Belinda lived in a little white house,
With a little black kitten and a little grey mouse,
And a little yellow dog and a little red wagon,
And a realio, trulio, little pet dragon.
```

**Explanation:** Belinda lived in a small white house with four companions: a black kitten, a grey mouse, a yellow dog, and a pet dragon. She named her kitten, mouse, and dog Ink, Blink, and Mustard, respectively. The poet uses playful language and rhyming words to introduce the characters and create a cheerful atmosphere. The expression "realio, trulio" is a humorous, informal way of saying "really, truly."

## Stanza 2

```stanza
Now the name of the little black kitten was Ink,
And the little grey mouse, she called him Blink,
And the little yellow dog was sharp as Mustard,
But the dragon was a coward, and she called him Custard.
```

**Explanation:** The poet introduces the names of Belinda's pets. The black kitten is called Ink, the grey mouse is named Blink, and the yellow dog is called Mustard. The dog is described as sharp and alert. However, the dragon is considered a coward because he is always afraid and wants to feel safe. Belinda therefore names him Custard. The stanza creates humour by contrasting the dragon's impressive appearance with his fearful nature.

## Stanza 3

```stanza
Custard the dragon had big sharp teeth,
And spikes on top of him and scales underneath,
Mouth like a fireplace, chimney for a nose,
And realio, trulio daggers on his toes.
```

**Explanation:** The poet describes Custard's frightening physical appearance. The dragon has large, sharp teeth, spikes on his back, and scales underneath his body. His mouth resembles a fireplace, while his nose looks like a chimney. His claws are as sharp as daggers. Despite his terrifying appearance, Custard is afraid of danger and constantly asks for a safe cage. This contrast between his powerful appearance and his cowardly behaviour adds humour to the poem.

## Stanza 4

```stanza
Belinda was as brave as a barrel full of bears,
And Ink and Blink chased lions down the stairs,
Mustard was as brave as a tiger in a rage,
But Custard cried for a nice safe cage.
```

**Explanation:** Belinda is described as extremely brave, like a barrel full of bears. Ink and Blink are supposedly so courageous that they can chase lions down the stairs. Mustard is compared to an angry tiger because of his apparent bravery. In contrast, Custard is frightened and repeatedly asks for a safe cage. The poet exaggerates the bravery of the other characters while presenting Custard as a coward.

## Stanza 5

```stanza
Belinda tickled him, she tickled him unmerciful,
Ink, Blink and Mustard, they rudely called him Percival,
They all sat laughing in the little red wagon
At the realio, trulio, cowardly dragon.
```

**Explanation:** Belinda repeatedly tickles Custard, while Ink, Blink, and Mustard tease him by calling him Percival. They all sit in the little red wagon and laugh at him because he is afraid and keeps asking for a safe cage. Their behaviour shows that they do not take Custard seriously and consider him a coward. The poet uses humour to show how the other characters ridicule Custard because of his fearful nature.

## Stanza 6

```stanza
Belinda giggled till she shook the house,
And Blink said Weeck! which is giggling for a mouse,
Ink and Mustard rudely asked his age,
When Custard cried for a nice safe cage.
```

**Explanation:** Belinda laughs so much that the poet humorously suggests her laughter makes the entire house shake. Blink, the mouse, also laughs, making the sound "Weeck!" because that is how a mouse giggles. Ink and Mustard continue teasing Custard by rudely asking his age. Custard remains frightened and once again asks for a safe cage. The stanza highlights the difference between how the other characters perceive themselves and how they treat Custard.

## Stanza 7

```stanza
Suddenly, suddenly they heard a nasty sound,
And Mustard growled, and they all looked around.
Meowch! cried Ink, and ooh! cried Belinda,
For there was a pirate, climbing in the winda.
```

**Explanation:** The situation suddenly changes when everyone hears a frightening noise. Mustard growls, and the others look around to discover the source of the sound. Ink cries out in fear, and Belinda exclaims in alarm. They discover a pirate climbing through the window into their house. The sudden appearance of the pirate introduces danger into the story. Until this point, the other characters have claimed to be brave, but now they have to face a real threat.

## Stanza 8

```stanza
Pistol in his left hand, pistol in his right,
And he held in his teeth a cutlass bright,
His beard was black, one leg was wood;
It was clear that the pirate meant no good.
```

**Explanation:** The poet describes the pirate as a frightening and dangerous man. He carries a pistol in each hand and holds a shiny cutlass, a type of sword, between his teeth. He has a black beard and a wooden leg. His weapons and threatening appearance make it clear that he has entered the house with harmful intentions. The description builds suspense and prepares the reader for the confrontation between the pirate and Belinda's companions.

## Stanza 9

```stanza
Belinda paled, and she cried Help! Help!
But Mustard fled with a terrified yelp,
Ink trickled down to the bottom of the household,
And little mouse Blink strategically mouseholed.
```

**Explanation:** When Belinda sees the pirate, she turns pale with fear and cries for help. Mustard, who was previously described as brave as an angry tiger, runs away in terror. Ink also escapes and hides at the bottom of the house. Blink cleverly finds a mousehole and hides inside it. The behaviour of these characters contradicts their earlier claims of bravery. When faced with actual danger, they all become frightened and try to save themselves.

**Central Idea:** The supposed bravery of Belinda, Mustard, Ink, and Blink disappears when they face a real threat.

## Stanza 10

```stanza
But up jumped Custard, snorting like an engine,
Clashed his tail like irons in a dungeon,
With a clatter and a clank and a jangling squirm,
He went at the pirate like a robin at a worm.
```

**Explanation:** Unlike the others, Custard immediately jumps up to confront the pirate. He makes loud noises while snorting and swinging his tail. The sounds of his movements resemble the clanging of metal objects in a dungeon. Custard charges at the pirate with the determination of a robin attacking a worm. Although everyone had previously considered him a coward, he is the only one who acts courageously when danger arrives. This moment reveals Custard's true character. His actions prove that he possesses courage despite his fearful nature.

## Stanza 11

```stanza
The pirate gaped at Belinda's dragon,
And gulped some grog from his pocket flagon,
He fired two bullets, but they didn't hit,
And Custard gobbled him, every bit.
```

**Explanation:** The pirate is shocked when he sees Custard attacking him. He drinks some grog, an alcoholic drink traditionally associated with sailors, from his flask. The pirate fires two bullets at Custard, but neither bullet hits him. Custard then attacks the pirate and swallows him completely. The dragon whom everyone had mocked for being a coward becomes the hero of the story. He defeats the dangerous intruder and saves everyone in the house.

## Stanza 12

```stanza
Belinda embraced him, Mustard licked him,
No one mourned for his pirate victim.
Ink and Blink in glee did gyrate
Around the dragon that ate the pirate.
```

**Explanation:** After Custard defeats the pirate, Belinda embraces him to express her affection and gratitude. Mustard shows his affection by licking him. Nobody feels sad about the pirate's death because he had entered their home with harmful intentions. Ink and Blink happily dance in circles around Custard, celebrating his victory. Custard is finally appreciated for his bravery, as he has protected everyone from the pirate.

## Stanza 13

```stanza
But presently up spoke little dog Mustard,
I'd have been twice as brave if I hadn't been flustered.
And up spoke Ink and up spoke Blink,
We'd have been three times as brave, we think,
And Custard said, I quite agree
That everybody is braver than me.
```

**Explanation:** After the danger has passed, Mustard claims that he would have been twice as brave if he had not become confused and frightened. Ink and Blink also boast that they would have been three times as brave as Custard. Surprisingly, Custard agrees with them and says that everyone is braver than he is. Even after proving his courage through his actions, he remains modest and does not boast about his achievement. The stanza highlights the difference between actual courage and empty boasting. Mustard, Ink, and Blink make excuses for their cowardice, while Custard remains humble despite being the real hero.

## Stanza 14

```stanza
Belinda still lives in her little white house,
With her little black kitten and her little grey mouse,
And her little yellow dog and her little red wagon,
And her realio, trulio little pet dragon.
```

**Explanation:** The poet returns to the description of Belinda's home and her companions. Belinda continues living with Ink, Blink, Mustard, and Custard in the same little white house. Although Custard has proved himself courageous by defeating the pirate, the poet returns to the familiar description of the characters. This creates a sense of continuity and prepares the reader for the humorous ending.

## Stanza 15

```stanza
Belinda is as brave as a barrel full of bears,
And Ink and Blink chase lions down the stairs,
Mustard is as brave as a tiger in a rage,
But Custard keeps crying for a nice safe cage.
```

**Explanation:** At the end of the poem, the poet repeats the earlier descriptions of the characters. Belinda is still presented as extremely brave, Ink and Blink are supposedly capable of chasing lions, and Mustard is compared to an angry tiger. Custard, however, continues asking for a safe cage, even though he has already defeated the pirate. This creates irony because the character who performed the bravest act still considers himself a coward, while the others continue to boast about their imaginary courage. The ending is humorous, but it also conveys an important message: true courage is demonstrated through actions, not words.
