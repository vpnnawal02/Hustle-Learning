One-paragraph revision of Footprints Without Feet by H. G. Wells: a brilliant scientist's invisibility misused for crime.

## Summary

The story revolves around Griffin, a brilliant scientist who discovers how to make the human body invisible. After conducting numerous experiments, he swallows certain rare drugs that make his body transparent while keeping it solid. However, Griffin is a lawless and irresponsible man who misuses his scientific discovery for selfish purposes.

When his landlord tries to evict him, Griffin sets fire to the house and escapes by removing his clothes, becoming invisible and homeless. During winter in London, he enters a large departmental store to protect himself from the cold. After the store closes, he steals clothes, food and other necessities and spends the night there. The following morning, when the assistants arrive, Griffin panics and escapes by removing his clothes, once again becoming invisible and leaving behind the items he had stolen.

He then visits a theatrical shop in Drury Lane, where he obtains clothes, bandages, dark glasses, a false nose and a hat to disguise his appearance. To obtain money, he attacks the shopkeeper from behind and robs him. Griffin subsequently travels to the village of Iping and rents rooms at a local inn, where Mrs Hall, the landlord's wife, finds his strange appearance and secretive behaviour unusual. Griffin claims that an accident has affected his face and insists that he wants solitude to concentrate on his scientific work.

When his stolen money runs out, he steals money from a clergyman's house. Although the clergyman and his wife hear noises and discover that their money has disappeared, they cannot find anyone inside the room because Griffin is invisible. Later, strange incidents occur at the inn: Mrs Hall's furniture appears to move by itself, a hat flies at her face, and a chair attacks her and her husband, convincing her that the room is haunted.

The villagers become suspicious of Griffin, particularly after learning about the burglary. When Mrs Hall confronts him, Griffin loses his temper and removes his disguise, revealing himself as a headless man. The village constable, Mr Jaffers, arrives to arrest him, but Griffin removes his clothes one by one and becomes completely invisible. Despite attempts by the constable and other people to catch him, Griffin fights back and eventually escapes.

```keys
- The story highlights Griffin's extraordinary scientific achievement but also exposes his selfishness, dishonesty and misuse of knowledge.
- Through Griffin's actions, the author conveys that scientific discoveries should be used responsibly and that intelligence without morality can become dangerous.
```

## Frequently asked questions

A: After many experiments he swallowed rare drugs that made his body transparent like glass while keeping it solid.

Q: Why is Griffin called lawless despite being brilliant?

A: He burns his landlord's house, steals from shops and a clergyman, attacks a shopkeeper and fights the constable, using science only for crime.
