## Stanza 1

```stanza
Never shall a young man,
Thrown into despair
By those great honey-coloured
Ramparts at your ear,
Love you for yourself alone
And not your yellow hair.
```

**Explanation:** In this stanza, the poet tells Anne Gregory that no young man will ever love her purely for her inner qualities and personality. Young men are attracted to her beautiful yellow hair, which is described as "honey-coloured." The word "ramparts" means defensive walls, and here it refers metaphorically to the thick locks of Anne's hair that surround her ears. The poet suggests that Anne's physical beauty is so attractive that young men become overwhelmed by it. According to the poet, men are attracted to Anne because of her beautiful yellow hair rather than her true self, character, or personality.

**Central Idea:** Physical beauty, particularly Anne's yellow hair, attracts young men, making it difficult for them to love her purely for her inner qualities.

**Poetic Devices:**

- **Metaphor:** "Ramparts at your ear" compares Anne's hair to defensive walls.
- **Imagery:** "Honey-coloured" and "yellow hair" create a visual picture of Anne's beautiful hair.
- **Symbolism:** The yellow hair represents physical beauty and outward appearance.

## Stanza 2

```stanza
But I can get a hair-dye
And set such colour there,
Brown, or black, or carrot,
That young men in despair
May love me for myself alone
And not my yellow hair.
```

**Explanation:** In this stanza, Anne Gregory responds to the poet's statement. She argues that she can change the colour of her hair by using hair dye. She could make it brown, black, or carrot-coloured (orange). Anne believes that if she changes her hair colour, young men might stop being attracted to her yellow hair and instead love her for her personality and inner qualities. Her response suggests that she believes physical beauty can be changed and that changing her appearance might help her discover whether someone genuinely loves her for who she is. However, her argument does not necessarily mean that changing her hair colour will make young men love her for her inner qualities.

**Central Idea:** Anne believes that she can overcome the problem of being loved only for her physical beauty by changing the colour of her hair.

**Poetic Devices:**

- **Imagery:** "Brown, or black, or carrot" creates a picture of different possible hair colours.
- **Repetition:** "Love me for myself alone / And not my yellow hair" repeats the central idea of the first stanza, emphasising Anne's desire to be loved for herself.

## Stanza 3

```stanza
I heard an old religious man
But yesternight declare
That he had found a text to prove
That only God, my dear,
Could love you for yourself alone
And not your yellow hair.
```

**Explanation:** In the final stanza, the poet refers to an old religious man who had made an interesting observation the previous night. The religious man claimed that he had found a passage in a religious text proving that only God could love Anne purely for herself, without being influenced by her physical appearance. The statement suggests that human beings are often attracted to outward beauty and may find it difficult to look beyond physical appearance. In contrast, God's love is presented as unconditional and free from such superficial attractions. The stanza concludes the poem with a thought-provoking idea: genuine love should value a person's inner qualities rather than merely their physical appearance.

**Central Idea:** The poet suggests that only God can love Anne entirely for her inner self, without being influenced by her physical beauty.

**Poetic Devices:**

- **Repetition:** "Could love you for yourself alone / And not your yellow hair" repeats the central idea of the previous stanzas.
- **Contrast:** Human attraction to physical beauty is contrasted with God's unconditional love.
- **Irony:** Anne believes that changing her hair colour might make young men love her for herself, but the religious man's statement suggests that human beings may remain influenced by physical appearance.

## Overall Message of the Poem

The poem For Anne Gregory explores the difference between physical attraction and genuine love. Through a conversation between the poet and Anne, it highlights how people are often attracted to outward beauty rather than inner qualities. Anne believes that changing her yellow hair to another colour might encourage men to love her for her personality. However, the religious man's statement suggests that only God can love a person completely for their inner self, without being influenced by physical appearance. The central message is that physical beauty is temporary, while inner qualities form a deeper part of a person's identity. The poem questions whether human beings can truly love someone without being influenced by outward appearance.
