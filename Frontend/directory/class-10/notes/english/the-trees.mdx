A stanza-by-stanza explanation of **The Trees** by Adrienne Rich. Read each stanza slowly, then read its explanation.

> [!TIP] Read the poem on two levels at once — trees escaping a house, and women stepping out toward freedom.

## Stanza 1 — The forest waits

```stanza
The trees inside are moving out into the forest
the forest that was empty all these days
where no bird could sit
no insect hide
no sun bury its feet in shadow
the forest that was empty all these nights
will be full of trees by morning
```

The trees are leaving the house for the open forest — impossible in reality, so the poem is **symbolic**: the trees stand for women ready to leave confinement and fill the world again. The empty forest, with no birds, insects or shadows, is humanity waiting to come alive — and by morning it will be full and bright.

## Stanza 2 — The struggle to break free

```stanza
All night the roots work
to disengage themselves from the cracks
in the veranda floor
The leaves strain toward the glass
small twigs stiff with exertion
long-cramped boughs shuffling under the roof
like newly discharged patients
half-dazed, moving
to the clinic doors
```

All night the roots tear free from the veranda floor, leaves press toward the glass, and cramped branches shuffle under the roof — compared to **newly discharged patients**, half-dazed yet hurrying out of the clinic. Every part of the tree strains with the desperate effort of women determined to change their lives.

## Stanza 3 — The poet watches

```stanza
I sit inside, doors open to the veranda
writing long letters
in which I scarcely mention the departure
of the forest from the house
The night is fresh, the whole moon shines
in a sky still open
the smell of leaves and lichen
still reaches like a voice into the rooms
```

The poet sits writing long letters, barely mentioning the great departure happening around her. The night is clear, the full moon shines, and the smell of leaves and lichen drifts indoors **like a voice** — nature itself calling out for freedom from the house.

## Stanza 4 — The breaking out

```stanza
My head is full of whispers
which tomorrow will be silent
Listen. The glass is breaking
The trees are stumbling forward
into the night. Winds rush to meet them
The moon is broken like a mirror
its pieces flash now in the crown
of the tallest oak
```

Her head fills with the trees' restless whispers — sounds gone by tomorrow. Glass shatters, trees stumble into the night, and winds rush to meet them. The moon looks **broken like a mirror**, its pieces flashing in the tallest oak. The escape is complete: noisy, urgent and unstoppable.

```keys
- Trees symbolise women leaving domestic confinement for freedom and purpose.
- Each stanza stages the escape: waiting forest, night-long struggle, quiet witness, violent breaking out.
- Key images: discharged patients, the voice-like smell of leaves, the mirror-broken moon.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the central idea of The Trees?

A: Confined trees breaking out of the house symbolise women breaking free toward liberty and fulfilment.
