One-paragraph revision of The Proposal by Anton Chekhov: a one-act farce in which a marriage proposal keeps collapsing into quarrels.

## Summary

The play is a one-act farce that humorously exposes the foolishness, pride, and quarrelsome nature of wealthy people. The story revolves around Ivan Vassilevitch Lomov, a thirty-five-year-old wealthy landowner who visits his neighbour, Stepan Stepanovitch Chubukov, to ask for the hand of his twenty-five-year-old daughter, Natalya Stepanovna, in marriage. Chubukov is delighted when he learns about Lomov's intention and readily agrees to the proposal.

However, before Lomov can propose to Natalya, they begin arguing over the ownership of Oxen Meadows, a small piece of land located between their properties. Both insist that the land belongs to them, and the disagreement soon turns into a heated quarrel involving accusations and insults. Chubukov joins the argument and supports his daughter's claim, making the situation even worse. Lomov becomes extremely agitated and suffers from palpitations, eventually leaving the house without making his proposal.

When Natalya learns from her father that Lomov had actually come to propose marriage, she becomes desperate and insists that he be brought back immediately. Chubukov manages to bring Lomov back, and Natalya quickly agrees that Oxen Meadows belong to him. However, instead of discussing their marriage peacefully, Lomov and Natalya soon begin another argument, this time over whose hunting dog is better: Lomov's Guess or Natalya's Squeezer.

They exchange insults and continue quarrelling until Lomov becomes so distressed that he collapses, and Natalya believes he is dead. Chubukov panics but eventually discovers that Lomov is alive. In an attempt to end the chaos, he hastily blesses the couple and urges them to kiss and get married. Although Lomov and Natalya finally accept the proposal, they immediately resume arguing about their dogs, even after their engagement.

```keys
- Chekhov satirises the obsession with property, social status, and material interests among wealthy families.
- The play shows how pride, stubbornness, and an inability to control one's temper can turn trivial disagreements into major conflicts.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What do Lomov and Natalya quarrel about in The Proposal?

A: First the ownership of Oxen Meadows, then the superiority of their hunting dogs Guess and Squeezer, with Chubukov joining the first quarrel.

Q: How does The Proposal end?

A: Chubukov hastily blesses the engaged couple, yet Lomov and Natalya immediately resume arguing about their dogs.
