One-paragraph revision of A Question of Trust by Victor Canning: the respectable thief outwitted by a cleverer thief.

## Summary

The story revolves around Horace Danby, a fifty-year-old man who appears to be a respectable citizen but is actually a thief. He runs a successful business of making locks and employs two assistants. Although he is generally considered honest, he has a peculiar habit of robbing a safe every year to fulfil his passion for collecting rare and expensive books. He carefully plans each robbery and steals enough money to support his hobby for the next twelve months.

One summer, he decides to rob a house called Shotover Grange, where he expects to find jewellery worth fifteen thousand pounds. After studying the house for two weeks, he enters it when the servants are away. However, while attempting to open the safe, he is interrupted by a young woman dressed in red. She speaks confidently and convinces Horace that she is the lady of the house. She threatens to call the police but eventually offers to let him go if he opens the safe for her because she claims to have forgotten its combination.

Believing her story, Horace removes his gloves and opens the safe, handing over the jewels to her. The woman cleverly deceives him and escapes with the jewellery. Horace keeps his promise not to steal for two days, but his desire to purchase more books soon makes him consider another robbery. Before he can act, the police arrest him for the theft at Shotover Grange. His fingerprints, left behind because he had removed his gloves, become crucial evidence against him.

When Horace explains that the lady of the house had asked him to open the safe, nobody believes him. The real lady, who is approximately sixty years old, denies his story. Horace is eventually imprisoned and becomes an assistant librarian in the prison. He frequently remembers the clever young woman who tricked him and becomes angry whenever anyone mentions the saying honour among thieves.

```keys
- The story highlights the irony that a thief who carefully plans his crimes is himself deceived by another thief.
- It teaches that dishonesty has consequences, appearances can be deceptive, and misplaced trust can lead to serious trouble.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: How is Horace Danby caught at Shotover Grange?

A: He removes his gloves to help the supposed lady of the house, leaving fingerprints that become the evidence for his arrest.

Q: Who was the real culprit in A Question of Trust?

A: The young woman in red, a fellow thief posing as the owner, who tricks Horace into opening the safe and escapes with the jewels.
