One-paragraph revision of Bholi by K. A. Abbas: how education turns a mocked girl into a woman who rejects dowry.

## Summary

The story revolves around Sulekha, a girl who is nicknamed Bholi, meaning a simpleton, because of her slow mental development and stammering. When she was ten months old, she fell off a cot and possibly suffered brain damage. At the age of two, she contracted smallpox, which left deep marks on her face. She began speaking at the age of five but stammered, and other children often mocked her, making her timid and withdrawn.

Her father, Ramlal, was a prosperous farmer and a village revenue official who had seven children, but Bholi received little attention from her family because of her appearance and perceived lack of intelligence. When a primary school for girls opened in the village, the Tehsildar instructed Ramlal to send his daughters to school. Although Ramlal's wife opposed girls' education, she suggested sending Bholi because she believed that her appearance and lack of intelligence would make it difficult for her to marry.

On her first day at school, Bholi was frightened and reluctant to go, but her experience changed when she met her teacher. Unlike her family and other children, the teacher treated her with kindness, patience and encouragement. When Bholi struggled to say her name, the teacher praised her efforts and assured her that regular attendance would help her learn to speak confidently. She also gave Bholi a picture book and promised that education would help her become knowledgeable and earn the respect of others. This kindness filled Bholi with hope and marked the beginning of her transformation.

Years later, Bholi's parents arranged her marriage to Bishamber Nath, a wealthy but much older grocer who was a widower and walked with a limp. They considered the match fortunate because he had property and money and initially demanded no dowry. However, during the wedding ceremony, Bishamber noticed the pockmarks on Bholi's face and demanded five thousand rupees from her father before agreeing to marry her. Ramlal, fearing humiliation and social disgrace, reluctantly paid the money.

Having gained confidence and self-respect through education, Bholi refused to accept this humiliating treatment. She threw the wedding garland into the sacred fire, rejected Bishamber and declared that she would not marry a greedy and contemptible man. Despite her family's concern about her future, Bholi calmly assured her father that she would look after her parents in their old age and continue teaching at the same school that had transformed her life. Her teacher supported her decision with pride and satisfaction.
