One-paragraph revision of The Midnight Visitor by Robert Arthur: how Ausable defeats an armed rival without a weapon.

## Summary

The story revolves around Ausable, an unconventional secret agent who uses his intelligence and presence of mind to overcome a dangerous situation. Fowler, a young writer who is interested in stories of mystery and adventure, accompanies Ausable to his hotel room, expecting to experience the excitement associated with secret agents. However, he is disappointed because Ausable is fat, speaks with an American accent and lives in an ordinary hotel room, unlike the glamorous spies he has read about.

His disappointment soon turns into excitement when they enter the room and discover Max, a rival secret agent, standing with a pistol in his hand. Max has come to steal an important report concerning new missiles that is expected to reach Ausable that night. Although Max is armed and appears to have the upper hand, Ausable remains calm and uses his intelligence to devise a clever plan. He complains about a balcony outside his window, claiming that someone has entered his room through it before. In reality, there is no balcony, but Max believes Ausable's convincing explanation.

When someone knocks at the door, Ausable tells Max that it is the police, whom he has arranged to protect the important report. Frightened by the possibility of being caught, Max decides to escape through the supposed balcony and threatens Ausable to send the police away. As Max climbs out of the window, he loses his balance and falls, believing that he will land on the balcony. However, the balcony does not exist. The person knocking at the door turns out to be Henry, a waiter who has brought the drinks Ausable ordered.

Fowler is astonished to discover that Ausable has defeated Max without using a weapon or physical force. Through this incident, Ausable proves that intelligence, calmness and quick thinking are more effective than physical strength or weapons in a dangerous situation.

```keys
- The story highlights the importance of presence of mind, resourcefulness and the ability to remain calm under pressure.
- It challenges the stereotypical image of a secret agent: brains defeat the pistol, not glamour or force.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: How does Ausable get rid of Max?

A: He invents an imaginary balcony and claims the police are at the door, so Max jumps out of the window and falls.

Q: Who was actually knocking at Ausable's door?

A: Henry, a waiter with the drinks Ausable had ordered; there were no police at all.
