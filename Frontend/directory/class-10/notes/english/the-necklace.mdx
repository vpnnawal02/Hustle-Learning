One-paragraph revision of The Necklace by Guy de Maupassant: vanity, a borrowed necklace and the tragic irony of ten wasted years.

## Summary

The story revolves around Matilda Loisel, a beautiful woman who is dissatisfied with her modest life and dreams of wealth, luxury and social status. Although her husband is a simple clerk in the office of the Board of Education, Matilda believes she deserves comfort, elegant dresses, expensive jewellery and grand parties. One day, her husband brings home an invitation to a grand ball at the residence of the Minister of Public Instruction. Instead of feeling happy, Matilda becomes upset because she has no suitable dress to wear.

Her husband generously gives her four hundred francs, which he had saved to buy a gun, so that she can purchase a new dress. However, Matilda remains unhappy because she has no jewellery to complement her outfit. Her husband suggests that she borrow some jewellery from her wealthy friend, Madame Forestier. Matilda visits her friend and borrows a beautiful diamond necklace. At the ball, she looks elegant and attractive, receives considerable admiration and enjoys herself immensely.

She returns home with her husband in the early hours of the morning, only to discover that the necklace is missing from her neck. The couple searches everywhere but cannot find it. After unsuccessful inquiries, they decide to replace it with an identical one. They find a similar necklace worth forty thousand francs, which they purchase for thirty-six thousand francs. Matilda's husband uses eighteen thousand francs inherited from his father and borrows the remaining amount from moneylenders, accepting ruinous financial terms.

The couple returns the replacement necklace to Madame Forestier without revealing the loss. To repay their enormous debt, they dismiss their maid, move into a small attic room and spend the next ten years living in poverty. Matilda performs exhausting household chores, while her husband works extra hours and takes on additional copying work. Their difficult life transforms Matilda from a beautiful, delicate woman into a hardened and prematurely aged person.

After ten years of sacrifice, they finally repay the entire debt. One Sunday, Matilda meets Madame Forestier and decides to tell her the truth about the lost necklace and the hardships they endured to replace it. Madame Forestier is shocked and reveals that her original necklace was imitation jewellery worth no more than five hundred francs.

```keys
- The story highlights the dangers of vanity, materialism and dissatisfaction, while emphasising the importance of honesty and contentment.
- The tragic irony is that Matilda and her husband suffer for ten years to replace a necklace that was not genuinely valuable.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: Why did the Loisels suffer for ten years?

A: To secretly replace the lost necklace, they borrowed thirty-six thousand francs and lived in poverty repaying it, only to learn the original was imitation worth five hundred francs.

Q: What would honesty have changed in The Necklace?

A: Confessing the loss immediately would have revealed the necklace's true value and avoided the enormous debt and decade of hardship.
