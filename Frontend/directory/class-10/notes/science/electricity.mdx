Electricity powers homes, industries and transport. This chapter covers charges, conductors, potential, current, Ohm's law, resistance and resistivity, series and parallel combinations, electric power and Joule heating.

## Charges, conductors and insulators

- Glass rod rubbed with silk gets positive charge. Ebonite rod rubbed with wool gets negative charge. Unlike charges attract, like charges repel.
- SI unit of charge is coulomb. Proton carries +1.6 x 10-19 C, electron carries -1.6 x 10-19 C. About 6.25 x 10-18 electrons make 1 C.
- Conductors allow charges to flow, e.g. silver, copper, aluminium, graphite and human body. Silver is best. Insulators block flow, e.g. glass, rubber, plastics, dry wood, paper, mica, porcelain and dry air.
- Conductors have free electrons that move between atoms. Insulators hold electrons tightly.
- Static electricity has charges at rest, e.g. rubbed rod or lightning. Current electricity has charges in motion, e.g. household supply.

## Potential, potential difference and current

- Electric potential at a point is work done moving unit positive charge from infinity to that point, denoted V, SI unit volt. 1 V = 1 J per 1 C.
- Potential difference between two points is work done moving unit charge between them.

```formula:Potential difference
V = W / Q
```

- Voltmeter measures potential difference, always connected in parallel, has high resistance.
- When bodies at different potentials are joined by wire, charges flow until potentials equalise. This flow is electric current, driven by potential difference.
- Current is charge passing per unit time. In metals it is flow of electrons.

```formula:Electric current
I = Q / t, 1 A = 1 C / 1 s
1 mA = 10-3 A, 1 microA = 10-6 A
```

- Ammeter measures current, always in series, has very low resistance.
- Cell or battery maintains potential difference by chemical reactions. Single dry cell gives 1.5 V. It converts chemical energy to electrical energy. Bulb acts as resistor.
- Conventional current flows from positive to negative terminal. Electron flow is opposite, from negative to positive.
- Electric circuit is a continuous conducting path of wires, components and switch between cell terminals. Circuit diagram uses standard symbols.

## Ohm's law and resistance

- At constant temperature, current through a conductor is directly proportional to potential difference across it.

```formula:Ohm's law
V proportional to I
V = I x R, I = V / R, R = V / I
```

- Doubling voltage doubles current if resistance is constant. Doubling resistance halves current if voltage is constant.
- Resistance opposes current due to collisions of electrons with atoms and ions. R = V / I. SI unit ohm. 1 ohm gives 1 A from 1 V.
- V-I graph is a straight line through origin at constant temperature. Slope V / I equals resistance.
- Good conductors have very low resistance like copper and aluminium for wires. Resistors have high resistance like nichrome, manganin and constantan to control current. Insulators like rubber have extremely high resistance and protect from shocks.

## Factors affecting resistance and resistivity

- Resistance depends on length, area of cross-section, material and temperature.
- Directly proportional to length: R proportional to l. Doubling length doubles resistance.
- Inversely proportional to area: R proportional to 1 / A. Doubling area halves resistance.
- Inversely proportional to square of diameter: R proportional to 1 / d2. Doubling diameter gives one-fourth resistance.
- Nichrome has about 60 times more resistance than copper for same size. Metal resistance rises with temperature. Alloys like nichrome, manganin and constantan are almost unaffected by temperature.
- Combining: R = rho x l / A, where rho is resistivity depending on material and temperature, also called specific resistance.

```formula:Resistivity
rho = R x A / l, SI unit ohm-metre
```

- Resistivity is independent of size and shape. Alloys have much higher resistivity than pure metals. Manganin about 25 times copper, constantan about 30 times, nichrome about 60 times.
- Heating elements use alloys because high resistivity gives more heat and they do not oxidise easily at high temperature. Nichrome is used in irons, toasters, kettles, heaters and geysers.
- Conductors have very low resistivity, insulators very high. Metal resistivity rises with temperature. Semiconductors like silicon and germanium lie between and their resistivity falls with temperature. They are used in solar cells and transistors.

## Series and parallel combinations

- Resistors in series increase total resistance. Resistors in parallel decrease total resistance.
- Series is end-to-end connection. Parallel connects between same two points.

| Feature | Series | Parallel |
| ------- | ------ | -------- |
| Total resistance | R = R1 + R2 + R3 | 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3 |
| Current | Same through each | Splits, total I = I1 + I2 + I3 |
| Voltage | Splits, V = V1 + V2 + V3 | Same across each, equal to battery |

- Parallel total is always less than the smallest individual. Example: 6 ohm and 12 ohm in parallel give 4 ohm.

## Electric power and commercial unit

- Electric power is rate of doing electrical work or consuming energy. SI unit watt. 1 W = 1 J per 1 s. 1 kW = 1000 W.

```formula:Electric power
P = W / t
P = V x I, P = I2 x R
```

- Energy consumed equals power times time. In watt-hour if power is in watts and time in hours.
- SI unit of energy is joule. Commercial unit is kilowatt-hour. 1 kWh = 1000 W x 3600 s = 3.6 x 10-6 J. One unit of electricity equals 1 kWh.
- Meter measures consumption in kWh. Energy in kWh equals power in kW times time in hours. Cost equals units times cost per unit.

## Heating effect of current

- High-resistance wire like nichrome becomes hot when current flows, converting electrical energy to heat. Resistance acts like friction producing heat.
- Work W = Q x V, with Q = I x t and V = I x R, gives W = I2 x R x t. If all becomes heat, H = I2 x R x t. This is Joule law of heating.
- Heat is proportional to square of current, resistance and time. Doubling current gives four times heat. Doubling resistance or time doubles heat.
- Use amperes, ohms and seconds to get joules. Alternatively E = P x t.
- Fans convert most energy to motion with little heat. Heaters, kettles, dryers, immersion rods and geysers convert most energy to heat using nichrome coils.

> [!TIP] Exam tip: series divides voltage and keeps current same, parallel divides current and keeps voltage same — write this line before numericals.
