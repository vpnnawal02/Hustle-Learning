Reproduction produces offspring of the same kind and ensures continuation of species. This chapter covers asexual methods like fission and budding, vegetative propagation, cell division, human reproduction and sexual reproduction in plants.

## Types of reproduction

- Reproduction multiplies numbers and increases population.
- Asexual reproduction involves one organism producing two or more individuals on its own. Seen in unicellular organisms, some multicellular organisms and some plants.
- Sexual reproduction involves male and female individuals producing gametes that fuse to form a new organism.

## Asexual reproduction

### Fission

- Common in unicellular organisms.
- Binary fission gives two daughter cells, e.g. Paramecium.
- Multiple fission gives many daughter cells, e.g. Plasmodium.
- Planes of fission differ among organisms.

### Budding

- A small cyst-like outgrowth forms on parent and gives a new individual.
- Bud may stay attached as in yeast, or separate as in Hydra.

### Regeneration and fragmentation

- Regeneration regrows a lost organ or body part, e.g. lizard tail.
- Fragmentation breaks the organism into pieces, each growing into a new organism, e.g. Planaria, Hydra.

### Spore formation

- Fungi make spores inside sporangia that grow into new individuals when dispersed from fruiting bodies.
- Thick outer layer protects spores in adverse conditions. They germinate in suitable conditions.

### Vegetative propagation

- Vegetative parts like leaves, stems and roots give new plants.
- Natural methods: leaves e.g. Bryophyllum, stem e.g. turmeric and ginger, runners or stolon e.g. grass and strawberry, bulbs e.g. onion and lily.
- Artificial methods: cutting, grafting, layering and tissue culture.
- Can be natural or artificial.

## Cell division for sexual reproduction

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
| ------- | ------- | ------- |
| Occurs in | Somatic cells | Sex cells |
| Chromosomes | Maintained | Halved |
| Daughter cells | Two diploid | Four haploid |
| Used for | Growth, repair, asexual reproduction, regeneration | Gamete formation for sexual reproduction |

## Human male reproductive system

- Main organs are a pair of testes that make sperms and testosterone.
- Testes lie in scrotal sacs outside body. Seminiferous tubules make sperms which mature in epididymis. Leydig or interstitial cells secrete testosterone.
- Accessory glands: prostate gland and seminal vesicles make semen and nourish sperm. Penis with urethra is copulatory organ.
- Ducts: vas deferens carries sperm from each testis to urethra. Urethra is common passage for semen and urine.

## Human female reproductive system

- Consists of a pair of ovaries, a pair of fallopian tubes or oviducts, plus uterus and vagina.
- Ovaries make eggs or ova plus oestrogen and progesterone.
- Uterus is site of foetal development. Vagina receives sperm. Oviducts carry ovum from ovaries to uterus.

## Menstrual cycle and human reproduction

- Menstruation is cyclic release of ovum and its removal with blood-rich endometrium when fertilisation does not happen.
- Controlled by pituitary hormones LH and FSH and ovarian hormones oestrogen and progesterone. Cycle repeats about every 28 days.
- Humans reproduce sexually. Sperm fuses with egg to form zygote that grows into progeny.

## Reproductive health and contraception

- Reproductive health covers prevention of STDs and unwanted pregnancy, plus awareness of the reproductive system.
- Contraceptives prevent pregnancy and some prevent STDs. Types: mechanical barriers, hormonal or chemical methods, surgical methods.

| Method | How it works | Note |
| ------ | ------------ | ---- |
| Coitus interruptus | Withdrawal before ejaculation | Very unreliable |
| Rhythm method | Avoid coitus in fertile period | Unreliable |
| Condoms | Barrier stops semen entering female tract | Effective and prevents STDs |
| Diaphragms | Barrier inside female tract | Blocks semen entry |
| Contraceptive pills | Change hormones to stop ovulation | Chemical method |
| Emergency pill | Taken after coitus to prevent implantation | Alters hormones quickly |
| IUD, intrauterine device | Inserted in uterus for a couple of years | Prevents implantation of zygote |
| Sterilisation, vasectomy in males and tubal ligation in females | Surgical, permanent | For both sexes |

## Sexual reproduction in flowering plants

- Occurs through flowers. Androecium and gynoecium are essential whorls.
- Non-essential whorls: sepals protect bud and photosynthesise if green, petals attract insects for pollination.
- Essential whorls: androecium makes pollen with male gametes, gynoecium makes ovules with female gametes. Bisexual flowers have both, unisexual have one.
- Stamen has anther plus filament. Anther makes haploid pollen. Pistil has stigma, style and ovary.

### Pollination

- Transfer of pollen from anthers to stigma, required for fertilisation.
- Self-pollination or autogamy is within same flower or same plant. Cross-pollination or allogamy is to another flower with agents like water, wind, insects, birds and bats.

### Fertilisation in plants

- Pollens germinate on stigma and produce two male nuclei. Ovule has egg cell and two polar nuclei.
- One male nucleus fuses with two polar nuclei to form triploid endosperm. Other fuses with egg to form zygote that becomes embryo.
- After fertilisation ovary becomes fruit, ovules become seeds, other parts wither.
