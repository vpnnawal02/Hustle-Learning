The human eye is a natural optical instrument using a convex lens to form images on the retina. This chapter covers eye structure, accommodation, defects and correction, prism dispersion, rainbow, atmospheric refraction and scattering.

## The human eye

- Eyeball is approximately spherical with diameter about 2.5 cm.
- Light enters through cornea, the transparent bulging front part with convex outer surface.
- Iris is the coloured ring behind cornea that controls pupil size. Pupil is the central opening that appears black.
- Eye lens is transparent, soft, flexible convex lens of protein material. Suspensory ligaments hold it and connect to ciliary muscles.
- Ciliary muscles change lens thickness and focal length to focus at different distances, unlike a camera lens of fixed focal length.
- Retina is the light-sensitive membrane at the back where the image forms, like camera film.
- Rods respond to light intensity and help in dim light. Cones respond to colours in bright light. Owls have many rods for night vision.
- Optic nerve carries electrical signals to brain. Blind spot where nerve leaves has no rods or cones.
- Aqueous humour fills space between cornea and lens. Vitreous humour fills space between lens and retina and supports the eye.
- Cornea converges most incoming light, eye lens finally focuses it as a real and inverted image on retina. Brain interprets it as erect.
- Iris adjusts pupil automatically: contracts in bright light, expands in dim light. This protects from glare and improves vision when moving between light and dark.

## Power of accommodation and range of vision

- Accommodation is the ability to focus distant and nearby objects by changing lens focal length.
- Distant objects: ciliary muscles relax, ligaments tighten, lens becomes thin with large focal length and low power. This is the unaccommodated state.
- Nearby objects: muscles contract, ligaments loosen, lens becomes thick with small focal length and high power. This is the accommodated state.
- Near point of normal eye is 25 cm, also called least distance of distinct vision. Far point is at infinity. Hold books about 25 cm away.
- Power of accommodation of normal eye is about 4 dioptres.

## Defects of vision

### Myopia

- Person sees nearby clearly but not distant objects. Far point is at finite distance instead of infinity.
- Caused by excessive lens power from short focal length, or elongation of eyeball.
- Distant image forms in front of retina.
- Corrected with concave diverging lens of suitable power, which forms a virtual image at the far point of the myopic eye.

```formula:Lens formula for myopia
1/f = 1/v - 1/u, with u = -infinity, so f = v
P = 1/f, f in metres, P in dioptres
```

### Hypermetropia

- Person sees distant clearly but not nearby objects. Near point is beyond 25 cm.
- Caused by low lens power from large focal length, shortening of eyeball, or weak ciliary muscles.
- Nearby image forms behind retina.
- Corrected with convex converging lens, which forms a virtual image at the near point of the hypermetropic eye.

```formula:Lens formula for hypermetropia
1/f = 1/v - 1/u, with u = -25 cm, v negative
P = 1/f
```

### Presbyopia and cataract

- Presbyopia occurs in old age from weakening ciliary muscles and loss of lens flexibility. Near point moves away. Corrected with convex spectacles.
- Person with both myopia and hypermetropia uses bifocal lenses: upper concave for distance, lower convex for reading.
- Contact lenses or surgery can also correct refractive defects.
- Cataract makes the lens cloudy or opaque, usually in old age, causing blurred vision. Treated by surgical removal and artificial lens. Spectacles cannot correct it.

## Two eyes, eye donation and prism

- One eye gives about 150 degree horizontal field, two eyes about 180 degrees, giving wider view.
- Two eyes view from slightly different angles, brain combines them into three-dimensional vision for better distance judgement.
- Eye donation restores vision through corneal transplant. People of any age can pledge. Spectacle users, cataract-operated, diabetic, hypertensive or asthmatic persons can donate if medically eligible. Certain infections like AIDS, hepatitis B or C and rabies may disqualify.
- Eye bank must be informed within 4 to 6 hours after death. Removal takes 10 to 15 minutes at home or hospital without disfigurement. Unsuitable eyes may be used for research.
- Triangular glass prism has two triangular ends and three rectangular faces. Angle between refracting faces is angle of prism.
- In a slab opposite faces are parallel so emergent ray stays parallel. In a prism faces are inclined so emergent ray deviates.
- Light bends towards normal entering glass, away from normal leaving glass. Ray through prism bends towards base. Angle of deviation is between original incident direction and emergent ray.

## Dispersion, recombination and rainbow

- Newton in 1665 showed white light has seven colours: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red.
- Spectrum is the band formed on screen. Dispersion is splitting of white light by a transparent medium.
- Different colours travel at different speeds in glass and deviate differently. Red travels fastest and deviates least at the top. Violet travels slowest and deviates most at the bottom. From red to violet frequency increases and wavelength decreases.
- Recombination: second identical prism in inverted position recombines seven colours into white light by equal and opposite deviation.
- Rainbow is a natural spectrum from dispersion by raindrops, seen opposite the Sun with back to Sun.
- Each drop acts as a tiny prism. Sunlight refracts, disperses, totally internally reflects, then refracts out. Red appears at top, violet at bottom. Also visible in fountain spray with Sun behind observer.

## Atmospheric refraction

- Bending of light through atmospheric layers of different optical densities. Cooler layers are denser, warmer layers rarer. Upper atmosphere is rarer, lower denser under normal conditions.
- Hot air above fire causes irregular refraction, so objects appear to waver.
- Twinkling of stars: starlight refracts through changing layers, brightness varies. Stars are point sources so variation is noticeable. Planets are extended sources of many points whose variations cancel, so they do not twinkle.
- Stars appear higher than actual position because light bends towards normal entering denser air, but brain assumes straight path. Same for Sun.
- Advance sunrise and delayed sunset: Sun visible about 2 minutes before sunrise and 2 minutes after sunset even below horizon, adding about 4 minutes of daylight. Sun appears flattened at horizon.

## Scattering of light

- Tyndall effect is scattering by tiny particles, seen when sunlight enters a dusty room or passes through forest canopy mist.
- Blue light has shorter wavelength and scatters more than red. Blue scatters about 10 times more than red.
- Large particles like dust and water drops scatter all colours equally so light appears white. Very small particles like nitrogen and oxygen molecules scatter blue much more than red.
- Sky is blue because air molecules scatter blue strongly in all directions into our eyes. Direct sun stays nearly white. In space with no atmosphere sky appears black.
- Red is used for danger signals because longest wavelength scatters least in fog and smoke and is seen farthest.
- Sun appears red at sunrise and sunset because light travels longer through atmosphere, blue scatters away, mainly red reaches eyes. At noon path is short so Sun appears nearly white.

> [!TIP] Exam tip: red deviates least and scatters least, violet deviates most and scatters most — use this pair for prism and sky questions.
