All organisms respond to stimuli such as light, heat, touch, pain and gravity. Animals use nervous and endocrine systems, while plants mainly use hormones. Coordination is organs working together to produce a proper response.

## Receptors and nervous system basics

- Multicellular animals except sponges have neurons to respond and coordinate. Hydra uses a nerve network. Vertebrates use nervous plus endocrine systems.
- Five sense organs: eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin.
- Receptors detect specific stimuli and convert them into nerve impulses.

| Receptor | Detects |
| -------- | ------- |
| Photoreceptors | Light |
| Phonoreceptors | Sound |
| Olfactory receptors | Smell |
| Gustatory receptors | Taste |
| Thermoreceptors | Heat and cold |

- Sensory nerves carry impulses from receptors to brain and spinal cord. Motor nerves carry instructions to effectors. Muscles and glands are effectors.

## Human nervous system and neurons

- Controls voluntary actions like writing and cycling, and involuntary actions like heartbeat and breathing. Also coordinates systems, e.g. food in mouth triggers saliva.
- Neuron has cell body with nucleus, dendrites that receive impulses towards the body, and axon that carries impulses away, often with myelin sheath.
- Sensory neurons carry impulses to brain and spinal cord. Motor neurons carry impulses to effectors. Relay neurons connect neurons inside brain and spinal cord.
- Synapse is the gap between neurons. Neurotransmitters carry the impulse across and ensure one-way flow.

## Nerves and divisions

- Main organs: brain in skull, spinal cord in backbone, nerves connecting them to body.
- Cranial nerves connect brain to head. Spinal nerves connect spinal cord to rest of body. Visceral nerves connect to internal organs. They carry both sensory and motor fibres, so called mixed nerves.
- Central nervous system is brain plus spinal cord. Peripheral nervous system is all nerves.
- Voluntary nervous system controls conscious actions. Autonomic nervous system controls involuntary actions like heartbeat, breathing, digestion and sweating.

## Reflex action and reflex arc

- Reflex action is rapid, automatic, involuntary response that protects the body, e.g. withdrawing hand from hot object.
- Pathway: Receptor to Sensory neuron to Relay neuron in spinal cord to Motor neuron to Effector muscle.
- Most are spinal reflexes controlled by spinal cord, though impulse also reaches brain for awareness. Some are cerebral reflexes controlled by brain, e.g. pupil shrinking in bright light.
- Cerebral pupil reflex: light receptors signal brain, brain orders circular iris muscles to contract, pupil shrinks, less light enters.

## How muscles move

- Motor impulses make muscle cells contract through special proteins that shorten the cells. Contracting muscles pull bones, e.g. biceps pulls forearm up. Contraction is reversible.

## Central nervous system: brain

- Brain is protected by cranium, three meninges and cerebrospinal fluid that absorbs shocks.
- Three regions: forebrain, midbrain, hindbrain.
- Forebrain has cerebrum for thinking, learning, reasoning, intelligence and memory. Association areas store information, sensory areas receive sensations, motor areas order movement. It coordinates voluntary actions.
- Midbrain controls head, neck and trunk reflexes to visual and auditory stimuli, plus pupil size and lens shape.
- Hindbrain: pons regulates respiration, cerebellum maintains posture, balance and smooth movements, medulla controls heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure, peristalsis, and reflexes like swallowing, coughing, sneezing and vomiting.

## Spinal cord

- Cylindrical structure from medulla, protected by vertebral column and meninges. 31 pairs of spinal nerves arise from it.
- Controls spinal reflexes and carries impulses to and from brain.
- Brain functions: receive sensory impulses, send motor instructions, process information, coordinate activities, store memory for learning and intelligence.

## Hormones and endocrine glands

- Hormones are chemical messengers secreted in small amounts by ductless endocrine glands directly into blood, acting on target organs.
- Exocrine glands have ducts, e.g. salivary glands. Endocrine glands are ductless.
- Pancreas, testes and ovaries are mixed: pancreas gives insulin to blood and pancreatic juice to duct, testes give testosterone plus sperms, ovaries give oestrogen and progesterone plus ova.

| Gland | Location | Hormone | Main role |
| ----- | -------- | ------- | --------- |
| Hypothalamus | Brain | Releasing and inhibitory hormones | Regulates pituitary |
| Pituitary | Below brain | Growth hormone and regulators | Controls growth and other glands. Deficiency causes dwarfism, excess causes overgrowth |
| Thyroid | Near windpipe | Thyroxine | Controls carbohydrate, fat and protein metabolism. Needs iodine. Deficiency causes goitre. Use iodised salt |
| Thymus | Lower neck, upper chest | Immune hormones | Develops immune system. Large in children, shrinks after puberty |
| Pancreas | Below stomach | Insulin | Lowers blood glucose. Deficiency causes diabetes |
| Adrenal | Above kidneys | Adrenaline | Emergency hormone: raises heartbeat, breathing, pressure and glucose for fight or flight |
| Testes | Male | Testosterone | Male organs, deeper voice, beard, plus sperm production |
| Ovaries | Female | Oestrogen, progesterone | Female organs and characters, menstrual and pregnancy control, plus egg production |

> [!TIP] Exam tip: remember adrenal as glands of emergency and pituitary as master gland regulating others.

## Control in plants

- Plants have no nerves or sense organs but detect light, gravity, water, chemicals and touch. They respond slowly mainly by growth controlled by phytohormones.
- Dormancy is resting state with low metabolism. Seeds and buds stay dormant until hormones and suitable conditions break it for germination or growth.

| Plant hormone | Promotes or inhibits | Key roles |
| ------------- | -------------------- | --------- |
| Auxins | Promote | Cell enlargement, fruit growth, bending to light and gravity |
| Gibberellins | Promote | Shoot elongation, fruit growth, breaking seed and bud dormancy |
| Cytokinins | Promote | Cell division, stomata opening, delaying leaf ageing, breaking dormancy |
| Abscisic acid | Inhibit | Dormancy, closing stomata, wilting and fall of leaves, flowers, fruits |

## Plant movements: tropic versus nastic

- Parts like stems, roots and leaves move by unequal growth under hormones. Auxin on shaded side makes stem bend to light.
- Tropisms depend on stimulus direction and are growth movements. Nastic movements do not depend on direction and may or may not involve growth.

| Tropism | Stimulus | Example |
| ------- | -------- | ------- |
| Phototropism | Light | Shoot to light positive, root away negative |
| Geotropism | Gravity | Root down positive, shoot up negative |
| Chemotropism | Chemical | Pollen tube to ovule |
| Hydrotropism | Water | Root to water positive |
| Thigmotropism | Touch | Tendril winding around support |

- Phototropism mechanism: uniform light gives equal auxin and growth. Side light piles auxin on shaded side, shaded side grows faster, stem bends to light. In roots auxin inhibits growth, so shaded side grows slower and root bends away.
- Geotropism shown by horizontal potted plant: roots bend down, stem bends up.
- Hydrotropism shown by two troughs: uniformly watered root stays straight, root near buried clay pot bends towards water.
- Tendrils are thin climbing threads. Stem tendrils in bitter gourd, bottle gourd, grapevine. Leaf tendrils in pea, glory lily. Contact side grows slower, tendril coils.
- Nastic examples: Mimosa pudica folds on touch, dandelion opens in morning light and closes in evening, moonflower closes in day and opens at night. Tropic is directional, nastic is non-directional and seen in leaves and petals.
- Thigmonasty in sensitive plant: touch creates electrical impulse, water moves from one half of pulvinus to intercellular spaces of other half, pulvinus loses firmness, leaf folds and droops. Water returns in 15 to 30 minutes.
- Photonasty in petals: inner surface grows more to open, outer surface grows more to close. These are growth movements, unlike Mimosa folding.
