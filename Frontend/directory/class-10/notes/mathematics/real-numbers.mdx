The opening chapter of Class 10 Maths builds the number-system base used across algebra. Master the two theorems and the standard proof pattern.

## Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be factorised as a product of primes, uniquely apart from order. This gives HCF as the product of smallest powers of common primes and LCM as the product of greatest powers of all primes.

```formula:HCF × LCM
HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b (for two positive integers)
```

## Proving irrationality

Standard pattern: assume the number is rational (p/q in lowest terms), derive a contradiction about a prime dividing both p and q, and conclude irrationality.

Q: Prove that √5 is irrational. {demo}

A: Assume √5 = a/b with co-prime positive integers a, b. Then 5b² = a², so 5 divides a; write a = 5c, giving 5c² = b², so 5 divides b too — contradicting co-primality. Hence √5 is irrational. (Demonstration outline.)
