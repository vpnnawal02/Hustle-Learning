## Stanza 1: Amanda's Behaviour and the Constant Instructions

```stanza
Don't bite your nails, Amanda!
Don't hunch your shoulders, Amanda!
Stop that slouching and sit up straight,
Amanda!
```

**Line 1: "Don't bite your nails, Amanda!"** The poem begins with an adult instructing Amanda not to bite her nails. Nail-biting is often considered a bad habit, and the adult wants Amanda to stop doing it. However, the direct command also introduces the controlling tone that continues throughout the poem. Instead of gently explaining things to Amanda, the adult repeatedly tells her what she must or must not do. The exclamation mark after Amanda's name emphasises the speaker's impatience or urgency. This line establishes the pattern of constant instructions that Amanda experiences.

**Line 2: "Don't hunch your shoulders, Amanda!"** The adult instructs Amanda not to hunch her shoulders. The word hunch means to bend or round one's shoulders forward rather than keeping the back straight. The adult wants Amanda to maintain proper posture. Although this may be a reasonable expectation, the repeated commands suggest that Amanda is being corrected frequently. The poet presents the instruction as another example of the restrictions imposed on Amanda.

**Line 3: "Stop that slouching and sit up straight,"** The adult continues correcting Amanda's posture. The word slouching means sitting or standing with a drooping posture, often with the shoulders bent forward. The adult expects Amanda to sit upright and maintain good posture. The expression "Stop that" conveys a commanding tone. It suggests that the adult is becoming impatient with Amanda's behaviour. Instead of allowing Amanda to relax naturally, the adult constantly monitors how she sits and behaves.

**Line 4: "Amanda!"** The repetition of Amanda's name emphasises the adult's continuous attention to her behaviour. The name appears repeatedly throughout the poem, creating the impression that Amanda is constantly being called upon, corrected and instructed. This repetition also contributes to the poem's rhythm and highlights the pressure Amanda experiences.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 1:** The adult repeatedly tells Amanda to stop biting her nails, correct her posture and sit properly. Although these instructions concern ordinary behaviour, their constant repetition creates an atmosphere of control and restriction. The stanza introduces the central conflict of the poem: the adult wants Amanda to behave according to certain expectations, while Amanda longs for freedom from constant supervision.

## Stanza 2: Amanda's Fantasy of Being a Mermaid

```stanza
(There is a languid, emerald sea,
where the sole inhabitant is me —
a mermaid, drifting blissfully.)
```

**Line 1: "There is a languid, emerald sea,"** Amanda escapes from the adult's instructions by imagining a peaceful sea. The word languid means relaxed, slow-moving or lacking energy. It suggests a calm and unhurried environment. The word emerald refers to a precious green gemstone. Here, it describes the beautiful green colour of the sea. Together, these words create an image of a calm, beautiful and peaceful sea. Amanda imagines a place where there are no commands, no criticism and no one telling her how to behave. The sea represents the freedom and tranquillity she desires.

**Line 2: "where the sole inhabitant is me —"** The word sole means only, while inhabitant means someone who lives in a particular place. Amanda imagines that she is the only person living in this peaceful sea. She does not want anyone else to disturb her. Her fantasy reflects her desire for privacy and independence. In reality, Amanda is constantly being instructed by an adult. In her imaginary world, however, she has complete control over her surroundings. The line suggests that Amanda wants to escape not necessarily from the adult herself, but from the constant supervision and restrictions she experiences.

**Line 3: "a mermaid, drifting blissfully."** Amanda imagines herself as a mermaid floating peacefully through the sea. A mermaid is a mythical creature with the upper body of a human and the tail of a fish. The word drifting suggests moving slowly and effortlessly with the water. The word blissfully means extremely happily or peacefully. As a mermaid, Amanda can move freely without anyone telling her what to do. Her fantasy represents her desire to experience peace, independence and freedom from constant instructions.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 2:** Amanda imagines herself as a mermaid living alone in a calm, green sea. In this imaginary world, she can drift peacefully without anyone disturbing her. The fantasy reveals her desire for solitude and freedom from the constant instructions she receives in reality.

## Stanza 3: More Instructions from the Adult

```stanza
Did you finish your homework, Amanda?
Did you tidy your room, Amanda?
I thought I told you to clean your shoes,
Amanda!
```

**Line 1: "Did you finish your homework, Amanda?"** The adult asks Amanda whether she has completed her homework. Homework is an important part of a student's education, and the adult expects Amanda to fulfil her academic responsibilities. However, the question also continues the pattern of constant monitoring. Amanda is repeatedly asked about her tasks rather than being given space to manage them independently. The poet shows how ordinary parental concerns can become burdensome when they are expressed through continuous questioning.

**Line 2: "Did you tidy your room, Amanda?"** The adult asks Amanda whether she has cleaned and organised her room. The word tidy means to arrange things neatly and remove disorder. The adult expects Amanda to maintain cleanliness and order in her personal space. Once again, the instruction focuses on what Amanda has not necessarily completed. The repeated questions create the impression that Amanda is under constant supervision, with little opportunity to relax.

**Line 3: "I thought I told you to clean your shoes,"** The adult reminds Amanda that she was previously instructed to clean her shoes. The phrase "I thought I told you" suggests frustration and impatience. The adult believes Amanda has not followed an earlier instruction. The line also indicates that the adult expects Amanda to remember and obey every command. Although cleaning shoes is a reasonable responsibility, the tone suggests that the adult is becoming irritated.

**Line 4: "Amanda!"** The repeated use of Amanda's name reinforces the adult's commanding tone. It also creates a contrast with Amanda's imaginary world, where she is free from constant reminders. The repetition suggests that Amanda has little opportunity to escape the adult's attention, even when the instructions concern routine tasks.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 3:** The adult questions Amanda about her homework, room and shoes. These questions reflect expectations concerning schoolwork, cleanliness and responsibility. However, the repeated instructions also highlight the pressure Amanda experiences. She is constantly reminded of her duties, leaving her longing for an escape into a world where nobody supervises her.

## Stanza 4: Amanda's Fantasy of Being an Orphan

```stanza
(I am an orphan, roaming the street.
I pattern soft dust with my hushed, bare feet.
The silence is golden, the freedom is sweet.)
```

**Line 1: "I am an orphan, roaming the street."** Amanda imagines herself as an orphan wandering freely through the streets. An orphan is a child whose parents have died. In her fantasy, Amanda imagines a life without parental supervision or constant instructions. The word roaming means wandering freely from place to place without a fixed destination. Although being an orphan would ordinarily involve significant difficulties and emotional challenges, Amanda focuses on the freedom she imagines such a life would provide. This fantasy reveals how strongly she desires independence and relief from the constant demands placed on her. It does not necessarily mean that Amanda genuinely wants her parents to disappear. Rather, she wants freedom from the pressure she experiences at home.

**Line 2: "I pattern soft dust with my hushed, bare feet."** Amanda imagines walking barefoot on soft dust and leaving patterns with her footsteps. The word pattern suggests making shapes or designs through repeated movements. The expression hushed, bare feet creates an image of quiet, gentle movement without shoes. Amanda imagines a simple life in which she can wander wherever she wishes, without having to follow rules about her appearance or behaviour. The soft dust and quiet footsteps contribute to the peaceful atmosphere of her imaginary world.

**Line 3: "The silence is golden, the freedom is sweet."** This line expresses Amanda's desire for peace and independence. The expression "silence is golden" means that silence can be extremely valuable and desirable. For Amanda, silence is precious because her real life is filled with instructions, questions and reminders. The phrase "the freedom is sweet" means that independence would bring her great happiness. The word sweet is used figuratively to describe the pleasure of being free. Together, the expressions show that Amanda values peace and freedom more than constant supervision.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 4:** Amanda imagines herself as an orphan who can wander freely through the streets without anyone giving her instructions. She enjoys the silence and independence of this imaginary life. The fantasy reveals her desire to escape the restrictions she experiences at home and enjoy greater personal freedom.

## Stanza 5: The Adult's Warning About Chocolate

```stanza
Don't eat that chocolate, Amanda!
Remember your acne, Amanda!
Will you please look at me when I'm speaking to you,
Amanda!
```

**Line 1: "Don't eat that chocolate, Amanda!"** The adult tells Amanda not to eat chocolate. The instruction suggests that the adult is concerned about Amanda's eating habits or believes that chocolate might negatively affect her skin. The direct command continues the pattern of restrictions that runs throughout the poem. Instead of allowing Amanda to make ordinary choices independently, the adult continues to regulate her behaviour.

**Line 2: "Remember your acne, Amanda!"** The adult reminds Amanda that she has acne. Acne is a skin condition that commonly causes pimples and spots. The adult appears to believe that eating chocolate may worsen Amanda's acne and uses this concern as a reason to stop her from eating it. However, the line's main purpose is to demonstrate the constant nature of the adult's instructions, rather than to provide medical advice about acne. The reminder may also make Amanda feel self-conscious about her appearance.

**Line 3: "Will you please look at me when I'm speaking to you,"** The adult asks Amanda to make eye contact while being spoken to. The expression "Will you please" is grammatically polite, but in this context it conveys frustration because the adult has repeatedly been trying to get Amanda's attention. The adult expects Amanda to listen and respond appropriately. However, Amanda's lack of engagement may indicate that she is mentally escaping into her imaginary world. She seems more interested in her fantasies than in the instructions being given to her.

**Line 4: "Amanda!"** The repeated use of Amanda's name emphasises the adult's impatience. It also reinforces the contrast between Amanda's desire for independence and the adult's expectation of obedience.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 5:** The adult warns Amanda against eating chocolate because of her acne and demands that she pay attention while being spoken to. These instructions show that the adult monitors Amanda's habits, appearance and behaviour. Amanda's continued mental escape into fantasy suggests that the constant instructions are making her feel restricted and disconnected from the adult.

## Stanza 6: Amanda's Fantasy of Being Rapunzel

```stanza
(I am Rapunzel, I have not a care;
Life in a tower is tranquil and rare;
I'll certainly never let down my bright hair!)
```

**Important Note About the Text:** The standard NCERT text of this stanza is as printed above.

**Line 1: "I am Rapunzel, I have not a care;"** Amanda imagines herself as Rapunzel, a character from a famous fairy tale. In the traditional story, Rapunzel is kept in a tall tower and has exceptionally long hair. The expression "I have not a care" means that Amanda imagines herself free from worries. Although Rapunzel is confined in a tower in the fairy tale, Amanda focuses on the possibility of living alone and peacefully. Her fantasy is not necessarily about being imprisoned. Instead, she imagines a place where nobody constantly tells her what to do. The line reveals her desire for emotional peace and independence.

**Line 2: "Life in a tower is tranquil and rare;"** The word tranquil means calm, peaceful and free from disturbance. Amanda imagines that living alone in a tower would allow her to enjoy silence and freedom from other people's demands. The word rare suggests that such a peaceful life would be unusual or special. The irony is that Rapunzel's tower is traditionally a place of confinement. However, Amanda imagines it as a refuge from the restrictions she experiences in her everyday life. This contrast highlights the depth of her desire for privacy and independence.

**Line 3: "I'll certainly never let down my bright hair!"** In the traditional Rapunzel story, Rapunzel lets down her long hair so that someone can climb up to her tower. Amanda declares that she will never let down her hair. This means she does not want anyone to climb up and disturb her peaceful isolation. The line symbolises her desire to remain independent and prevent others from entering her private world. Her fantasy reveals that Amanda wants a space where she can make her own decisions without being constantly monitored.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 6:** Amanda imagines herself as Rapunzel living peacefully in a tower. Unlike the traditional fairy-tale character, who is confined and eventually allows others to reach her, Amanda wants to remain alone and undisturbed. The fantasy reflects her desire for privacy, independence and freedom from constant instructions.

## Stanza 7: The Adult's Concern About Amanda's Mood

```stanza
Stop that sulking at once, Amanda!
You're always so moody, Amanda!
Anyone would think I nagged at you,
Amanda!
```

**Line 1: "Stop that sulking at once, Amanda!"** The adult tells Amanda to stop sulking immediately. The word sulking means remaining silent or appearing unhappy, often because one feels upset or dissatisfied. The phrase "at once" makes the instruction more forceful. It suggests that the adult expects Amanda to change her behaviour immediately. However, the command also reveals a contradiction: Amanda is being repeatedly instructed, yet she is criticised when she appears unhappy about it. The adult seems to focus on correcting Amanda's visible reactions rather than understanding the feelings behind them.

**Line 2: "You're always so moody, Amanda!"** The adult accuses Amanda of being constantly moody. The word moody refers to frequently changing moods or appearing unhappy, irritable or withdrawn. The word always exaggerates the criticism by suggesting that Amanda is unhappy all the time. This line indicates that the adult interprets Amanda's behaviour as a personal flaw rather than considering whether the repeated instructions might be contributing to her mood. The poet invites readers to consider the effect that constant criticism can have on a child.

**Line 3: "Anyone would think I nagged at you,"** The adult says that Amanda's behaviour might make other people believe that she is constantly being nagged. The word nagged means repeatedly criticising, reminding or instructing someone in an irritating or persistent manner. This line is ironic because the adult has been giving Amanda repeated instructions throughout the poem. The adult appears unaware of the contradiction between her own behaviour and her complaint about Amanda's mood. The line exposes the central conflict of the poem: the adult believes she is simply correcting Amanda, while Amanda experiences the constant instructions as restrictive and frustrating.

**Line 4: "Amanda!"** The final repetition of Amanda's name reinforces the adult's persistent attempts to control her behaviour. It also leaves the conflict unresolved. Amanda continues to seek freedom through her imagination, while the adult continues to demand obedience and a more cheerful attitude. The ending encourages readers to consider the importance of understanding children's feelings instead of responding only with criticism.

**Overall Meaning of Stanza 7:** The adult tells Amanda to stop sulking and criticises her for being moody. Ironically, the adult denies nagging even though the entire poem is filled with repeated instructions. This reveals the communication gap between the two characters. The stanza highlights how constant correction can affect a child's mood and create emotional distance between a child and an adult.
