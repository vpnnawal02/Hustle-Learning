This chapter introduces how substances change into new substances through chemical reactions, and how to represent them with balanced chemical equations.

> [!NOTE] Demonstration content: the questions below illustrate the solution format. They are practice examples, not verbatim NCERT exercise answers.

## Key ideas in this chapter

- A balanced equation has equal atoms of each element on both sides.
- Combination, decomposition, displacement and double-displacement reactions.
- Oxidation means gain of oxygen (or loss of electrons); reduction is the reverse.

## Worked examples

Q: Why should a chemical equation be balanced? {demo}

A: A balanced equation obeys the law of conservation of mass — atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction, so each element must appear the same number of times on both sides.

Q: Identify the reaction type: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu. {demo}

A: Displacement reaction — zinc, being more reactive, displaces copper from copper sulphate solution.

```keys
- Always balance last by inspection, starting with the most complex molecule.
- State symbols (s, l, g, aq) make equations complete.
- Corrosion and rancidity are slow oxidation processes with prevention methods.
```
