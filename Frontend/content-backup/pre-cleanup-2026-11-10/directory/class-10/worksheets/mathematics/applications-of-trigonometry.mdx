> [!TIP] Draw a labelled diagram for every question before writing equations — most errors come from mislabelled triangles, not computation.

## Worksheet 1

Q: From a point on level ground situated 30 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of its top is 30°. Calculate the height of the tower.

A: 10√3 m (about 17.32 m).

Q: A kite is flying 60 m above the ground. Its string is stretched from the kite to a point on the ground, making an angle of 60° with the horizontal. Assuming the string is taut, determine its length.

A: 40√3 m (about 69.28 m).

Q: A kite is attached to a 100 m long string that makes an angle of 60° with the horizontal ground. Calculate the height of the kite above the ground, assuming the string remains fully stretched.

A: 50√3 m (about 86.60 m).

Q: A circus performer climbs a rope stretched from the top of a vertical pole to a point on the ground. The pole is 12 m high, and the rope makes an angle of 30° with the ground. Find the length of the rope the performer climbs.

A: 24 m.

Q: A rope of length 20 m is stretched tightly from the top of a vertical pole to a point on the ground. If the rope forms an angle of 30° with the ground, calculate the height of the pole.

A: 10 m.

Q: A bridge spanning a river makes an angle of 45° with the riverbank. If the bridge measures 150 m along its length, determine the width of the river.

A: 75√2 m (about 106.07 m).

Q: An observer whose eyes are 1.5 m above the ground stands 28.5 m away from a tower. The angle of elevation of the tower's top from the observer's eyes is 45°. Find the total height of the tower.

A: 30 m.

Q: An electrician needs to repair a fault on a pole that is 4 m high. He must reach a point 1.3 m below the top of the pole. If the ladder makes an angle of 60° with the horizontal ground, calculate the length of the ladder required.

A: 1.8√3 m (about 3.12 m).

Q: A point on the ground is 40 m away from the foot of a tower. The angle of elevation of the tower's top is 30°, while the angle of elevation of the top of a water tank situated on the tower is 45°. Calculate (i) the height of the tower and (ii) the height of the water tank.

A: (i) 40√3/3 m (about 23.09 m) and (ii) 40(3 − √3)/3 m (about 16.91 m).

Q: A tree breaks due to strong winds, and its top touches the ground at a point 30 m from its base. The broken portion makes an angle of 30° with the ground. Determine the original height of the tree.

A: 30√3 m (about 51.96 m).

Q: The shadow of a vertical tower becomes 10 m longer when the Sun's angle of elevation changes from 45° to 30°. Find the height of the tower, correct to one decimal place. Take √3 = 1.73.

A: 13.7 m.

Q: From the top of a hill, the angles of depression of two consecutive kilometre stones on a straight road are 30° and 45°. Calculate the height of the hill.

A: 500(√3 + 1) m (about 1366 m).

Q: A mountain is observed from two points lying on the same straight line with its base. The angle of elevation of its summit from the nearer point is 30°. The second point is 10 km farther away, and the angle of elevation there is 15°. Calculate the height of the mountain. Take tan 15° = 0.27.

A: About 5.07 km.

Q: A person standing on the bank of a river observes the top of a tree on the opposite bank at an angle of elevation of 60°. After moving 40 m away from the river, the angle of elevation becomes 30°. Find the height of the tree and the width of the river.

A: Tree height = 20√3 m (about 34.64 m) and river width = 20 m.

Q: An aeroplane flying at an altitude of 1200 m observes two ships travelling in the same direction. The angles of depression of the ships from the aeroplane are 60° and 30°, respectively. Calculate the distance between the two ships.

A: 800√3 m (about 1385.6 m).

Q: An aeroplane flying 200 m above the ground observes two points on opposite banks of a river. The angles of depression of these points are 45° and 60°. Determine the width of the river.

A: (200 + 200/√3) m (about 315.5 m).

Q: Two vertical pillars of equal height stand on opposite sides of a road that is 100 m wide. From a point on the road between the pillars, the angles of elevation of their tops are 60° and 30°. Determine the position of the observation point and the height of each pillar.

A: 25 m from the 60° pillar (75 m from the other); height = 25√3 m (about 43.30 m).

Q: From the top of a lighthouse situated 100 m above sea level, the angle of depression of a ship sailing directly towards it changes from 30° to 45°. Calculate the distance travelled by the ship between the two observations.

A: 100(√3 − 1) m (about 73.20 m).

Q: A tower has a height of 100√3 m. Calculate the angle of elevation of its top from a point on the ground 100 m from its base.

A: 60°.

Q: From a window 15 m above the ground, the angles of elevation and depression of the top and foot of a house on the opposite side of a street are 30° and 45°, respectively. Determine the height of the opposite house. Take √3 = 1.732.

A: (15 + 5√3) m (about 23.66 m).

Q: From the top of a building 60 m high, the angles of depression of the top and the foot of a tower are 30° and 60°, respectively. Calculate the height of the tower.

A: 40 m.

Q: A small island lies in the middle of a river 100 m wide. A tall tree stands on the island. Points P and Q are directly opposite each other on the two banks. The angles of elevation of the top of the tree from P and Q are 30° and 45°, respectively. Find the height of the tree.

A: 50(√3 − 1) m (about 36.60 m).

Q: A girl standing 1.2 m above the ground observes a balloon moving horizontally at a height of 88.2 m above the ground. Initially, the angle of elevation of the balloon from her eyes is 60°. After some time, this angle decreases to 30°. Calculate the horizontal distance travelled by the balloon during this interval.

A: 58√3 m (about 100.46 m).

## Worksheet 2

Q: A tree 12 m tall is damaged by the wind, causing its top to touch the ground and form an angle of 60° with the horizontal. Find the height above the ground at which the tree breaks.

A: (24√3 − 36) m (about 5.57 m).

Q: From a point on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a vertical tower has a tangent value of 5/12. After moving 192 m towards the tower, the tangent of the angle of elevation becomes 3/4. Determine the height of the tower.

A: 180 m.

Q: The shadow of a flagpole is three times the shadow of another flagpole standing nearby. If the Sun's rays make an angle of 60° with the ground when the longer shadow is formed, determine the angle when the shorter shadow is formed, assuming both flagpoles have equal heights.

A: arctan(3√3), about 79.1°.

Q: From a point X on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top Q of a vertical tower PQ is 60°. Another point Y lies vertically 40 m above X, and the angle of elevation of Q from Y is 45°. Find the height of the tower and the horizontal distance XO.

A: Tower height = (60 + 20√3) m (about 94.64 m); XO = 20(√3 + 1) m (about 54.64 m).

Q: An aeroplane is observed from a point A on the ground at an angle of elevation of 60°. After 30 seconds, the angle of elevation becomes 30°. If the aeroplane flies horizontally at a constant altitude of 3600√3 m, calculate its speed.

A: 240 m/s, that is, 864 km/h.

Q: A straight road leads to the base of a tower. A man standing at the top of the tower observes a car approaching along the road at an angle of depression of 30°. Six seconds later, the angle of depression becomes 60°. Calculate the additional time the car will take to reach the base of the tower, assuming its speed remains constant.

A: 3 seconds.

Q: Two towers stand 140 m apart on level ground. From the top of the second tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the first tower is 30°. If the second tower is 60 m high, determine the height of the first tower.

A: (60 + 140√3/3) m (about 140.83 m).

Q: A vertical tower stands on level ground. From a point 20 m away from its base, the angle of elevation of its top is 60°. Calculate the height of the tower.

A: 20√3 m (about 34.64 m).

Q: A ladder rests against a vertical wall, making an angle of 60° with the ground. If the foot of the ladder is 9.5 m from the wall, determine the length of the ladder.

A: 19 m.

Q: A ladder 15 m long reaches the top of a vertical wall. If the ladder makes an angle of 60° with the wall, calculate the height of the wall.

A: 7.5 m.

Q: A vertical tower stands on level ground, and a vertical flagstaff is fixed on its top. An observer standing 70 m from the tower finds that the angles of elevation of the top and bottom of the flagstaff are 60° and 45°, respectively. Calculate the heights of the flagstaff and the tower.

A: Tower = 70 m; flagstaff = 70(√3 − 1) m (about 51.96 m).

Q: Two points A and B lie on the same straight line on one side of a tower. The angles of depression of these points from the top of the tower are 60° and 45°, respectively. If the tower is 15 m high, find the distance between A and B.

A: (15 − 5√3) m (about 6.34 m).

Q: A vertical tower is surmounted by a flagstaff 8 m high. From a point on the horizontal ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and top of the flagstaff are 30° and 60°, respectively. Find the height of the tower.

A: 4 m.

Q: The angle of elevation of a tower from a point on the ground is 30°. After moving 150 m towards the tower, the angle of elevation becomes 60°. Calculate the height of the tower. Take √3 = 1.732.

A: 75√3 m (about 129.90 m).

Q: From a point on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 32°. After moving 100 m towards the tower, the angle becomes 63°. Determine the height of the tower and the original distance of the observation point from its base. Take tan 32° = 0.6248 and tan 63° = 1.9626.

A: Height ≈ 91.7 m; original distance ≈ 146.7 m.

Q: From a point A on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 30°. On moving 20 m towards the tower to point B, the angle of elevation becomes 60°. Find the height of the tower and the distance of point A from its base.

A: Height = 10√3 m (about 17.32 m); A is 30 m from the base.

Q: A building is 15 m high. From its top, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 30°, while the angle of depression of the tower's foot is 60°. Calculate the height of the tower and the horizontal distance between the building and the tower.

A: Tower = 20 m; distance = 5√3 m (about 8.66 m).

Q: A vertical tower stands on level ground and has a flagpole fixed at its top. From a point 9 m away from the tower's base, the angles of elevation of the top and bottom of the flagpole are 60° and 30°, respectively. Find the heights of the tower and the flagpole.

A: Tower = 3√3 m (about 5.20 m); flagpole = 6√3 m (about 10.39 m).

Q: An observer whose eye level is 1.5 m above the ground stands 28.5 m away from a tower that is 30 m tall. Determine the angle of elevation of the tower's top from the observer's eyes.

A: 45°.

Q: A boy 1.5 m tall stands some distance from a 30 m high building. The angle of elevation of the building's top from his eyes is initially 30°. As he walks towards the building, the angle increases to 60°. Find the distance he walks.

A: 19√3 m (about 32.91 m).

Q: The shadow of a vertical tower on level ground becomes 40 m longer when the Sun's angle of elevation changes from 60° to 30°. Calculate the height of the tower.

A: 20√3 m (about 34.64 m).

Q: From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and top of a transmission tower fixed on a 20 m high building are 45° and 60°, respectively. Find the height of the transmission tower.

A: 20(√3 − 1) m (about 14.64 m).

## Worksheet 3

Q: From the top of a multistorey building, the angles of depression of the top and bottom of an 8 m high building are 30° and 45°, respectively. Calculate the height of the taller building and the horizontal distance between the two buildings.

A: Both equal (12 + 4√3) m (about 18.93 m).

Q: A statue 1.6 m tall stands on a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the statue's top is 60°, while the angle of elevation of the pedestal's top is 45°. Determine the height of the pedestal.

A: 0.8(√3 + 1) m (about 2.19 m).

Q: From the top of a 120 m high tower, two cars are observed on opposite sides of the tower along the same straight road. Their angles of depression are 60° and 45°. Calculate the distance between the two cars. Take √3 = 1.732.

A: (120 + 40√3) m (about 189.28 m).

Q: From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60°, while the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Find the height of the cable tower.

A: 7(1 + √3) m (about 19.12 m).

Q: From the top of a 75 m high lighthouse, two ships on the same side are observed at angles of depression of 30° and 45°. If the farther ship is directly behind the nearer one, find the distance between them.

A: 75(√3 − 1) m (about 54.90 m).

Q: From the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top of a building is 30°. From the foot of the building, the angle of elevation of the tower's top is 60°. If the tower is 50 m high, calculate the height of the building.

A: 50/3 m (about 16.67 m).

Q: From a point on a bridge across a river, the angles of depression of the opposite riverbanks are 30° and 45°. If the bridge is 30 m above the riverbanks, determine the width of the river.

A: 30(√3 + 1) m (about 81.96 m).

Q: From the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top of a hill is 60°. From the foot of the hill, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 30°. If the tower is 50 m high, find the height of the hill.

A: 150 m.

Q: Two boats approach a lighthouse from opposite directions. The angles of elevation of the lighthouse's top from the boats are 30° and 45°, respectively. If the distance between the boats is 100 m, calculate the height of the lighthouse.

A: 50(√3 − 1) m (about 36.60 m).

Q: Two observers standing on opposite sides of a cliff 80 m high measure the angles of elevation of its top as 30° and 60°, respectively. Find the distance between the two observers.

A: 320√3/3 m (about 184.75 m).

Q: An aeroplane flying at an altitude of 210 m observes two points on opposite banks of a river. The angles of depression of the points are 45° and 60°. Calculate the width of the river. Take √3 = 1.73.

A: (210 + 70√3) m (about 331.1 m).

Q: A flagstaff stands on top of a tower 5 m high. From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the top of the flagstaff and the top of the tower are 60° and 45°, respectively. Find the height of the flagstaff.

A: 5(√3 − 1) m (about 3.66 m).

Q: Two vertical poles are 15 m apart. From the top of the second pole, the angle of depression of the top of the first pole is 30°. If the second pole is 24 m high, calculate the height of the first pole. Take √3 = 1.732.

A: (24 − 5√3) m (about 15.34 m).

Q: From the top of a lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships on the same side are 45° and 30°. If the ships are 200 m apart, determine the height of the lighthouse.

A: 100(√3 + 1) m (about 273.20 m).

Q: From a point on a bridge across a river, the angles of depression of points on the opposite banks are 30° and 45°. If the bridge is 8 m above the riverbanks, calculate the width of the river.

A: 8(√3 + 1) m (about 21.86 m).

Q: From the top of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top of a chimney is 60°, while the angle of depression of the chimney's foot is 30°. If the tower is 40 m high, calculate the height of the chimney. Also determine whether the chimney satisfies the stated minimum height requirement of 100 m for a smoke-emitting chimney.

A: 160 m. Yes, it satisfies the 100 m requirement.

Q: From the top of a 60 m high building AB, the angles of depression of the top and bottom of a vertical lamp post CD are 30° and 60°, respectively. Calculate: (i) the horizontal distance between the building and the lamp post, (ii) the height of the lamp post, and (iii) the difference between the heights of the building and the lamp post.

A: (i) 20√3 m (about 34.64 m), (ii) 40 m, and (iii) 20 m.

Q: A motorboat is observed from the top of a 150 m high cliff while moving away from it. The angle of depression of the boat changes from 60° to 45° in 2 minutes. Calculate the speed of the boat in metres per hour.

A: (4500 − 1500√3) m/h (about 1902 m/h).

Q: A person rowing a boat away from a lighthouse 100 m high observes that the angle of elevation of the lighthouse's top decreases from 60° to 30° in 2 minutes. Calculate the speed of the boat in metres per minute. Take √3 = 1.732.

A: 100√3/3 m/min (about 57.73 m/min).

Q: The lower window of a house is 2 m above the ground, and the upper window is 4 m vertically above the lower one. At a certain instant, the angles of elevation of a balloon from these windows are 60° and 30°, respectively. Calculate the height of the balloon above the ground.

A: 8 m.

Q: One observer measures the angle of elevation of a hot-air balloon's basket as 60°. Another observer, standing 100 m away from the first, measures the angle of elevation as 30°. Find: (i) the height of the basket above the ground, (ii) the distance from the first observer's eye to the basket, and (iii) the horizontal distance between the second observer and the basket.

A: (i) 50√3 m (about 86.60 m), (ii) 100 m, and (iii) 150 m.

Q: A tower is 24 m high. From its foot, the angle of elevation of the top of another tower standing on the same horizontal plane is 60°. From the foot of the second tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the first tower is 30°. Find the distance between the towers, the height of the second tower, and the length of the wire joining their tops.

A: Distance = 24√3 m (about 41.57 m); second tower = 72 m; wire = 24√7 m (about 63.50 m).

```keys

- Two-position problems: equal heights from two angles give hills, rivers, buildings and balloons.
- Moving targets: equal speeds over angle changes give aeroplanes, boats, cars and walking distances.
```
