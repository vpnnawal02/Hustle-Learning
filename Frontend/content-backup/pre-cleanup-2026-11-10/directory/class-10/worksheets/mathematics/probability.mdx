Two timed practice worksheets for Probability. Attempt one worksheet at a time on paper, then tap each question to check its answer.

> [!TIP] For every question, first write the total number of equally likely outcomes — most errors come from miscounting the sample space, not the formula.

## Worksheet 1

### Section A – Multiple Choice Questions

Q: A fair die is rolled once. The probability of obtaining a prime number is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/6
(b) 1/3
(c) 1/2
(d) 2/3

A: (c) 1/2, since {2, 3, 5} are 3 of 6 outcomes.

Q: Two fair coins are tossed simultaneously. The probability of obtaining exactly one head is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/4
(b) 1/2
(c) 3/4
(d) 1

A: (b) 1/2, since {HT, TH} are 2 of 4 outcomes.

Q: A card is drawn randomly from a well-shuffled deck of 52 playing cards. The probability of drawing a red card is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/4
(b) 1/2
(c) 1/13
(d) 1/26

A: (b) 1/2, since 26 of 52 cards are red.

Q: A box contains 20 electric bulbs, of which 4 are defective. The probability of selecting a non-defective bulb is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/5
(b) 1/4
(c) 4/5
(d) 3/5

A: (c) 4/5, since 16 of 20 bulbs are non-defective.

Q: A spinner has 12 equally likely numbered sectors from 1 to 12. The probability of obtaining 8 is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/12
(b) 1/6
(c) 1/4
(d) 1/3

A: (a) 1/12.

Q: A number is selected randomly from integers 1 to 25. The probability that the selected number is not prime is: (1 mark)

(a) 9/25
(b) 16/25
(c) 1/5
(d) 3/5

A: (b) 16/25, since 9 of the 25 numbers are prime.

Q: The probability of an event that is certain to occur is: (1 mark)

(a) 0
(b) 1/2
(c) 1
(d) −1

A: (c) 1.

Q: A bag contains 5 red, 8 white and 7 black balls. The probability of selecting a ball that is neither white nor black is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/4
(b) 1/5
(c) 2/5
(d) 7/20

A: (a) 1/4, since only the 5 red balls qualify out of 20.

Q: Two fair dice are rolled simultaneously. The total number of possible outcomes is: (1 mark)

(a) 6
(b) 12
(c) 24
(d) 36

A: (d) 36.

Q: The probability that a leap year selected at random contains 53 Sundays is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/7
(b) 2/7
(c) 3/7
(d) 1/2

A: (b) 2/7, since a leap year has 2 extra days beyond 52 weeks.

### Section B – Very Short Answer Questions

Q: Two fair coins are tossed simultaneously. Find the probability of obtaining: (i) two heads and (ii) at least one head. (2 marks)

A: (i) 1/4 and (ii) 3/4.

Q: A number is selected randomly from integers 1 to 20. Find the probability that the selected number is: (i) a multiple of 3 or 7 and (ii) a prime number. (2 marks)

A: (i) 2/5 and (ii) 2/5.

Q: A bag contains 3 red balls, 5 black balls and 4 white balls. One ball is drawn randomly. Find the probability that it is: (i) white and (ii) not red. (2 marks)

A: (i) 1/3 and (ii) 3/4.

Q: A lottery has 1,000 tickets, out of which 5 carry prizes. If a person purchases one ticket at random, find the probability of winning a prize. (2 marks)

A: 1/200.

Q: A fair die is rolled once. Find the probability of obtaining: (i) an even number or a multiple of 3 and (ii) an even number and a multiple of 3. (2 marks)

A: (i) 2/3 and (ii) 1/6.

### Section C – Short Answer Questions

Q: A bag contains 4 red balls, 5 black balls and 6 white balls. One ball is drawn randomly. Find the probability that the selected ball is: (i) white, (ii) not black and (iii) red or white. (3 marks)

A: (i) 2/5, (ii) 2/3 and (iii) 2/3.

Q: A carton contains 100 shirts. Of these, 88 are free from defects, 8 have minor defects and 4 have major defects. One shirt is selected randomly. Find the probability that it is accepted by: (i) a retailer who accepts only defect-free shirts and (ii) a retailer who rejects only shirts with major defects. (3 marks)

A: (i) 22/25 and (ii) 24/25.

Q: A box contains 90 discs numbered from 1 to 90. One disc is selected randomly. Find the probability that the number on the selected disc is: (i) a two-digit number, (ii) a perfect square and (iii) divisible by 5. (3 marks)

A: (i) 9/10, (ii) 1/10 and (iii) 1/5.

Q: A number is selected randomly from integers 1 to 35. Find the probability that the selected number is: (i) prime, (ii) a multiple of 7 and (iii) a multiple of 3 or 5. (3 marks)

A: (i) 11/35, (ii) 1/7 and (iii) 16/35.

### Section D – Long Answer Questions

Q: Two fair dice are rolled simultaneously. Find the probability of obtaining: (i) a sum of 7, (ii) a sum that is a prime number, (iii) a sum of at least 10 and (iv) both dice showing the same number. (4 marks)

A: (i) 1/6, (ii) 5/12, (iii) 1/6 and (iv) 1/6.

Q: A standard deck of 52 playing cards is shuffled, and one card is drawn randomly. Find the probability that the card drawn is: (i) a king of a red suit, (ii) a face card, (iii) a queen of a black suit and (iv) the jack of hearts. (4 marks)

A: (i) 1/26, (ii) 3/13, (iii) 1/26 and (iv) 1/52.

### Section E – Higher Order Thinking Questions

Q: Two dice, one blue and one green, are rolled simultaneously. (i) Write all possible outcomes. (ii) Find the probability that the sum of the numbers obtained is 8. (iii) Find the probability that both dice show the same number. (iv) Find the probability that the product of the numbers is less than 9. (5 marks)

A: (i) 36 ordered pairs from (1, 1) to (6, 6); (ii) 5/36; (iii) 1/6; and (iv) 4/9.

Q: Examine the following statements and comment on their correctness with proper justification: (i) When two coins are tossed simultaneously, there are three possible outcomes, so each has probability 1/3. (ii) When a die is rolled once, odd and even are the two outcomes, so each has probability 1/2. (5 marks)

A: (i) Incorrect: HT and TH are distinct equally likely outcomes, so the probabilities are 1/4, 1/4 and 1/2. (ii) Correct: {1, 3, 5} and {2, 4, 6} are symmetric, so each has probability 1/2.

## Worksheet 2

### Section A – Multiple Choice Questions

Q: Three unbiased coins are tossed simultaneously. The probability of obtaining three heads is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/2
(b) 1/4
(c) 1/8
(d) 3/8

A: (c) 1/8.

Q: Seventeen cards numbered from 1 to 17 are placed in a box. The probability of drawing an odd-numbered card is: (1 mark)

(a) 8/17
(b) 9/17
(c) 1/2
(d) 7/17

A: (b) 9/17.

Q: A cube has the letters A, B, C, D, E and A written on its six faces. The probability of obtaining the letter A is: (1 mark)

(a) 1/6
(b) 1/2
(c) 1/3
(d) 2/3

A: (c) 1/3, since A appears on 2 of 6 faces.

Q: One letter is selected randomly from the word ASSASSINATION. The probability of selecting a vowel is: (1 mark)

(a) 5/13
(b) 6/13
(c) 7/13
(d) 4/13

A: (b) 6/13, since 6 of the 13 letters are vowels.

Q: A tank contains 5 male fish and 8 female fish. The probability of randomly selecting a male fish is: (1 mark)

(a) 5/8
(b) 8/13
(c) 5/13
(d) 1/13

A: (c) 5/13.

Q: A piggy bank contains 150 fifty-paisa coins, 50 one-rupee coins, 20 two-rupee coins and 10 five-rupee coins. The probability of selecting a fifty-paisa coin is: (1 mark)

(a) 15/23
(b) 5/23
(c) 3/23
(d) 10/23

A: (a) 15/23, since 150 of 230 coins are fifty-paisa coins.

Q: A jar contains 24 marbles. The probability of selecting a green marble is 2/3. The number of blue marbles in the jar is: (1 mark)

(a) 6
(b) 8
(c) 12
(d) 16

A: (b) 8, since 16 marbles are green and the rest are blue.

Q: A class consists of 25 girls and 15 boys. The probability of randomly selecting a boy is: (1 mark)

(a) 5/8
(b) 3/5
(c) 3/8
(d) 1/4

A: (c) 3/8.

Q: Peter rolls two different dice and calculates their product. Rina rolls one die and squares the number obtained. Who has a greater probability of obtaining 25? (1 mark)

(a) Peter
(b) Rina
(c) Both have equal probabilities
(d) Neither can obtain 25

A: (b) Rina, since Peter needs (5, 5) with probability 1/36 while Rina needs a 5 with probability 1/6.

Q: A box contains cards numbered 3, 5, 7, 9, ..., 35, 37. The probability of selecting a prime number is: (1 mark)

(a) 7/18
(b) 11/18
(c) 1/2
(d) 13/18

A: (b) 11/18, since 11 of the 18 cards are prime.

### Section B – Very Short Answer Questions

Q: Savita and Hamida are friends. Assuming a year has 365 days, find the probability that: (i) they have the same birthday and (ii) they have different birthdays. (2 marks)

A: (i) 1/365 and (ii) 364/365.

Q: A jar contains 54 marbles, each of which is blue, green or white. The probability of selecting a blue marble is 1/3 and that of selecting a green marble is 4/9. Find the number of white marbles. (2 marks)

A: 12.

Q: A fair die is rolled once. Find the probability of obtaining: (i) a number greater than 5 and (ii) a composite number. (2 marks)

A: (i) 1/6 and (ii) 1/3.

Q: A bag contains 5 red balls, 8 white balls and 7 black balls. One ball is drawn randomly. Find the probability that the selected ball is: (i) red or white and (ii) neither white nor black. (2 marks)

A: (i) 13/20 and (ii) 1/4.

Q: A box contains 20 cards numbered from 1 to 20. One card is drawn randomly. Find the probability that the selected number is: (i) a prime number and (ii) divisible by 2 or 3. (2 marks)

A: (i) 2/5 and (ii) 13/20.

### Section C – Short Answer Questions

Q: A group contains 12 people. Three are extremely patient, six are extremely honest and the remaining three are extremely kind. One person is selected randomly. Find the probability that the selected person is: (i) extremely patient, (ii) extremely honest and (iii) extremely kind. (3 marks)

A: (i) 1/4, (ii) 1/2 and (iii) 1/4.

Q: A bag contains 44 identical cards with circles or squares. There are 24 circles (9 blue, rest green) and 20 squares (11 blue, rest green). One card is drawn randomly. Find the probability that it bears: (i) a square, (ii) a green figure and (iii) a blue circle. (3 marks)

A: (i) 5/11, (ii) 6/11 and (iii) 9/44.

Q: A stationery shop has 144 pens, of which 20 are defective and the rest are in good condition. Nuri selects one pen randomly and receives it only if it is in good condition. Find the probability that: (i) she receives the pen and (ii) she does not receive the pen. (3 marks)

A: (i) 31/36 and (ii) 5/36.

Q: Cards numbered from 1 to 30 are placed in a bag. One card is drawn randomly. Find the probability that the number on the selected card is: (i) not divisible by 3, (ii) a prime number greater than 7 and (iii) not a perfect square. (3 marks)

A: (i) 2/3, (ii) 1/5 and (iii) 5/6.

### Section D – Long Answer Questions

Q: Five cards — the ten, jack, queen, king and ace of diamonds — are shuffled face down. One card is selected randomly. (i) Find the probability that the selected card is a queen. (ii) If the king is drawn first and set aside, find the probability that the next card drawn is an ace. (iii) Under the same condition, find the probability that the next card drawn is a king. (4 marks)

A: (i) 1/5, (ii) 1/4 and (iii) 0.

Q: All four aces are removed from a standard deck of 52 playing cards. The remaining cards are shuffled, and one card is selected randomly. Find the probability that the selected card is: (i) a red card, (ii) a face card, (iii) a club and (iv) a king. (4 marks)

A: (i) 1/2, (ii) 1/4, (iii) 1/4 and (iv) 1/12.

### Section E – Higher Order Thinking Questions

Q: A box contains 20 bulbs, of which 4 are defective. One bulb is selected randomly and found to be non-defective. It is not replaced. A second bulb is then selected randomly. (i) Find the probability that the first bulb selected is non-defective. (ii) Find the probability that the second bulb selected is also non-defective. (5 marks)

A: (i) 4/5 and (ii) 15/19.

Q: All kings and queens are removed from a standard deck of 52 playing cards. The remaining cards are shuffled, and one card is drawn randomly. Find the probability that the selected card is: (i) a black card, (ii) a red face card, (iii) either a red card or a queen and (iv) a red card and a queen. (5 marks)

A: (i) 1/2, (ii) 1/22, (iii) 1/2 and (iv) 0.

```keys
- Classical probability: favourable outcomes over equally likely outcomes, from coins and dice to cards and ballots.
- Complements: P(not E) = 1 − P(E) handles neither-nor, non-defective and different-birthday questions.
- Conditional draws: without-replacement recompute the denominator, as in bulbs, pens and second draws.
```
