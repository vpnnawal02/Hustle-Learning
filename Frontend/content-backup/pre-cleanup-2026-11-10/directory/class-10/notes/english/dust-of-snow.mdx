A stanza-by-stanza explanation of **Dust of Snow** by Robert Frost. Read each stanza slowly, then read its explanation.

> [!TIP] Learn the poem's central idea and two devices first — most exam questions come from those.

## Stanza 1

```stanza
The way a crow
Shook down on me
The dust of snow
From a hemlock tree
```

A crow sitting on a hemlock tree shakes its branches, and fine particles of snow fall on the poet like dust. The **crow** is often seen as an unpleasant bird and the **hemlock** is a poisonous tree, so both traditionally suggest negativity — yet this ordinary incident changes the poet's mood.

**In simple words:** A crow shakes snow off a hemlock tree, and the falling snow lands on the poet.

## Stanza 2

```stanza
Has given my heart
A change of mood
And saved some part
Of a day I had rued
```

The falling snow lifts the poet's sadness and saves part of an otherwise wasted day. **Rued** means regretted — he had been unhappy with how his day was going, but this small, unexpected touch refreshes him.

**In simple words:** The falling snow changes the poet's mood and saves a part of his unhappy day.

## Poetic devices

| Device | Example | What it shows |
| ------ | ------- | ------------- |
| Symbolism | Crow and hemlock tree | Traditionally represent negativity and sadness |
| Imagery | The dust of snow | Fine snow particles falling, seen clearly in the mind |
| Alliteration | Has given my heart | The repeated h sound creates music |
| Enjambment | The way a crow / Shook down on me | The sentence flows past the line break |
| Rhyme scheme | ABAB CDCD | A regular, song-like pattern |

```keys
- A crow shakes snow from a hemlock onto the poet, lifting his gloom and saving part of a rued day.
- Negative symbols (crow, hemlock) bring a positive change — small joys can rescue a bad day.
- Devices: symbolism, imagery, alliteration, enjambment and a steady ABAB CDCD rhyme.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the central idea of Dust of Snow?

A: Small, ordinary incidents can lift sadness — even symbols of negativity like the crow and hemlock bring the poet unexpected cheer.
