A stanza-by-stanza explanation of **For Anne Gregory** by W. B. Yeats. Read each stanza slowly, then read its explanation.

> [!TIP] Follow the repeated refrain — "love you for yourself alone, and not your yellow hair" — it carries the whole argument.

## Stanza 1 — Loved for hair, not self

```stanza
Never shall a young man
Thrown into despair
By those great honey-coloured
Ramparts at your ear
Love you for yourself alone
And not your yellow hair
```

No young man, the poet says, will love Anne for herself — only for her **honey-coloured** hair, thick as **ramparts** (fortress walls) around her ears. Beauty overwhelms; character goes unseen.

**Central idea:** Physical beauty blocks love for inner qualities. **Devices:** metaphor (ramparts), imagery (honey-coloured), symbolism (yellow hair = outward appearance).

## Stanza 2 — Anne answers: dye it

```stanza
But I can get a hair-dye
And set such colour there
Brown, or black, or carrot
That young men in despair
May love me for myself alone
And not my yellow hair
```

Anne replies: she will dye her hair **brown, black or carrot** (orange), so men must love *her*. Changing looks, she hopes, will reveal who loves the person. The refrain returns — her deepest wish, repeated.

**Central idea:** Beauty can be altered, so perhaps true love can be tested. **Devices:** imagery (the colours), repetition (the refrain).

## Stanza 3 — Only God can

```stanza
I heard an old religious man
But yesternight declare
That he had found a text to prove
That only God, my dear
Could love you for yourself alone
And not your yellow hair
```

An old religious man claims scripture proves **only God** loves without regard to looks. Humans, the stanza implies, cannot fully escape attraction to appearance — dye or no dye. Divine love is unconditional; human love, conditional on beauty.

**Central idea:** Only God's love is free of outward influence. **Devices:** repetition, contrast (human vs divine love), irony (the dye solution may change nothing).

## Overall message

Beauty fades; character endures — yet humans keep loving the surface. The poem asks whether anyone except God can love the self beneath the hair.

```keys
- Three voices: the poet's claim, Anne's dye solution, the holy man's divine answer.
- Refrain repeats every stanza: loved for self alone, not yellow hair.
- Message: inner worth outlasts looks, but humans struggle to see past them.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the central idea of For Anne Gregory?

A: People love outward beauty, not inner selves — only God loves unconditionally, beyond appearance.
