A stanza-by-stanza explanation of **Amanda!** by Robin Klein. The poem alternates an adult's instructions with Amanda's fantasies — watch the two voices.

> [!TIP] Read upright stanzas as the adult's voice and bracketed stanzas as Amanda's dreams. The whole conflict sits in that contrast.

## Stanza 1 — Constant instructions

```stanza
Don't bite your nails, Amanda!
Don't hunch your shoulders, Amanda!
Stop that slouching and sit up straight
Amanda!
```

Nail-biting, **hunched** shoulders, **slouching** posture — every line corrects Amanda. The repeated name and exclamation marks sound impatient: ordinary advice delivered as constant control.

## Stanza 2 — Mermaid fantasy

```stanza
(There is a languid, emerald sea
where the sole inhabitant is me —
a mermaid, drifting blissfully.)
```

Amanda escapes into a **languid** (slow, relaxed), **emerald** (green-gem) sea where she is the **sole inhabitant**. As a **mermaid drifting blissfully**, nobody commands her — freedom, solitude and peace.

## Stanza 3 — More monitoring

```stanza
Did you finish your homework, Amanda?
Did you tidy your room, Amanda?
I thought I told you to clean your shoes
Amanda!
```

Homework, **tidy** room, cleaned shoes — reasonable duties, but asked as relentless checking. **"I thought I told you"** shows irritation: Amanda is supervised, never trusted to manage alone.

## Stanza 4 — Orphan fantasy

```stanza
(I am an orphan, roaming the street
I pattern soft dust with my hushed, bare feet
The silence is golden, the freedom is sweet.)
```

Amanda imagines orphanhood — not from hating her parents, but for **roaming** freely. **Patterning dust** with **hushed, bare feet** in **golden silence**: peace is precious because her real life is all noise and orders.

## Stanza 5 — Chocolate and attention

```stanza
Don't eat that chocolate, Amanda!
Remember your acne, Amanda!
Will you please look at me when I'm speaking to you
Amanda!
```

Chocolate, **acne**, eye contact — even her skin and attention are policed. **"Will you please"** sounds polite but carries frustration. Corrected everywhere, Amanda drifts further into dreams.

## Stanza 6 — Rapunzel fantasy

```stanza
(I am Rapunzel, I have not a care
Life in a tower is tranquil and rare
I'll certainly never let down my bright hair!)
```

As **Rapunzel**, Amanda wants the tower's **tranquil** (perfectly calm) peace — but unlike the fairy tale, she will **never let down her hair**: nobody may climb up and disturb her. Privacy as protection.

> [!NOTE] The NCERT text of this stanza matches the version above, including the exclamation in the final line.

## Stanza 7 — Sulking and nagging

```stanza
Stop that sulking at once, Amanda!
You're always so moody, Amanda!
Anyone would think I nagged at you
Amanda!
```

Ordered to stop **sulking**, called **moody** — then the adult claims nobody would think she **nagged**. The irony is the poem's punchline: a full poem of nagging denies it is nagging. Correction without understanding only widens the gap.

**Overall meaning:** Constant instruction, however well-meant, feels like control to a child — and the child answers with imagination, the one place nobody can supervise.

```keys
- Two voices alternate: the adult's commands vs Amanda's mermaid, orphan and Rapunzel dreams.
- Irony peaks in stanza 7: the nagging voice denies nagging.
- Message: correct behaviour, but first understand feelings — or children retreat into fantasy.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the central idea of Amanda!?

A: Over-instruction stifles children; Amanda's fantasies show her hunger for freedom, privacy and understanding.
