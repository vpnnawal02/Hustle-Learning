A line-by-line explanation of **Fog** by Carl Sandburg. Read the stanza slowly, then read each line's explanation.

> [!TIP] The whole poem is one extended comparison — fog behaves exactly like a cat. Track each cat-like action.

## The stanza

```stanza
The fog comes
on little cat feet
It sits looking
over harbour and city
on silent haunches
and then moves on
```

**Line 1 — "The fog comes on little cat feet."** The fog arrives as silently and gradually as a cat walking softly. The poet opens with a **metaphor**: fog is a cat.

**Line 2 — "It sits looking over harbour and city."** Like a cat sitting quietly to watch, the fog settles over the harbour and the city, turning them misty and unclear. Describing fog as something that can *sit* and *look* is **personification**.

**Line 3 — "on silent haunches."** **Haunches** are an animal's hind legs and hips. The fog rests the way a cat sits motionless on its haunches — still, silent, watchful. This blends **metaphor and imagery**.

**Line 4 — "and then moves on."** Just as a cat slips away after sitting a while, the fog lifts and drifts off. It never stays forever. **Metaphor** again — arrival, rest and departure, all cat-like.

**Overall meaning:** Fog comes silently, sits over the harbour and city, and leaves quietly — nature's quiet visitor, captured through the image of a cat.

```keys
- One metaphor runs the whole poem: fog arrives, sits and leaves like a cat.
- Personification (sitting, looking) and imagery (silent haunches, harbour and city) carry it.
- Mood: stillness, patience and quiet departure — nothing dramatic, everything observed.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the central idea of Fog?

A: Fog mirrors a cat's silent arrival, watchful rest and quiet departure over the harbour and city.
