A stanza-by-stanza explanation of **The Tale of Custard the Dragon** by Ogden Nash. A ballad about a cowardly dragon who turns hero.

> [!TIP] Track who is called brave and who acts brave — the poem's whole joke lives in that gap.

## Stanza 1 — The household

```stanza
Belinda lived in a little white house
With a little black kitten and a little grey mouse
And a little yellow dog and a little red wagon
And a realio, trulio, little pet dragon
```

Belinda lives with Ink (kitten), Blink (mouse), Mustard (dog) — and a **realio, trulio** (playfully mispronounced "really, truly") little pet dragon. Rhyming diminutives set a cheerful, nursery-tale mood.

## Stanza 2 — Custard the coward

```stanza
Now the name of the little black kitten was Ink
And the little grey mouse, she called him Blink
And the little yellow dog was sharp as Mustard
But the dragon was a coward, and she called him Custard
```

Ink, Blink, **sharp as Mustard** (alert, biting) — but the dragon is a **coward**, named **Custard** (soft, wobbly dessert). A fearsome creature with a pudding name: the central joke is born.

## Stanza 3 — Terrifying looks

```stanza
Custard the dragon had big sharp teeth
And spikes on top of him and scales underneath
Mouth like a fireplace, chimney for a nose
And realio, trulio daggers on his toes
```

Teeth, spikes, scales, fireplace mouth, chimney nose, **dagger** toes — Custard looks like pure menace. Looks, the poem insists, mean nothing: he still begs for a safe cage.

## Stanza 4 — Everyone brave but Custard

```stanza
Belinda was as brave as a barrel full of bears
And Ink and Blink chased lions down the stairs
Mustard was as brave as a tiger in a rage
But Custard cried for a nice safe cage
```

Everyone claims cartoon courage — Belinda out-braves bears, the kitten and mouse chase lions, Mustard out-rages tigers — while Custard **cried for a nice safe cage**. Boasts pile up, begging for the pirate to test them.

## Stanza 5 — Teasing Percival

```stanza
Belinda tickled him, she tickled him unmerciful
Ink, Blink and Mustard, they rudely called him Percival
They all sat laughing in the little red wagon
At the realio, trulio, cowardly dragon
```

Tickled **unmerciful** (without mercy), mocked as **Percival** (a grand knight's name for a coward), laughed at from the little red wagon. The household mistakes fearfulness for worthlessness.

## Stanza 6 — Giggles and Weeck

```stanza
Belinda giggled till she shook the house
And Blink said Weeck! which is giggling for a mouse
Ink and Mustard rudely asked his age
When Custard cried for a nice safe cage
```

Laughter shakes the house; Blink's giggle is **Weeck!**; Ink and Mustard mockingly ask Custard's age. Comedy with an edge — the reader starts suspecting the brave ones are all talk.

## Stanza 7 — The pirate arrives

```stanza
Suddenly, suddenly they heard a nasty sound
And Mustard growled, and they all looked around
Meowch! cried Ink, and ooh! cried Belinda
For there was a pirate, climbing in the winda
```

**Suddenly, suddenly** — danger crashes the comedy. Growls, **Meowch!**, **ooh!**, and a pirate climbs in the **winda** (baby-talk for window, keeping the nursery rhyme lilt). Claims of courage are about to be audited.

## Stanza 8 — The pirate described

```stanza
Pistol in his left hand, pistol in his right
And he held in his teeth a cutlass bright
His beard was black, one leg was wood
It was clear that the pirate meant no good
```

Twin pistols, a **cutlass** (short sword) in the teeth, black beard, wooden leg — a storybook villain **meaning no good**. The threat is real this time, not a boast.

## Stanza 9 — The brave ones flee

```stanza
Belinda paled, and she cried Help! Help!
But Mustard fled with a terrified yelp
Ink trickled down to the bottom of the household
And little mouse Blink strategically mouseholed
```

Pale Belinda screams, tiger-brave Mustard **flees with a terrified yelp**, Ink **trickles** downstairs, Blink **strategically mouseholed** (strategy = hiding). Every boast collapses in one stanza.

**Central idea:** Claimed bravery evaporates at the first real danger.

## Stanza 10 — Custard attacks

```stanza
But up jumped Custard, snorting like an engine
Clashed his tail like irons in a dungeon
With a clatter and a clank and a jangling squirm
He went at the pirate like a robin at a worm
```

**But** — the turn. Snorting **like an engine**, tail clashing **like irons in a dungeon**, Custard charges **like a robin at a worm** (tiny bird, total confidence). The coward is the only one who fights.

## Stanza 11 — Gobbles him, every bit

```stanza
The pirate gaped at Belinda's dragon
And gulped some grog from his pocket flagon
He fired two bullets, but they didn't hit
And Custard gobbled him, every bit
```

The pirate **gapes**, fortifies himself with **grog** (sailors' rum) from his **flagon** (flask), fires twice, misses twice — and is **gobbled, every bit**. Mocked coward, complete victor.

## Stanza 12 — Celebration dance

```stanza
Belinda embraced him, Mustard licked him
No one mourned for his pirate victim
Ink and Blink in glee did gyrate
Around the dragon that ate the pirate
```

Embraces, licks, and a **gyrating** (spinning) dance around the dragon — **no one mourned** the pirate, who came to harm. Custard is a hero at last, and even the rhyme (gyrate/pirate) joins the party.

## Stanza 13 — Excuses and humility

```stanza
But presently up spoke little dog Mustard
I'd have been twice as brave if I hadn't been flustered
And up spoke Ink and up spoke Blink
We'd have been three times as brave, we think
And Custard said, I quite agree
That everybody is braver than me
```

Danger past, the excuses start: Mustard would have been **twice as brave** if not **flustered**; Ink and Blink, **three times**. And Custard — the actual dragon-slayer — **quite agrees** everyone is braver. Boasters boast; heroes stay humble.

## Stanza 14 — Back home

```stanza
Belinda still lives in her little white house
With her little black kitten and her little grey mouse
And her little yellow dog and her little red wagon
And her realio, trulio little pet dragon
```

The opening stanza returns almost word for word. Nothing outwardly changes — same house, same pets — which makes the irony land harder.

## Stanza 15 — Nothing learned

```stanza
Belinda is as brave as a barrel full of bears
And Ink and Blink chase lions down the stairs
Mustard is as brave as a tiger in a rage
But Custard keeps crying for a nice safe cage
```

Same boasts, same cage-crying — as if the pirate never happened. True courage showed itself once, changed no one's self-image, and the comedy loops forever: **actions prove, words pretend**.

**Overall meaning:** Courage is what you do when danger comes, not what you claim before it — and the humblest creature in the room may be the bravest.

```keys
- Boast vs act: bears, lions, tigers in words; fleeing, trickling, mouseholing in danger.
- Custard's arc: mocked coward → engine-snorting fighter → humble hero → still asking for a cage.
- Devices: ballad rhythm, comic similes, ironic repetition of stanzas 1 and 4 at the close.
```

## Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the central idea of The Tale of Custard the Dragon?

A: Real courage shows in action, not boasting — the mocked dragon fights while the braggarts hide, yet stays humble.
